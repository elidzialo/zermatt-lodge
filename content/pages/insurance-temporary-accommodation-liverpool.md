If a flood, fire or major leak has made your Liverpool home unliveable, your home insurance may pay for temporary "alternative accommodation" while repairs are done. Usually your insurer, or a provider it uses, arranges the property. You can often ask for something that fits your family, such as enough bedrooms, a kitchen, parking and a location near school and work. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Fazakerley, north Liverpool, sleeping 7, which insurers and families can book for these stays.

This guide explains how alternative accommodation typically works, what to ask, and how to make a disrupted few weeks or months more manageable.

*This is general information, not insurance advice. Always check your own policy wording and speak to your insurer.*

## What is alternative accommodation cover?

Alternative accommodation cover pays for somewhere temporary to live if your home becomes **uninhabitable** after an insured event, until repairs are finished. Comparison site Confused.com lists the common triggers as:

- **Flooding**
- **Fire**
- **Subsidence**
- **Storm damage**
- **Escape of water**, such as burst pipes

It's usually part of **buildings insurance**, but it isn't always included as standard. **Contents insurance** may cover storing your belongings if the temporary home doesn't have room for them.

## What does "uninhabitable" mean?

Insurers usually consider a home uninhabitable if it's **structurally damaged** or lacks essentials such as **running water, heating, electricity, or working toilets, showers or baths**. A **loss adjuster** normally assesses the property. If the essentials still work and repairs will be quick, you may be asked to stay put.

## Limits and how long cover lasts

Policies set limits in different ways:

- **A fixed maximum.** Confused.com says these are commonly around £25,000 to £50,000.
- **A percentage of the buildings sum insured**, for example 20%.
- **Some higher-end policies guarantee a period** in a similar property in the same area.

Repairs can take months, especially after a fire. Find out your limit early, so you and your insurer can plan a realistic stay.

## Who arranges the accommodation?

Usually the **insurer chooses**, often through a specialist accommodation provider. Some insurers let you suggest a property, but **they must agree to it before you book**. Don't book and expect to claim it back without approval.

**If you rent:** your tenancy agreement sets out your landlord's responsibilities. Council tenants should speak to their local authority.

## What to ask your insurer

1. **What's my alternative accommodation limit**, and how is it calculated?
2. **How long is the stay likely to be**, and how will extensions work if repairs overrun?
3. **Can we stay in the same area**, near schools, work and family?
4. **How many bedrooms** will we get? Can children have separate rooms?
5. **Is parking included?** Do you cover extra travel costs?
6. **Is storage covered** for belongings that need to come out of the house?
7. **What about pets?** Is kennel or boarding covered, or pet-friendly accommodation?
8. **Who do I call** if something's wrong with the temporary property?

## What a displaced family needs from a temporary home

When your normal life has been turned upside down, the right temporary home really matters:

- **A whole house**, not a hotel room, so children have space and some routine
- **A full kitchen**, because eating out every day is exhausting and expensive
- **Enough bedrooms and bathrooms** for everyone
- **Parking**, so you can keep the school run and commute going
- **A garden or outdoor space** for children
- **Somewhere quiet** to deal with phone calls, contractors and paperwork
- **A location you can manage**, ideally close to your own home and your contractor's site visits

## Keeping life going

- **School:** keep children in their current school if you can. Tell the school what's happened.
- **GP:** if you're staying further away, the NHS says you can register as a temporary patient for up to 3 months, with no ID or proof of address needed.
- **Post:** arrange a redirection.
- **Paperwork:** keep a folder of every receipt, email and call log for your claim.
- **Downtime:** board games, a film night or a walk at Croxteth Hall can help everyone feel more normal.
