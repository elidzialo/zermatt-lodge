Irish racegoers have more options than ever to reach the Grand National: short direct flights into Liverpool from Dublin, Belfast and the west of Ireland, the Belfast to Birkenhead ferry, or the Dublin to Holyhead crossing with a drive across North Wales. The 2027 festival runs from **Thursday 8 to Saturday 10 April**. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, a few miles from Aintree Racecourse, sleeping up to 7 with free parking for two cars, so it suits groups who bring a car over on the ferry as well as those who fly in.

This guide compares the routes and explains how to get from each one to Aintree.

## Option 1: Fly into Liverpool John Lennon Airport

### Which airports have direct flights?

Flight data for Liverpool John Lennon Airport shows direct routes from:

| From | Airlines |
|---|---|
| Dublin | Aer Lingus, Ryanair (about 55 minutes in the air) |
| Cork | Ryanair |
| Knock | Ryanair |
| Shannon | Ryanair |
| Belfast International | easyJet |
| Belfast City | easyJet |
| Derry | easyJet |

Schedules change by season, and some routes run less often than others, so check before you plan. Festival week flights sell out, so book early.

### From the airport to Aintree

The Jockey Club says the airport is about **20 minutes' drive** from Aintree Racecourse. Options include:

- **Hire car** from the airport, if your group wants to drive around Liverpool during the trip
- **Taxi** straight to your accommodation
- **Bus and train:** the Arriva 500 runs between the airport and the city centre, and from there Merseyrail serves Aintree

Our [airport to Fazakerley guide](/guides/liverpool-airport-to-fazakerley) has the step-by-step options for reaching north Liverpool.

## Option 2: Belfast to Birkenhead by ferry

Stena Line runs the Belfast to Liverpool (Birkenhead) route:

- **Crossing time:** about 8 hours
- **Sailings:** typically two a day
- **Foot passengers:** accepted, as well as cars

Birkenhead is on the Wirral, across the River Mersey from Liverpool. With a car, you drive through one of the Mersey road tunnels into Liverpool, which carries a toll. See our [Mersey tunnel tolls guide](/guides/mersey-tunnel-tolls-guide). From the tunnel, it's a straightforward drive north to Aintree and Fazakerley. Check a live route for timings.

Compare the departure times: a sailing that arrives the evening before your race day is far less stressful than one that lands on the morning itself. Our [Belfast ferry guide](/guides/belfast-ferry-birkenhead-stay) covers the terminal and arrival in more detail.

## Option 3: Dublin via Holyhead

There used to be a direct Dublin to Liverpool ferry, but **P&O Ferries ended its Liverpool to Dublin service at the end of 2023** after losing its Liverpool berth. For travellers from Dublin bringing a car, the main route is now:

- **Dublin to Holyhead** with Stena Line (about 3 hours 15 minutes) or Irish Ferries (about 3 hours 30 minutes), each running around four sailings a day
- **Then drive** from Anglesey along the North Wales coast to Liverpool. Check a live route, and allow extra time on busy weekends

Ferry routes change, so always check the operators' current timetables before booking.

## Which option suits your group?

- **A couple or small group with no car needed:** fly, and use taxis and trains
- **A group of 5 to 7 who want a car for the weekend:** take the ferry with your own car, or fly and hire one
- **From Northern Ireland with a car:** Belfast to Birkenhead avoids a drive across Wales
- **From Dublin with a car:** Holyhead is the practical route

## On race day

Whichever way you arrive, the advice for race day is the same:

- **Use the train if you can.** Festival trains run every seven and a half minutes between Liverpool Central/Moorfields and Aintree, according to the Jockey Club
- **Driving?** Car parking must be booked online in advance
- **Bring a card or phone.** The racecourse is cashless for food and drink, and cash is only for on-course bookies. Pounds sterling are used throughout
- **Small bags only.** No bigger than 30cm x 10cm x 20cm, and there's no left luggage

Our [first-timer's guide](/guides/grand-national-first-timers-guide) covers enclosures, dress and the day itself.

## Plan a long weekend

Many Irish groups arrive on the Thursday or Friday and leave on the Sunday or Monday. That gives you time for a day in the city, such as the waterfront, the museums or a match if there's a home fixture, and avoids travelling home tired straight after the big race.
