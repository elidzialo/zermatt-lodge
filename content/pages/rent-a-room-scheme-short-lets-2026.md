If you let a spare room in the home you live in, the Rent a Room Scheme can make the first £7,500 a year tax-free. This guide covers what GOV.UK and HMRC say, in plain English. It is not tax advice: ask HMRC or an accountant about your own case.

## The limit

- **£7,500 a year** tax-free from letting furnished accommodation in your only or main home.
- **£3,750** if someone else also receives income from letting accommodation in the same property, for example a joint owner.
- The limit is the same even if you let for less than 12 months of the year.

These are the figures in HMRC's helpsheet HS223 for the 2025 to 2026 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026), and on the GOV.UK "Rent a room in your home" page.

## Who it is for

The scheme is for a **resident landlord**: someone who lets part of the home they live in. You do not have to own the home. If you rent it, you need your landlord's permission to let a room.

It covers furnished accommodation in your home. HMRC's helpsheet gives a guest house or bed and breakfast run in your home as an example, and it does not set a minimum or maximum length of stay.

## What it does not cover

HMRC's helpsheet lists, among others:

- accommodation that is not part of your main home when you let it
- unfurnished accommodation
- space used as an office or for a business
- a UK home let while you live abroad

The helpsheet does not settle whether a self-contained annexe counts as part of your home. If that is your set-up, read [letting an annexe for short stays](/guides/letting-an-annexe-short-term-uk) and check with HMRC.

## Short stays and booking sites

GOV.UK says that if you only occasionally rent out part of your home, for example through short-term rental apps, you should check whether you need to tell HMRC about the income. Keep a record of every booking and payout from every site you use.

## If you earn more than the limit

| Method | What you pay tax on |
|---|---|
| A | Your actual profit: income minus expenses and capital allowances |
| B | Your income minus £7,500 (or £3,750), with no expenses allowed |

You can choose the one that gives the lower bill. You report it through Self Assessment.
