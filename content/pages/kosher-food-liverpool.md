Kosher food in Liverpool takes some planning. The city has **one kosher deli, The Deli (formerly Roseman's)**, and we couldn't find a kosher restaurant in current published sources. Most visitors **bring supplies or order from shops on the Manchester Beth Din's (MBD) official licensee list**. Whatever you buy, check the current supervision first.

If you're also looking for somewhere to stay, our [kosher homes page](/kosher-homes) lists homes where hosts have declared a kosher kitchen or Shabbat features.

## Kosher food in Liverpool

### The Deli

Local press reports that the shop, now called **The Deli** and formerly **Roseman's**, employs someone to check that its products meet Jewish dietary requirements. Check opening hours before Shabbat and Yom Tov.

### A note on supervision

Kashrut in Liverpool is overseen by the **Liverpool Kashrut Commission**. It's sensible to **ask the commission or the local rabbi** about current supervision before you rely on any shop.

### Eating out

We couldn't find a kosher restaurant or takeaway in Liverpool in current sources. If you're visiting for a simcha or a long stay, plan to cook, or arrange catering from a licensed caterer (see below).

## Kosher food in Greater Manchester

The **Manchester Beth Din** publishes an official list of licensees, and the **London Shechita Board** and **KLBD** publish their own lists.

### Licensed by the Manchester Beth Din

At the time of writing, the MBD list includes, among others:

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Butcher | Glatt Butchers |
| Grocers | Halpern's; Kayes Kosher |
| Bakeries | Brackmans, Royal Bake, The Granary, Bakers Heaven, The Cake Co, The Cookie Jar, State Fayre |
| Fishmongers | Singing Fish; Getzels |
| Restaurants and takeaways | Benny's Bistro, JS, Pagoda, Sushi World and others |
| Caterers | Celia's Kitchen, Niru, Orange Catering and others |

The list changes, so **check the MBD's official licensees page** for current details and addresses before you go.

## How to plan your food for a Liverpool stay

**Before you travel**

- **Bring the basics.** Long-life milk, wine and grape juice, challah or flour, and any meat you need for Shabbat.
- **Order ahead.** Many licensed shops and caterers take orders by phone. Ask whether they deliver to Liverpool or whether you'll need to collect.
- **Bring cool bags and ice packs** for the drive.

**When you arrive**

- **Supermarkets** may stock some kosher-certified products. Check the hechsher on each item rather than assuming.
- **Ask the local community** for practical help. Contact it directly for shul, mikvah and service details too.

**In a rental kitchen**

A standard holiday home won't have a kosher kitchen. Many observant visitors bring:

- Their own pots, pans and cutlery, or disposables
- Foil or liners for oven shelves and worktops
- A hot plate or urn for Shabbat

Ask the host about the kitchen before you book, and check our [Shabbat-friendly stays guide](/guides/kosher-shabbat-friendly-stays-liverpool) for questions to ask.
