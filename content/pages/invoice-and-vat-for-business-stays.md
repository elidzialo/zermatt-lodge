If your company books a holiday home for staff, the rule is simple: **ask for an invoice in the company's name before you pay**. Whether you can reclaim VAT depends on the host. You can only reclaim it if the host is **VAT registered** and gives you a **valid VAT invoice**. Above £250 that means a full invoice showing their VAT number. Under HMRC rules, **self-catering holiday accommodation is standard-rated**, and the 28-day reduced-value rule that applies to hotels **does not apply** to holiday homes.

This guide explains what to ask for, in plain English. It's general information, not tax advice. Your finance team or accountant has the final say.

## Why book direct for business stays

When you book through a large platform, the receipt usually comes from the platform, may not show your company name and may not separate the host's charge from the platform's fees. Booking **direct with the host** usually makes it easier to:

- Get an invoice addressed to **your company**, with a purchase order number if you need one
- List the **guests' names** and stay dates clearly
- Agree **payment terms** that suit your accounts team
- Change names or dates if a project moves

Ask about all of this **before** you book. It's much harder to fix after the stay.

## Is VAT charged on a holiday home?

It depends on the host.

### If the host is VAT registered

HMRC's guidance on hotels and holiday accommodation (VAT Notice 709/3) says that since **1 April 2022**, after the temporary reduced rate ended, VAT should be charged at the **standard rate**.

HMRC counts a wide range of properties as holiday accommodation, including **any house, flat or chalet**. It says accommodation "advertised or held out as suitable for holiday or leisure use is always treated as holiday accommodation".

### If the host isn't VAT registered

Many small hosts aren't VAT registered. A business only has to register once its taxable turnover passes **£90,000** in a rolling 12-month period. If the host isn't registered, **there's no VAT on the bill**, and nothing to reclaim. That doesn't make the booking more expensive. It just means the price you see is the full cost.

**What to ask:** "Are you VAT registered? If so, can you give us a VAT invoice with your VAT number?"

## The 28-day rule: hotels vs holiday homes

You may have heard that VAT drops after 28 nights. That's true for **hotels**, but not for holiday homes.

HMRC says that for guests staying **more than 28 days** in hotels, inns, boarding houses and similar places, from the 29th day "you should charge VAT only on that part of the payment that is not for accommodation".

But the same notice is clear: "The reduced value rule does not apply to holiday accommodation", and "there is no reduced value rule for holiday accommodation."

So for a long project, don't expect the VAT to fall after four weeks in a rented house. Compare the **total cost** instead.

## What a valid VAT invoice must show

If the host is VAT registered and the total is **over £250 including VAT**, you need a **full VAT invoice**. It should show:

1. A unique, sequential **invoice number**
2. The **date of issue**
3. The **tax point**, if different from the issue date
4. The host's **name, address and VAT registration number**
5. **Your company's name and address**
6. A clear **description** of the service, for example "Accommodation, 4-bedroom house, 3 to 10 March"
7. The **extent** of the service, such as number of nights
8. The **price before VAT** and the **VAT rate**
9. The **total before VAT**
10. Any **cash discount** offered
11. The **total VAT** in sterling

A **simplified invoice** is allowed only when the total including VAT is **£250 or less**. For most group stays, you'll need the full version.

**Tip:** if the invoice includes a cleaning fee, ask for it to appear as a separate line. It makes checking the bill easier.

## Deposits, cancellations and extras

HMRC's notice also covers some common business-booking situations:

- **Deposits** are generally treated as advance payments, and **VAT is due when the payment is made**.
- If a booking is **cancelled and the deposit is kept**, VAT remains due on it. It can only be reduced to the extent the money is refunded.
- **Booking fees** are treated like deposits.
- **Service charges** are standard-rated.
- **Voluntary tips** are outside the scope of VAT.

In practice: read the cancellation policy before you pay, especially for projects with uncertain start dates.

## A checklist for bookers

Before you confirm a business stay, get answers to these:

- **Invoice:** can it be addressed to the company, with a PO number?
- **VAT:** is the host VAT registered? Will the invoice show their VAT number?
- **Payment:** card, bank transfer or both? When is payment due?
- **Cancellation:** what's refundable, and by when?
- **Guests:** can names change mid-stay if your crew rotates?
- **Length of stay:** what are the minimum and maximum stays, and is there a monthly rate?
- **Practicalities:** parking, wifi, separate beds and washing facilities
