Rail engineering crews have one need that most guests don't: **they sleep in the day**. A possession that runs from late evening to the early hours means your team gets back at dawn and needs quiet, dark rooms until the afternoon. When you book a crew house on Merseyside, check for separate beds, blackout, enough bathrooms for everyone to wash at once, parking close to the door and a washer for PPE. Get those right and the rest is easy.

## What's happening on the railway this winter

Rail work happens all year, but some recent and upcoming jobs show the pattern:

- **Merseyrail Northern line tunnels:** Network Rail closed part of the Northern line between Hunts Cross and Liverpool Central from **Sunday 11 October to Saturday 7 November 2026**. The £3.5m job was to waterproof and strengthen four tunnels into the city centre from the south, and keep water away from the third rail power supply. Network Rail says power control system modernisation is continuing.
- **Christmas 2026:** Network Rail's festive programme is worth **£152 million** nationally, with journey planners updated to **4 January 2027**. The biggest North West-adjacent job it names is at **Huddersfield**, where the railway is disrupted from Christmas Day until the morning of **1 February 2027**.

Crews often move between jobs across the North West, so a base with quick motorway access matters as much as being close to one site.

## What a rail crew house needs

### Sleep in the day

- **Separate beds.** Most crews won't share. Check the real bed list, not just "sleeps 8".
- **Dark rooms.** Ask whether bedrooms have blackout blinds or curtains. If not, bring eye masks or ask the host if blackout can be added for a longer stay.
- **A quiet house.** Ask about neighbours, building work nearby and the bin day. A detached or semi-detached house on a residential street is usually quieter in the day than a city-centre flat above bars.

### Washing and kit

- **Two bathrooms or more** so everyone can shower after a shift without a queue.
- **Washer and dryer.** Hi-vis and work clothes need washing more than once a week.
- **Somewhere to dry boots and coats.** Ask about a hallway, porch or utility space.
- **Kit storage.** Don't leave tools in vehicles overnight on the street. See our [van parking guide](/guides/van-parking-accommodation-liverpool) for police tool-security advice.

### Food at odd hours

A proper kitchen lets the team eat a hot meal before a night shift and something simple when they get back. Ask about the fridge and freezer size: a crew of six eats a lot.

### Parking

Crews usually travel in vans or shared cars. Ask how many vehicles fit off-street and whether vans are allowed. Shift workers come and go when the street is empty, so being able to park at the door also matters for safety.

## What makes a good base

- **Motorways:** the M57 and M58 meet at Switch Island (M57 junction 7), and the M57 links south to the M62. That puts sites across Merseyside, Lancashire and Cheshire within reach without crossing the city centre.
- **The train:** Zermatt Lodge is an easy train ride from Liverpool city centre.
- **Quieter streets** than the centre, which helps with daytime sleep.

Always check the drive to your actual worksite at the time of day you'll travel. Night-time drives are often quicker than the map's daytime estimate, but don't count on it for start times.

## Booking tips for crew managers

1. **Send the shift pattern** with your enquiry so the host knows people will come and go at night.
2. **Agree the arrival.** A crew finishing a night shift may want to drop bags early. Ask whether that's possible.
3. **Ask for weekly or monthly rates** if the job runs for weeks.
4. **Agree how changes work.** Possessions get moved. Check the cancellation terms and how to shorten or extend a stay.
5. **Get the invoice sorted at the start.** See our guide to [invoices and VAT for business stays](/guides/invoice-and-vat-for-business-stays).
