A holiday home is Shabbat friendly when you can **get in and out without electronics**, **leave lights and heating running safely**, **keep food warm** on a hotplate or urn, and **walk to shul**. Most listings don't say any of this, so you need to ask. Use the checklist below before you book, pack for whatever the kitchen lacks, and take halachic questions to your own rav.

On Book Homes, hosts can declare kosher kitchens, Shabbat set-ups and nearby shuls. Start with our [kosher stays page](/kosher-homes).

## The door: the question most people forget

Many short lets now use **keypads, smart locks or app entry**. On Shabbat that can lock you out of your own rental.

Ask the host:

- Is there a **physical key** you can use for the whole stay?
- If there's a keypad, does it have a **manual key override**?
- Can a **lockbox** be left open, or the key left with you?
- Does the door lock automatically when it closes?
- Is there a **video doorbell** or camera that records as you walk past? Can it be switched off for your stay?

## Lights, sensors and heating

- **Sensor lights.** Ask about motion-sensor lights inside and outside, including on the drive and in the bathroom
- **Fridge light.** STAR-K suggests bringing **strong tape and magnets** for fridges without a plunger switch
- **Heating.** Is it on a timer or thermostat you can set before Shabbat? In a British winter this matters
- **Smart speakers and thermostats.** Ask if they can be unplugged
- **Lamps and timers.** Pack a lamp and a plug-in timer so you can choose where light falls

## Keeping food warm

STAR-K's list of things to bring for Shabbat in a rental:

- Hot water **urn**
- **Hotplate** (plata)
- **Crockpot** (slow cooker)
- **Lamp** and **timer**
- **Strong tape** and **magnets**

Ask the host whether any of these are already in the house, and check there are enough sockets. If you're bringing several appliances, ask if there's a safe spot away from curtains.

## The kitchen: if it isn't kosher

Most holiday homes are not kosher. STAR-K guidance, reprinted on Kashrut.com, gives a practical approach for rentals. In summary:

| Item | What the guidance suggests |
|---|---|
| **Oven** | Double-wrap food (two disposable tin pans rather than foil), or kasher a clean oven at high heat. Never leave it unattended while kashering |
| **Hob** | Gas grates can be kashered in the oven; electric coils by heating until red. On glass tops only the area over the element kashers |
| **Sink** | Not recommended to kasher. Use cold or lukewarm water, or a basin with a rack, with separate sets for meat and dairy |
| **Microwave** | Enclose food in two leakproof wrappings |
| **Grill, dishwasher, can opener** | Don't use. Bring a portable grill if you need one |
| **Cutlery and crockery** | Only temporarily and cold, never with sharp foods; inspect carefully. Don't use the knives |
| **Counters** | Cold items can go on clean counters. Put a towel or trivet under hot pans |

The same guidance covers **tevilah**: if the owner is Jewish, don't use utensils that need tevilah unless the owner has toveled them. A mezuzah on the door is one sign the owner may be Jewish.

**Easiest option:** bring your own pots, a tabletop grill or pizza maker, disposable tins and cutlery, and treat the house kitchen as a worktop and fridge.

This is a summary, not a psak. Ask your rav about your own case.

## The walk to shul

- **Map the route** from the door, not from the postcode centre
- **Check it in the dark.** In a northern UK winter, Shabbat starts mid afternoon. In Liverpool, for example, sunset is earliest on **13 December at 3.54pm**
- **Pavements and lighting.** Some suburban roads have no pavement
- **Eruv.** Don't assume. Ask the local rabbi or community directly
- **Contact the local community first** for shul, mikvah and service details

## Timing your arrival

Winter Fridays leave very little time. Ask for **early check-in** and plan to arrive by early afternoon. Check-in at 3pm or 4pm on a December Friday may simply not work.

Chanukah 2026 begins on **Friday 4 December**, so ask where you can safely light a chanukiah, and where the smoke alarms are.

## Questions to copy and send to a host

1. Is there a physical key, or a keypad with key override?
2. Are there motion-sensor lights inside or outside?
3. Can the heating run on a timer or be left on?
4. Is there a hotplate, urn or slow cooker in the house?
5. Are there enough sockets near the worktop?
6. Can smart speakers and doorbell cameras be switched off?
7. Can we check in early on Friday?
8. How far is the walk to the nearest synagogue?
