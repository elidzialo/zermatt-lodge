Letting a spare room for short stays is one of the simplest ways to host, because you are there. It also means guests are in your home, so it is worth getting the basics right first. Work through this list before you publish your listing anywhere.

## 1. Permission

- **Mortgage:** ask your lender in writing whether you can take paying guests.
- **Renting your home:** you need your landlord's written permission. Letting a room without it can break your tenancy.
- **Leasehold flat:** check the lease. Many leases limit short lets or business use.

## 2. Insurance

Tell your home insurer before you start. Standard home insurance may not cover paying guests, theft by a guest or a guest's injury. Look for cover that includes paying guests and public liability.

## 3. Fire safety

- Do a fire risk assessment and write it down. GOV.UK has a short guide for small paying guest accommodation.
- Fit smoke alarms on every floor and a carbon monoxide alarm near any fuel-burning appliance. Test them before each stay.
- Keep the escape route clear and tell guests how to get out at night.
- Have gas appliances checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

## 4. How many people live there

Count yourself, your family, any lodgers and your guests. GOV.UK says your home may be classed as a **house in multiple occupation (HMO)** if you let rooms to more than 2 people. HMOs have extra safety rules and often need a licence from the council.

## 5. Local rules

Ask your council whether letting rooms to short-stay guests needs planning permission or a licence where you live. A national short-let register for England is expected from March 2027.

## 6. House rules guests can see

Put these on the listing so guests know before they book:

- what is shared (kitchen, bathroom, lounge) and what is not
- quiet hours, smoking, visitors and pets
- check-in and check-out times, and how keys work
- whether you or anyone else is home during the stay

## 7. Your own safety and privacy

- Keep a lock on your own bedroom and a safe place for valuables and documents.
- Message guests before they arrive and agree an arrival time.
- Do not put your street address or house number in photos or listing text; booking sites share it after booking.
- Trust your judgement: you can cancel under the site's rules if something is wrong, but cancelling a confirmed booking can cost you, so set clear rules up front.
