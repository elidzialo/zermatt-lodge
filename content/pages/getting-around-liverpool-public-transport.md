Liverpool is a compact city, and between **Merseyrail trains**, buses, the **Mersey Ferry** and taxis you can get almost anywhere without a car. This guide covers the basics for visitors, especially families and groups staying a few nights.

We checked these prices and rules on 25 November 2026. Fares can change, so check Merseyrail and Merseytravel before you travel.

## Merseyrail: the quickest option

Merseyrail runs the local trains. In the city centre, the key stations are **Liverpool Central, Lime Street, Moorfields and James Street**. Lines run out to Southport, Ormskirk, Kirkby, Hunts Cross, the Wirral, Chester and Ellesmere Port.

### Tap and Go contactless

Since **February 2026**, Merseyrail's **Tap and Go** works with **contactless bank cards, watches and smartphones**, as well as MetroCards.

- **Tap in** at the gates or platform reader at the start of every journey
- **Tap out** at the end
- Use the **same card or device** both times
- Ticket offices are still open for paper tickets

### Day Saver prices

| Ticket | 1 area | All areas |
|---|---|---|
| Adult (19+) | £4.45 | £5.95 |
| Child or young person (5 to 18) | £2.20 | £2.95 |

Day Savers are **not valid between 06.31 and 09.29, Monday to Friday**. Outside those times, and all day at weekends and on bank holidays, they give unlimited travel. Merseyrail also says 15 to 18 year olds can travel at half the adult price.

**Tip for families:** for a day of sightseeing that starts after 9.30am, a Day Saver each usually keeps things simple.

## Buses

Buses in the Liverpool City Region are moving into **public control**. ITV reported in September 2026 that the change starts in St Helens, then the Wirral, and reaches the rest of the city region in 2027. New yellow buses are being introduced.

The region's **£2 bus fare cap** was last extended to the end of 2025 in the announcements we found, so check Merseytravel for today's fares.

## Taxis and late nights

- Black cabs wait at ranks at Lime Street and in the city centre.
- App-based cabs are widely used.
- After a big match or concert, expect queues. Walking to a station or a quieter street often helps.
