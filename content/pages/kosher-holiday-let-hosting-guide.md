Observant families need detail before they book, and a vague "kosher-friendly" line does not help them. The best listings say exactly what is there, in plain words, and are honest about what the host does and does not guarantee.

## Describe the kitchen precisely

Cover these in your listing:

- separate meat and dairy: sinks, worktops, ovens, dishwashers, crockery, cutlery and pots
- what is new or sealed, and what has been used
- whether there is a parev set, and what is labelled
- what guests may and may not do, such as kashering an item themselves
- what you do between guests

Keep it factual. Guests who need more detail will ask, and they can check with the relevant kashrut authority, such as the Manchester Beth Din (MBD), KLBD or the London Beth Din.

## Describe the Shabbat set-up

- **Keys:** a non-electric key, lockbox or keypad that can be left unlocked for Shabbat.
- **Lights:** whether any lights are on motion sensors, including outside lights, and whether they can be switched off.
- **Heating and hot water:** whether the timer can be set in advance.
- **Hot plate and urn:** whether they are supplied.
- **Fridge:** whether the light can be turned off.

## Never imply a certification

A home is not "certified kosher" unless a recognised authority has certified it in writing. Do not use certification names or logos otherwise. On Book Homes every kosher detail is shown as declared by the host, with a note that it is not a certification.

## Keep community details off your listing

Do not list addresses, maps or walking routes to shuls, schools or mikvaot, or service times. If you want to help guests, say they should contact the local community or kashrut authority directly. On Book Homes you can say whether a shul is within walking distance, without naming it.

## Family details that sell

Observant families are often large. Say exactly how many beds there are and who sleeps where, whether there are cots and high chairs, the size of the dining table and how many it seats.
