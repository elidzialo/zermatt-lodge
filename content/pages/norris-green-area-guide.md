Norris Green is a residential suburb in **north-east Liverpool**, bordered by **Fazakerley, Croxteth, West Derby, Clubmoor and Walton**. Its shops are around **Broadway and Broad Lane**, it has the **17-acre Norris Green Park**, and frequent buses run into the city centre. There are very few holiday lets in Norris Green itself. If you're searching for a whole house, [Zermatt Lodge](/zermatt-lodge) is a 4-bedroom house in Liverpool, about 15 minutes by train from the city centre.

## Where Norris Green is

According to Wikipedia, Norris Green's neighbours are:

| Direction | Neighbour |
|---|---|
| North | Fazakerley |
| North, north-east and east | Croxteth |
| South | West Derby |
| South-west | Clubmoor |
| West | Walton |

It's a mainly residential area of family homes, a few miles from the city centre and on the way to the M57 and East Lancashire Road.

## A short history

Norris Green was historically part of Lancashire. The council announced the development in **late 1925**, naming it after the Liverpool-based **Norris family**. The land had been a farming estate.

It was built as one of Liverpool's big inter-war council estates to ease a housing crisis. Wikipedia's article on Norris Green Park says **Lord Derby gave land** for the estate on condition that no pubs were built there, although the main Norris Green article treats the donation claim with caution. Either way, the main residential area still has no pubs; the few nearby are close to Broadway.

The estate was upgraded in the early 1970s. Around 2000, many concrete houses on the **Boot Estate** were found to be defective and demolished, and new homes were built in their place.

The population was about **15,000** at the 2011 census.

## Things to see

**Norris Green Park.** A 17-acre public park between **Broad Lane and Lorenzo Drive**. It holds the ruins of Norris Green mansion, built in 1830 and demolished in 1931. The surviving sandstone wall and arch carries a crest with the motto *"Alte volo"*, "I fly high", and the stable block remains are **Grade II listed**. The Friends of Norris Green Park, formed in 2018, look after it.

**The library.** Norris Green's 1930s art deco public library is one of the area's best-known buildings.

**The old railway bridge.** A large former Cheshire Lines Railway bridge on the old Loop Line is the most obvious landmark as you approach from the north or west.

**Famous faces.** Footballers Steve Coppell and Joe Royle, Coronation Street's Geoffrey Hughes, Holly Johnson of Frankie Goes to Hollywood and Ian McCulloch of Echo and the Bunnymen are all listed among people from Norris Green.

## Shops and food

The district centre is around **Broadway**, with more shops along **Broad Lane** (which is technically in Clubmoor). For a bigger shop, there are supermarkets in the surrounding districts. Takeaways and delivery apps cover the whole area.

## Getting around

- **Bus:** frequent buses run to and from the city centre via Norris Green and Broadway. Wikipedia lists the 14 to Willow Way in Croxteth, and the 61 between Aigburth and Seaforth. Check current times with Merseytravel
- **Train:** there's no station in Norris Green itself. Neighbouring Fazakerley has a Merseyrail Northern line station with trains to **Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes**, around 69 times a day
- **Car:** the East Lancashire Road and the M57 are close by

## Nearby days out

- **Croxteth Hall and Country Park** is just to the east, with a Victorian walled garden (10am to 3pm daily) and a country park open all year. Parking is free in the dedicated areas
- **West Derby Village**, to the south, has a village feel and independent places to eat
- **Anfield and Everton** are a short drive or bus ride away for football
- **Aintree** is to the north, for the racecourse

## Who stays near Norris Green

- **Families** visiting relatives in north Liverpool
- **Hospital visitors** going to Aintree University Hospital or the Walton Centre in Fazakerley
- **Contractors** working on housing, schools and infrastructure in north Liverpool
- **Football fans** who want a quieter base than the city centre

## Tips for staying in this part of Liverpool

- **Bring the car, or use the train.** Free parking at a house lets you drive to Croxteth Hall, the coast or the motorway, then take the Merseyrail train into town without paying city-centre parking
- **Shop on arrival.** A single big supermarket shop on day one saves money for a family or work crew
- **Match days.** When Liverpool or Everton play at home, roads towards Anfield and the docks are busy. Plan journeys around kick-off
- **Hospital visits.** Aintree University Hospital and the Walton Centre are in neighbouring Fazakerley, so check the journey from wherever you stay
- **Respect the neighbours.** This is a residential area of family homes, and quiet evenings are expected
