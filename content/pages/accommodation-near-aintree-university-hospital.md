If you need somewhere to stay near Aintree University Hospital, the closest option for a family or group is to stay in Fazakerley itself, the suburb the hospital sits in. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Fazakerley, close to Fazakerley station (which is right by the hospital's Longmoor Lane entrance), with free parking for two cars and a full kitchen for long days on the wards.

This guide covers how the hospital site works, what parking costs, which concessions exist, and how to choose somewhere to stay when you don't know how long you'll be needed.

## Where Aintree University Hospital is and how to reach it

Aintree University Hospital (postcode L9 7AL) is in Fazakerley, north-east of Liverpool city centre, on the A506 near junction 6 of the M57. You can reach it from two roads:

- **Lower Lane** - the main entrance and outpatient side
- **Longmoor Lane** - the entrance facing Fazakerley station

The site is shared with **The Walton Centre** (the regional neurology and neurosurgery hospital) and Woodlands Hospice. Aintree University Hospital manages parking across the whole site. If your relative is under the Walton Centre, our [Walton Centre family accommodation guide](/guides/walton-centre-family-accommodation) covers that hospital in more detail.

### By train

According to the hospital, Fazakerley station is across the road from the Longmoor Lane entrance, and it's about a 15-minute walk to the main entrance and outpatient area. Fazakerley is on Merseyrail's Northern line, with direct trains to Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes. That makes it easy for relatives arriving at Lime Street to get to the bedside without a car. See our guide to [Fazakerley to Liverpool city centre by train](/guides/fazakerley-to-liverpool-city-centre-by-train).

### By car

Coming from outside Liverpool, you'll usually arrive on the M57 or M58, both of which end close by. The hospital warns that congestion is common at peak times, so allow extra time for morning visiting or clinic slots. There are drop-off and pick-up points outside all main entrances, and freephone taxi lines inside them.

## Parking at Aintree Hospital: what it costs

Parking is the thing families ask about most, and the figures can add up over a long admission.

**The trust's Aintree page currently lists this visitor tariff:**

| Stay | Charge |
|---|---|
| Under 30 minutes | Free |
| Up to 1 hour 30 minutes | £3 |
| Up to 2 hours 30 minutes | £4 |
| Up to 4 hours 30 minutes | £4.50 |
| Over 4 hours 30 minutes | £5.50 |

A weekly ticket is listed at £17.50 from the machines. The short-stay car park by A&E and the Sefton Suite charges £10 for over 90 minutes, so don't use it for a long visit.

**A note of caution:** the Walton Centre's website, which covers the same site, lists a higher tariff (up to £8.50 for over six hours, and £22.50 for a seven-day pass). Prices may have changed since one of the pages was updated, so check the signs and machines on arrival.

The site uses number plate recognition. You pay before you leave, so note your registration. Park only in marked bays, because the trust issues breach notices for parking on verges, yellow lines or hatched areas.

### Concessions worth asking about

The trust lists free parking for:

- **Patients having cancer treatment**, including conditions caused by that treatment
- **Long-term outpatients** with at least three appointments in 30 days and treatment lasting at least three months
- **Blue Badge holders** in marked bays (tell the clinic reception and show your badge)
- **Exceptional circumstances** at a ward or clinic manager's discretion, for example carers or distressed relatives

If a clinic runs more than an hour late, the charge may be reduced by the delay, up to two hours. If you're visiting someone in a critical situation, ask the ward about a concession on your first day. Many families don't realise it exists.

## Choosing where to stay near the hospital

### What matters when a relative is in hospital

When someone is admitted unexpectedly, you rarely know whether you'll need two nights or two weeks. Look for:

- **Short travel to the ward**, ideally walkable or one train stop
- **Flexibility** if discharge dates slip
- **A kitchen**, because hospital cafe food gets old fast and visiting hours don't match restaurant hours
- **Separate bedrooms**, so people on different rotas can sleep
- **Parking that doesn't cost extra**, because you're already paying at the hospital
- **Laundry facilities** if you're also bringing clean clothes for the patient

### Hotels vs a house

Liverpool's hotels are mostly in the city centre, about 15 minutes away by train. That works for a solo visitor on a short trip. For a family with two cars, or several siblings taking turns at the bedside, a whole house usually works out simpler. You get one base, one kitchen and one set of keys, and you aren't paying for several hotel rooms plus city-centre parking. Our [house vs hotel comparison](/guides/house-vs-hotel-liverpool-groups) goes through the trade-offs honestly.

## Practical tips for hospital visitors

- **Save the ward's direct number** as soon as you arrive, and ask when the consultant's ward round usually happens.
- **Agree a visiting rota** if the ward limits visitors at the bedside, so nobody wastes a trip.
- **Bring a phone charger with a long cable.** Bedside sockets can be awkward.
- **Pack slippers, a dressing gown and a labelled bag** for the patient's things.
- **Keep snacks and water in the car.** Long waits happen.
- **Ask about the Patient Travel Cost Policy** if the patient is eligible. The trust directs people to the Cash Office on the ground floor of Aintree Lodge.
- **Look after yourself.** A proper meal and a night's sleep in a real bed make the next day easier.
