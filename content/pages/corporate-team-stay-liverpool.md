When a team of colleagues is in Liverpool for several days on the same project, booking one whole house often works better than a row of hotel rooms: one booking, one invoice, a table to work at and a kitchen so nobody lives on room service. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, about 15 minutes by train from the city centre, with free parking for two cars and beds for up to 7.

This guide is for the person organising the stay, usually an office manager, project lead or PA, and covers the questions that come up when booking a house for a business team.

## When a house makes sense for a business team

A shared house isn't right for every trip. It works best when:

- **The team is 4 to 7 people**, all working on the same project
- **The stay is 3 nights or longer**, so the kitchen and living space get used
- **The work isn't all in the city centre.** For example, a mix of city offices, a site in Knowsley or a client in north Liverpool
- **People are driving in** with kit or samples and need parking
- **The team needs to prepare together** in the evenings: rehearsing a pitch, reviewing documents or planning the next day

A city-centre hotel is still better for a solo executive on a one-night trip, or when everyone needs to walk to the same office.

## Booking checklist for team organisers

### Before you book

1. **Confirm numbers and names.** Who's going and for which nights?
2. **Check the cancellation terms.** Zermatt Lodge uses a Moderate policy: a full refund up to 5 days before check-in, 50% after that. Confirm the team before that point.
3. **Check what's included.** There's a one-off £129 cleaning fee at Zermatt Lodge.
4. **Ask for an invoice in the company name**, and a VAT invoice if your finance team needs one. See [invoices and VAT for business stays](/guides/invoice-and-vat-for-business-stays).
5. **Ask about Wi-Fi.** If the team will be working from the house, ask about the connection and plan a backup hotspot.

### Room allocation

Sort this before arrival to avoid awkwardness. With a king master with en-suite, a second king, a queen and a single:

- Give the en-suite to whoever has the earliest starts, the most senior person, or rotate it on a long stay
- Anyone who needs to take late calls across time zones may prefer the single room
- Put night-owls and early birds at opposite ends of the house if you can

Our [room allocation guide](/guides/room-allocation-guide-group-house) has more ideas.

### Workspace

A dining table seats the team for a working session. Bring:

- Extension leads and chargers
- A portable screen if you'll be reviewing slides together
- Printed materials, as there's no business centre on site

### Travel

- **Train:** Fazakerley to Liverpool Central takes about 15 minutes. Merseyrail now accepts contactless cards, phones and watches. Tap in and tap out
- **Car:** two free spaces at the house. City-centre parking is chargeable, so consider driving one car in and taking the train for the rest
- **Expenses:** keep receipts. Tax guidance from LITRG explains how the temporary workplace rules affect what travel and subsistence can be claimed. Check your company policy

## Making the stay work for the team

- **Agree a breakfast plan.** One big shop on day one saves money and time
- **Share the cooking.** Two or three people cook each night, or order in
- **Respect downtime.** After long days, people need space. Separate bedrooms make this easy
- **Keep the house quiet.** It's in a residential street

A games room with a pool table and a 55" Smart TV is a good way for colleagues to unwind together without going out every night.
