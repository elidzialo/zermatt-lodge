When a crew is away from home for weeks, food is the thing that wears people down. Takeaways every night get expensive, and long days on site need proper fuel. A crew house with a **real kitchen** fixes that. You don't need a chef, just a simple system: one big shop, a few batch-cooked meals, packed lunches made the night before, and safe fridge habits.

## Set the rules on day one

Agree these before the first shop:

- **Who pays?** Company card, per diem, or everyone chips in?
- **Who shops?** One person with a list is faster than five people with ideas.
- **Rota:** who cooks and who washes up each night. Swap, don't skip.
- **Shelf space:** a shelf each in the fridge for personal food, with the rest shared.

## The weekly shop

Do one big shop at the start of the week, and a top-up halfway through for milk, bread and fruit.

**Good crew staples:**

- Rice, pasta, potatoes and wraps
- Mince, chicken thighs and sausages (freeze some on day one)
- Tinned tomatoes, beans and chickpeas
- Frozen veg, which doesn't go off
- Eggs, bread, cereal and porridge oats for quick breakfasts
- Bread rolls, cheese, cooked meats and fruit for lunches

## Batch cooking

Cook two or three big meals early in the week, and portion them up:

- **Chilli or bolognese** in a big pan, served with rice, pasta or potatoes
- **Curry** with rice
- **Tray bakes:** chicken, sausages and veg roasted together

Each one feeds a crew of six or seven for one or two nights.

## Packed lunches

Make lunches the **night before**. It's quicker than a morning rush, and site canteens and vans don't always fit the schedule. A cool bag per person keeps things fresh in a van.

## Food safety in a shared house

UK government guidance on chilling and freezing food says:

- **Fridge:** between **0 and 5C**. Check with a fridge thermometer, ideally once a week.
- **Freezer:** around **-18C**.
- **Cool leftovers** at room temperature and get them in the fridge within **one to two hours**. Split big batches into smaller containers so they cool faster.
- **Eat leftovers within 48 hours,** or freeze them.
- **Defrost in the fridge,** and use food within 24 hours once fully defrosted.

Label tubs with the day they were cooked. It stops the "is this still OK?" debate.

## Shift workers

For night shifts, plan the main meal before the shift and something light on return. Our [rail engineering crew guide](/guides/rail-engineering-crew-accommodation-liverpool) covers daytime sleep and quiet hours.

## What to look for in a crew kitchen

- **A big fridge-freezer** (or two)
- **Enough hob rings and oven space** to cook for everyone at once
- **Big pans and baking trays**
- **A dining table** that seats the whole crew
- **A washer and dryer** nearby for work clothes

## Shopping

Large supermarkets in England can open for six hours on Sundays. Check holiday hours, especially over Christmas. Delivery apps also cover the area.
