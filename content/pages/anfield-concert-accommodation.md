For a concert at Anfield, the best place to stay is somewhere with an easy, reliable route to the official concert shuttles, because there's no parking at the stadium and the streets around it are closed for much of the day. The two practical choices are the city centre, near the Commutation Row shuttle, or somewhere on the Merseyrail Northern line, which links to the Sandhills shuttle.

Anfield has become one of the UK's big summer stadium venues. Here's how concert nights actually work, based on Liverpool FC's ticket-holder information and the bus operator's plans.

## Recent and upcoming concerts

Anfield hosted a run of summer shows in 2026, including Zach Bryan (12 June), Foo Fighters (25 and 27 June) and My Chemical Romance (30 June), according to Stagecoach. In 2025, Liverpool FC's residents' page listed Bruce Springsteen, Dua Lipa and Lana Del Rey.

For 2027, the club has said it will update residents ahead of the summer shows, but no acts or dates were listed at the time of writing. Our guide to [checking the Liverpool concert calendar](/guides/liverpool-concert-calendar-how-to-check) explains where announcements appear first.

## Getting to Anfield on concert day

### The concert shuttles

For the 2026 concerts, Stagecoach ran dedicated shuttles:

| | Details (2026 shows) |
|---|---|
| **Pick-up points** | Commutation Row (city centre, near Lime Street) and Sandhills station |
| **Start** | 3.5 hours before each concert |
| **Return to city centre** | From Arkles Lane, running until everyone has left |
| **Return to Sandhills** | From Walton Lane at Anfield Road, timed to connect with the last trains |
| **Fares** | £5 adult single, £6 adult return; contactless or cash |

Arrangements and fares can change from one tour to the next, so check the information sent with your tickets.

### By train

**Sandhills** is the key station. It's on every Merseyrail Northern line route, so you can reach it from Liverpool Central, Moorfields, Southport, Ormskirk, Kirkby or Hunts Cross, then pick up the shuttle.

### Driving and drop-off

Liverpool FC's information for recent shows was clear:

- **No car parking at the stadium.** Use city centre car parks.
- **Drop-off and pick-up** on Priory Road. Disabled drop-off points are on Gilman Street and Arkles Lane.
- **Don't park on residential streets.** Residents' parking is enforced and cars are fined.
- **Road closures** run for much of the day. For one recent concert, Walton Breck Road was closed to vehicles from 12pm to midnight.

Our [Liverpool match day parking guide](/guides/liverpool-match-day-parking-guide) covers the residents' zones in more detail.

### Taxis

The club named Everton Valley as the nearest taxi rank after the show. Liverpool City Council reminds visitors that black cabs must use the meter inside the city, and private hire cars must be pre-booked. Don't jump into an unbooked car that pulls up.

## The bag rule

This trips up a lot of people. For recent concerts, Liverpool FC said bags were not permitted and there was no bag storage, apart from official merchandise in the clear bag provided. Plan to carry only what fits in your pockets: phone, bank card, ID and keys. That's one more reason to stay somewhere you can leave everything safely.

## Getting back after the show

This is the part to plan properly.

- **Expect queues.** Tens of thousands of people leave at once. The shuttles and trains will be busy for a while.
- **Know your last train.** Sandhills shuttles were timed for the last trains in 2026, but check the actual last departure on your line that night. Evening services run less often than daytime ones.
- **Agree a meeting point.** Phone signal can be patchy in a crowd. Pick a landmark before the show in case you get split up.
- **Have a back-up.** A pre-booked taxi from a pick-up point away from the closures is worth arranging if you've got a long way to go.

## Where to stay: city centre or a short train ride out?

| | City-centre hotel | House on the Northern line |
|---|---|---|
| Shuttle access | Walk to Commutation Row | Train to Sandhills, then shuttle |
| Price on concert nights | Often high, books up fast | Split between your group |
| Parking | Paid city car parks | Free at many houses outside the centre |
| Group together | Separate rooms, maybe separate hotels | One place, one kitchen |
| Noise after the show | Busy streets | Quieter residential area |

Neither is wrong. If you're two people who want to go out in town after, the city centre makes sense. If you're a group of five, six or seven travelling from out of town with a car, a house a short train ride out is usually easier and better value.
