Clean workwear is one of those things nobody thinks about when booking a crew stay, and everyone notices by day four. A house with its own **washer and dryer** saves trips to a launderette and keeps hi-vis looking like hi-vis. This guide covers what to ask before you book, a wash routine that works for a crew, and the safety rules that decide what can and can't be washed at your accommodation.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Your employer's own procedures come first.

## Before you book: questions to ask

- **Is there a washing machine, and a dryer?** A dryer matters in a Merseyside winter
- **Is there somewhere to dry boots and coats?** A porch, hallway or utility space helps
- **Are detergent and supplies provided,** or should the crew bring their own?
- **Is the host happy with work clothes in the machine?** Most are, if you're sensible. Ask anyway
- **How many bathrooms?** Laundry and showers compete for hot water at the same time of day

## What must never be washed at your accommodation

### Asbestos-contaminated clothing

This is the big one. Under **regulation 14 of the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012**:

- Employers must provide **suitable protective clothing** to employees who are, or may be, exposed to asbestos
- Used protective clothing must be **disposed of as asbestos waste** or **cleaned at the exposure site** (if suitably equipped) **or at a suitably equipped laundry**
- Used clothing leaving the premises must first be **packed in a suitable, labelled container**
- If asbestos gets onto someone's own clothes because protective clothing failed, **those clothes are treated as protective clothing** too

HSE's guidance for workers says to wear **disposable overalls suitable for asbestos work** and **never reuse disposable clothing**. None of it belongs in a rental washing machine.

### Other hazardous contamination

The same common-sense rule applies to clothing contaminated with chemicals, lead or anything your site's risk assessment covers. Follow your employer's procedure for decontamination and laundry.

## Who pays for PPE

HSE says employers must make sure **suitable PPE is provided** to employees, and that **no charge can be made to the worker for PPE used only at work**. If your crew is short of overalls or hi-vis, raise it with the site manager rather than buying and washing extra.

## A wash routine for a crew

For four to seven people sharing one washer and dryer:

1. **Wash little and often.** One load every evening beats a backlog at the weekend
2. **Set a rota.** Give each person a night, or wash by type (hi-vis one night, towels the next)
3. **Shake off dirt outside.** Mud and dust in the drum wear the machine and stain the next load
4. **Read the hi-vis care label.** It tells you the maximum wash temperature and whether the garment can be tumble dried
5. **Empty pockets.** Screws, cable ties and pencils break machines
6. **Clean the filter** once a week, and leave the door open to dry after the last load
7. **Boots and coats** dry best in a warm hallway, not on radiators

## Towels and bedding

On a long stay, ask the host how bedding and towel changes work. Some hosts change linen weekly on monthly bookings; others leave spare sets. Agree it at the start so the crew isn't washing bedding as well as workwear.
