The secret to a smooth group trip is to settle the big questions before anyone pays: who's definitely coming, the budget per person, how you'll split the cost, who sleeps where, and what the house rules are. Then book with a payment method that protects you, ideally a credit card, and share the key information with everyone in one message. This checklist works for any holiday house. We've added a few Liverpool tips, as we run Zermatt Lodge, a 4-bedroom house in Liverpool that sleeps 7.

## Step 1: Agree the basics

Before you look at a single listing, get the group to agree:

- **Who's coming.** Get a firm yes or no. "Probably" causes most of the problems later.
- **Dates.** Include travel days. Check if your dates clash with big events, which push prices up and fill houses early. In Liverpool, that means football weekends, big concerts and the Grand National Festival, which runs from 8 to 10 April 2027.
- **Budget per person.** Agree a maximum, including food, travel and activities, not just the house.
- **The type of trip.** A quiet weekend, a city break, a family get-together or a sports trip all need different things.

Write it down in your group chat and pin it.

## Step 2: Check the house fits the group

When you shortlist houses, check:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Number of beds, not just "sleeps"** | "Sleeps 7" might include a sofa bed. Ask for the bed list |
| **Bed sizes** | Couples need doubles or kings. Friends may not want to share |
| **Bathrooms** | Seven people and one bathroom is a slow morning |
| **Parking** | How many cars can park, and is it off-street? |
| **Kitchen and dining space** | Can everyone sit down to eat together? |
| **Location and transport** | How far is it to where you're going, and how do you get back late at night? |
| **Check-in and checkout times** | Plan travel around them |
| **Minimum stay** | Many houses have a two-night minimum, more at busy times |

Ask the host anything that isn't clear. A good host will answer quickly and honestly.

## Step 3: Read the house rules together

Share the rules with everyone before booking, not on arrival. Common ones:

- **No parties or events.** Many hosts cancel bookings that turn out to be for parties, and may keep the money.
- **No extra guests** beyond the people named on the booking.
- **Quiet hours,** especially in residential streets.
- **No smoking** inside, and sometimes not on the property at all.
- **No pets,** unless agreed.

If anyone in the group isn't happy with the rules, find another house. Breaking them can cost the whole group its stay.

## Step 4: Pay safely

### Use a credit card if you can

Action Fraud advises paying by credit card when booking online, as most major card providers protect purchases.

Under **Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act**, if you pay at least part of the cost by credit card, and the price is **over £100 and not more than £30,000**, you can claim from the card provider if the booking goes wrong, for example if the property doesn't exist. The Financial Ombudsman says Section 75 doesn't cover payments made wholly by **debit card** or **bank transfer**. Citizens Advice has a template letter for making a claim.

### Warning signs

Be careful if:

- The host asks you to pay by **bank transfer** to a personal account, especially for a house you found on a listing site
- The price is much lower than similar houses nearby
- The host pressures you to pay quickly
- There's no address, no reviews and no way to check the host exists

If you think you've been scammed, Action Fraud says to tell your bank straight away and report it to Action Fraud online or on **0300 123 2040**.

### Who pays the host?

Usually one person, the organiser, books and pays. That means the organiser carries the risk, so:

- **Collect everyone's share before you pay** the host, or at least the deposit share
- **Keep a simple record** of who has paid what
- **Agree what happens if someone drops out.** Do they lose their share, or find a replacement?

## Step 5: Split the cost fairly

Choose one method and agree it in advance:

- **Equal per adult.** The simplest. Children often pay less or nothing.
- **By room.** The best rooms, such as an en-suite king, cost a bit more. The single room costs less.
- **By couple and single.** Couples pay for one room, singles for one room each.

Remember to split the shared costs too: food shopping, fuel, parking, tickets and any cleaning or damage charges.

## Step 6: Allocate rooms before you arrive

The doorstep scramble for the best room causes more arguments than anything else. Settle it in advance:

- **Couples first** for the double and king beds
- **Families together,** with children near their parents
- **Light sleepers** in the quietest room
- **Early risers** near the stairs, late sleepers away from them
- **A draw** for anything left

Post the room plan in the group chat.

## Step 7: Plan food and the first evening

- **Book restaurants ahead** for groups, especially on Friday and Saturday nights.
- **Plan the first dinner** at the house, so nobody has to go out after a long journey.
- **Agree a shopping list** and who's buying what.
- **Share the cooking.** A rota or pairs per meal works well.

## Step 8: Share the arrival details

A day or two before, send everyone:

- The **address and parking instructions** from the host
- **Check-in time** and how to get in
- **Wi-Fi** and any house instructions
- **The house rules** again
- **Who's driving whom,** and train times if anyone's coming by rail
- **The plan** for the first evening and next day

## Step 9: Leave the house well

- **Follow the checkout instructions:** bins, dishwasher, towels
- **Report any breakages** to the host straight away. It's better coming from you
- **Check every room** for chargers, toothbrushes and coats

## Liverpool tips for group organisers

- **Big event weekends book up early.** If your trip is for a match, a concert or the Grand National, book as soon as you have dates.
- **Bank holidays are busy.** In 2027, the spring bank holiday is Monday 31 May and the summer bank holiday is Monday 30 August.
- **Think about getting home.** If you're staying outside the city centre, check the last train or book taxis in advance.
- **Use the car for days out.** Park at the house and take the train into the city.
