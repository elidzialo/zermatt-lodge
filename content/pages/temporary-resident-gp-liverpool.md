If you're staying in Liverpool for work, a long visit or a hospital stay and need to see a doctor, you can register with a local GP surgery as a temporary patient for up to three months, and you don't need ID or proof of address. For anything that can't wait, use NHS 111, one of the city's urgent treatment centres or walk-in centres (open 8am to 8pm every day), or A&E for emergencies.

This guide is for contractors, NHS locums, families visiting patients, people relocating and anyone else spending a few weeks in Liverpool away from their own GP.

## Which service should you use?

| Situation | Where to go |
|---|---|
| Life-threatening emergency | Call **999** or go to A&E |
| Urgent, but not life-threatening, and you don't know where to go | **NHS 111** (phone or online) |
| Minor injury or illness that needs seeing today | Urgent treatment centre or walk-in centre |
| Ongoing issue, repeat prescription, a few weeks' stay | Register temporarily with a GP |
| Common minor illness or medicines advice | A pharmacy |

## Registering with a GP as a temporary patient

The NHS says:

- You can register with a GP surgery as a **temporary patient for up to 3 months**.
- You need a **temporary services form**, which you get from the surgery.
- You **don't need ID, proof of address or proof of immigration status**. Just your name, date of birth and the address you're staying at.
- After three months, you can re-apply as a temporary patient or register permanently.
- If a surgery refuses, it must write to you within **14 days** explaining why, and **you can still get any treatment you need immediately for up to 14 days**.

### Tips for registering

- **Phone first.** Ask whether the surgery is accepting temporary patients and whether you can pick up the form or fill it in online.
- **Have your home GP's details** to hand, plus a list of your medicines.
- **Bring your repeat prescription slip** if you'll run out of something while you're away.
- **Be ready to wait.** Surgeries are busy, and a routine appointment may not be quick.

## NHS 111

NHS 111 is for urgent problems that aren't emergencies. You can use it online or by phone, any time of day. It can direct you to the right service, book you into an urgent treatment centre in some cases, or arrange for a clinician to call you back.

It's especially useful when you're new to an area and don't know which service is closest or open.

## A&E in north Liverpool

**Aintree University Hospital** on Lower Lane, L9 7AL, has an emergency department. It's also the single receiving site for adult major trauma across Cheshire, Merseyside and the Isle of Man, according to University Hospitals of Liverpool Group. For a serious injury or illness, call 999 rather than driving yourself.

The Royal Liverpool University Hospital serves the city centre and south. Children are generally seen at Alder Hey Children's Hospital in West Derby.

## Medicines and pharmacies

If you've forgotten medication or run out, a pharmacy is often the quickest first step. Pharmacists can advise on minor illnesses and, in some circumstances, supply an emergency amount of a regular medicine. ## A checklist for longer stays

If you're in Liverpool for weeks, for a contract, a placement or to support someone in hospital:

- Save **NHS 111** and **999** in your phone, and note your accommodation's full address for calls.
- Note the nearest **urgent treatment centre** and its hours.
- Carry a **list of your medicines and allergies**.
- Bring enough regular medication, or arrange a prescription before you go.
- If you're staying three months or more, consider **registering permanently** where you're living.
