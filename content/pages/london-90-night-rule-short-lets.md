London has a rule the rest of England does not: a home can be used for short lets for up to 90 nights in a calendar year without planning permission. Go past that and you need permission from your borough. This is a plain summary, not legal advice.

## The rule in one paragraph

The Deregulation Act 2015 relaxed the old London rules. The government said short-term letting is "allowed to a maximum 90 nights in a calendar year", and that the person providing the accommodation "must be liable for council tax", which leaves out business premises. Outside those limits, short letting of a London home counts as a change of use that needs planning permission.

## What counts towards the 90 nights

- Every night the whole home, or part of it, is let for a short stay, on any platform or direct.
- The count resets each calendar year (1 January), not on a rolling 12 months.
- Use a simple log of nights so you can show your total if the council asks.

## Which borough is yours

- **Golders Green:** London Borough of Barnet.
- **Stamford Hill:** mostly the London Borough of Hackney, with parts in Haringey. Haringey has a page on short-term and nightly letting.

Check your exact address with the council before you plan your year.

## Other rules still apply

The 90-night rule only covers planning. You still need the right to let (lease, landlord and lender consent), the usual fire, gas and electrical safety checks, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and holiday let insurance. A national register for England is expected from March 2027: see [England's short-let register](/guides/england-short-let-registration-scheme-2027).

## Using more than one site within 90 nights

If you list on more than one platform, sync your calendars so you do not double book, and keep your own count of nights across all of them. See [how to sync your Airbnb calendar with another site](/guides/sync-airbnb-calendar-with-another-site).
