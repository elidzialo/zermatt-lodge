Liverpool is one of the best cities in the UK for free days out. You can fill a whole weekend without paying for a single ticket, which matters when you're travelling as a family or a big group.

We checked the details below on 23 November 2026.

## 1. World Museum

On William Brown Street, with an aquarium, live bugs and a planetarium. Free entry. See our [museums guide](/guides/liverpool-museums-guide).

## 2. The Walker Art Gallery

Next door to World Museum. Free entry.

## 3. The Museum of Liverpool

At the Pier Head, telling the story of the city. Little Liverpool is a gallery for children under six. Free entry.

## 4. Liverpool Cathedral

The cathedral says visiting is **completely free**, with donations welcome. The **Tower Experience** is the paid extra: £8 standard, £6.50 concession and £22.50 for a family of 2 adults and up to 3 children. The tower closes while the bells are being rung. On Sundays, the cathedral is open for worship from 10am to 12pm, so visit after midday.

## 5. The Metropolitan Cathedral

At the other end of Hope Street. The National Churches Trust lists it as open **7.30am to 6pm** every day, with the **Lutyens Crypt** open 10am to 4pm, Monday to Saturday.

## 6. Central Library and its roof terrace

On William Brown Street. Take the lift to the **fourth floor** for the **roof terrace** and views over the city. The **Picton Reading Room** is one of the most beautiful rooms in Liverpool, and the rare book displays, including Audubon's *Birds of America*, are free to see. The council lists opening hours of 9am to 8pm on weekdays, 9am to 5pm on Saturday and 10am to 5pm on Sunday.

## 7. Another Place, Crosby Beach

Antony Gormley's **100 cast-iron figures** stand along about 3km of beach at Crosby. They arrived in 2005 and are now permanent. **Go at low tide.** Sefton Council says:

- At a tide of **4 metres or lower**, all the statues are visible
- At **9 to 10 metres**, the beach can't be reached for about an hour either side of high tide
- **Don't walk out to the furthest statues.** There are areas of very soft sand and mud. Stay near the promenade

## 8. The waterfront and Pier Head

Walk from the Pier Head past the Three Graces to the Royal Albert Dock. It's free and flat, with benches along the way.

## 9. St George's Hall from outside

The Grade I listed hall is worth seeing from St George's Plateau. Inside, the Heritage Centre has tours and talks. See our [St George's Hall guide](/guides/st-georges-hall-events-guide).

## 10. Parks

Sefton Park, Stanley Park and Croxteth Park are all free to walk in. Our [winter walks guide](/guides/winter-walks-near-liverpool-2026) has ideas for colder months.

## 11. Window shopping at Christmas

The [Christmas markets](/guides/liverpool-christmas-markets-guide) and [Christmas lights](/guides/liverpool-christmas-lights-and-grotto) are free to wander, even if the rides and stalls aren't.

## 12. Tate Liverpool + RIBA North

Tate's temporary home at **Mann Island** is open daily while its Albert Dock gallery is redeveloped. Check the Tate website for which exhibitions are free.

## What's closed

The **Merseyside Maritime Museum** and **International Slavery Museum** are **closed until 2028** for repairs and redevelopment. Don't plan a day around them.
