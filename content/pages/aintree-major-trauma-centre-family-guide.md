If someone you love has been taken to the Major Trauma Centre in Liverpool, they're at Aintree University Hospital on Lower Lane in north Liverpool, the single receiving site for adult major trauma across Cheshire, Merseyside and the Isle of Man. The first job for families is to get there safely, find out which ward or unit they're on, and then sort out parking and somewhere to sleep for what may be a longer stay than you expect.

This guide is written for families who have just dropped everything to travel. It sticks to the practical things that help in the first few days.

## What the Major Trauma Centre is

University Hospitals of Liverpool Group describes Aintree as home to the region's Major Trauma Centre and the single receiving site for adult major trauma patients in Cheshire, Merseyside and the Isle of Man. In plain terms, people with serious, life-threatening injuries are brought here, sometimes past closer hospitals, because the specialist teams are all on one site.

That also explains why so many families arrive from outside Liverpool. Your relative may have been injured in Chester, Southport or the Isle of Man and flown or driven straight to Aintree. The hospital's helipad opened in 2017 and, according to the trust, has handled hundreds of air ambulance landings.

**Children** are generally cared for at Alder Hey Children's Hospital in West Derby, which NHS listings show with its own major trauma service. If your child has been taken there, see our [Alder Hey family accommodation guide](/guides/alder-hey-family-accommodation) instead.

## Your first-day checklist

Shock makes it hard to think clearly. Work through this list and let someone else take on parts of it.

1. **Confirm where your relative is.** Phone the hospital switchboard or the number the police or ambulance service gave you. Ask for the ward or unit name before you set off.
2. **Choose one family contact.** Nurses can't brief ten people separately. Pick one person to take updates and pass them on.
3. **Pack for three nights, not one.** Phone charger, medication, glasses, comfortable layers and a notebook for what doctors tell you.
4. **Bring the patient's details.** NHS number if you have it, GP details, a list of their medicines and any allergies.
5. **Write down your car registration.** You'll need it at the pay machines because the site uses number plate recognition.
6. **Tell work and school.** Most employers will understand. Do it once so you don't have to think about it again.
7. **Eat something.** It sounds obvious, but it's the thing families forget most.

## Getting to Aintree

### From Liverpool city centre or Lime Street

If you've come by train to Liverpool Lime Street, walk to Liverpool Central and take a Merseyrail Northern line train towards Kirkby or Headbolt Lane. Fazakerley is about 15 minutes from Central and the station is across the road from the hospital's Longmoor Lane entrance. Trainline lists the first trains from about 4:46am and the last back towards the city at about 11:36pm on a standard day, but check the timetable on the night you travel.

### By car

The hospital is close to the end of the M57 and M58, so families from Lancashire, North Wales and Cheshire can usually avoid driving through the city centre. Use the sat nav postcode **L9 7AL**. The trust warns that roads around the site are congested at peak times.

### Entrances

The **main entrance is on Lower Lane**. There's also an entrance on **Longmoor Lane**. Drop-off and pick-up points are outside all the main entrances for people who struggle to walk far.

## Parking: what it costs and how to cut it

The hospital's own location page lists these visitor tariffs. The Walton Centre, on the same site, publishes different figures, so check the signs on the day.

| Stay | Visitor car park (UHL page) |
|---|---|
| Under 30 minutes | Free |
| Up to 1.5 hours | £3 |
| Up to 2.5 hours | £4 |
| Up to 4.5 hours | £4.50 |
| Over 4.5 hours | £5.50 |
| Weekly ticket | £17.50 |

Ways to reduce the cost:

- **Ask the nurse in charge.** The trust says ward or clinic managers can grant free parking at their discretion, for example for distressed relatives called in at short notice.
- **Buy the weekly ticket** as soon as you know you'll be visiting for several days.
- **Avoid the short stay car park** by the emergency department for long visits. Over 1.5 hours there is £10.
- **Don't park on verges, pavements or hatched areas.** The trust issues breach notices for this.

## The days after: finding a rhythm

Major trauma care often moves in stages: resuscitation, critical care, a ward, then rehabilitation. Each move can mean new visiting arrangements, so ask the ward each time.

### Visiting

Visiting rules change, and critical care often has its own arrangements. Ask the ward team directly rather than relying on general web pages. Our guide on [how to check hospital visiting times in Liverpool](/guides/liverpool-hospital-visiting-times-how-to-check) explains where to look.

### Looking after yourselves

- **Take shifts.** Not everyone needs to be at the bedside all day. Two people at a time, with others resting, works better over a week.
- **Keep a log.** Write down names, what was said and questions for the next ward round.
- **Ask about PALS.** The Patient Advice and Liaison Service can help when you're unsure who to speak to.

### If your relative has a head or spinal injury

Some trauma patients are transferred to The Walton Centre, the specialist neurosciences hospital on the same site. Our [Walton Centre family accommodation guide](/guides/walton-centre-family-accommodation) covers that building and its parking.

## Somewhere to stay

The area around the hospital is mainly residential, so there's not much hotel stock right next to the site. Many families end up in city-centre hotels and commute back out, paying for parking each day.

Three things matter most when you book:

- **Flexibility.** You may not know how long you need. Ask about extending before you commit.
- **Space for everyone.** Parents, partners and adult children often arrive separately. Being in one place makes sharing news and lifts much easier.
- **A kitchen.** After a few days of hospital food and takeaways, a proper meal helps more than you'd think.
