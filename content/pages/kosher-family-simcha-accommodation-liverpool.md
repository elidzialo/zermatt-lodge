If you're coming to a bar or bat mitzvah, wedding, aufruf or other simcha in Liverpool or Manchester, the first question is **whether it includes Shabbat**. If it does, stay **within walking distance of the shul**: ask the simcha family which shul and venue, and contact the local community directly for any other details. If it's a weekday simcha, a whole house with parking anywhere with good transport is usually easier and cheaper for a big family than a row of hotel rooms. Liverpool and Manchester are only about **31 miles apart**, with the fastest trains taking about **31 minutes**.

For homes where hosts have declared kosher kitchens or Shabbat set-ups, see our [kosher stays page](/kosher-homes).

## Liverpool or Manchester?

Many North West families have relatives in both cities, and simchas often pull guests from each.

| | Liverpool | Manchester |
|---|---|---|
| Kosher shops | Very limited | Many licensed by the Manchester Beth Din |
| Caterers | Ask the family; many use Manchester | Several Beth Din licensed caterers |

## Shabbat simchas: what guests need

For a Shabbat simcha the rules for your accommodation are tighter:

1. **Walking distance.** Map the walk from the actual door to the shul and the kiddush venue. In winter it will be dark on Friday night from mid afternoon
2. **Key, not keypad.** Electronic locks are a problem on Shabbat. Ask for a physical key
3. **Hotplate and urn.** Ask what's in the house, or bring your own
4. **Food.** Agree with the simcha family who's feeding whom, and order anything you need well ahead
5. **Early arrival on Friday.** Ask for early check-in

Our [Shabbat-friendly holiday home checklist](/guides/shabbat-friendly-holiday-home-checklist) has a list of questions to send to hosts.

## Weekday simchas: more choice

For a Sunday wedding or a midweek party, guests can stay further out:

- **A whole house** keeps a family of 6 or 7 together, with one kitchen and space to get dressed
- **Free parking** matters if you're driving between Liverpool and Manchester
- **Trains** between Liverpool Lime Street and Manchester are frequent: around 259 a day on Trainline's listing, from about 31 minutes

## Travelling between Liverpool and Manchester

Many simchas run across both cities: a Shabbat in one and a Sunday wedding in the other, or relatives split between them.

- **On weekdays and Sundays**, the train is often easier than the car. Liverpool Lime Street to Manchester takes from about 31 minutes, with an average of about 51 minutes, and Trainline lists advance singles from £2.70
- **If you drive**, agree who is driving after the wedding before the day, and check parking at the venue
- **Late finishes:** Trainline lists the last Liverpool to Manchester trains around midnight. Check the exact time for your date
- **Big families:** one house in a central spot with parking for two cars can be easier than splitting across hotels in both cities

## Food at the house

If your accommodation isn't kosher, the simplest approach is to treat the kitchen as a fridge and a worktop. Bring sealed food, disposable tins and your own equipment, or order from a Manchester Beth Din licensed caterer and collect it on the way. Our [Shabbat-friendly holiday home checklist](/guides/shabbat-friendly-holiday-home-checklist) summarises published STAR-K guidance on using a rental kitchen.

## When the visit is for sad reasons

Families also travel for a levaya or to sit shiva. If you're coming at short notice, contact the local community directly. Merseyside Jewish Community Care offers hospital visiting and can help arrange kosher meals in Merseyside hospitals. A house nearby can give a family space to be together.

## Simcha guest checklist

- Ask the simcha family which shul and venue, and whether Shabbat is involved
- Book early: one weekend can fill the nearby rentals
- Check walking routes if it's Shabbat
- Plan food and order from licensed shops
- For big families, compare one house with the total cost of several hotel rooms
- Share an arrival plan so everyone isn't parking at once
