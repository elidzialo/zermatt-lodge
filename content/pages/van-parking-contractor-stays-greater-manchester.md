For trades crews, the van is the office, the store and the transport. Where it sleeps matters almost as much as where the crew sleeps. This guide covers what to check before booking a contractor stay in Greater Manchester.

## Ask the host before you book

Send the host:

- How many vans and cars you have.
- Each van's **height and length**, and whether any tow a trailer.
- Whether you need to load or unload at the house.

Then ask:

- How many vehicles fit on the **drive** or private parking?
- Is the rest **on the street**, and is it a permit zone?
- Are there height barriers, low trees or tight turns?
- Is there lighting or CCTV, and where would they suggest parking?

## The rules on parking at night

The Highway Code has a few rules that catch crews out:

- **Rule 248:** you must not park on a road at night facing against the direction of the traffic flow, unless you are in a recognised parking space.
- **Rule 250:** cars and goods vehicles up to 2,500 kg laden may park without lights on a road with a 30 mph limit or less if they are close to the kerb, facing the direction of traffic, at least 10 metres from a junction, or in a recognised parking place. Heavier vehicles and anything with a projecting load must not be left on the road at night without lights.
- **Rule 244:** no pavement parking in London or Scotland, and elsewhere only where signs allow it.
- **Rule 246:** vehicles over 7.5 tonnes laden, including any trailer, must not be parked on a verge or pavement without police permission.

## No Clean Air Zone charge

On 23 January 2025 the government said it had ruled out a Clean Air Zone in Greater Manchester, so drivers will not be charged to drive into the area. It backed an investment-led plan instead, including new lower and zero-emission buses.

## Keep tools safe

- Take expensive tools inside overnight where the house allows it, or use a van vault.
- Park where the van can be seen from the house.
- Do not leave tools on show.
- Check your own insurance for overnight parking away from your usual address.

## Be a good neighbour

Residential streets are shared. Do not block drives or dropped kerbs, keep engine running and loading noise down early and late, and follow the host's parking instructions.
