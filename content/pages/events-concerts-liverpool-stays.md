## Winter 2026

### Christmas in Liverpool

Liverpool City Council's festive programme for 2026 includes:

- **Skate St George's:** a new outdoor ice rink at St George's Hall
- **Liverpool Christmas Wonderland at the Pier Head:** a covered ice rink, rides and games beneath the Three Graces
- **Pop-up markets** on Basnett Street and in the Cunard Building on selected dates
- **Liverpool City Halls** events including A Christmas Carol and Carols by Lantern Light

The council hadn't confirmed every opening date when it announced the programme, so check each event's own website. Our [Liverpool Christmas markets guide](/guides/liverpool-christmas-markets-guide) and [Christmas family stay guide](/guides/christmas-family-stay-liverpool) go into more detail.

### Becher Chase Day at Aintree

Aintree's big winter race day, the Becher Chase, is on **Saturday 5 December 2026**. It's the chance to see horses race over the famous Grand National fences without the April crowds. See our [Becher Chase weekend guide](/guides/becher-chase-weekend-liverpool).

### Tour de France stage finish (3 July 2027)

This one is a first for Liverpool. Liverpool City Council says stage two of the men's race will start in **Keswick**, run through Lancashire, Knowsley and St Helens, and finish with a sprint along **The Strand** on the waterfront on **Saturday 3 July 2027**. Expect road closures across the city region that day, so trains will be the easiest way to get around. Check the council's travel updates nearer the time.

### On the Waterfront and Africa Oyé

On the Waterfront is a summer concert series at the Pier Head, with its sixth edition starting on 17 June 2027. Africa Oyé, a free festival of African and Caribbean music and culture, fills Sefton Park on 19 to 20 June 2027.

### Anfield concerts

Anfield has become a big summer concert venue. In June 2026 it hosted Zach Bryan (12 June), Foo Fighters (25 and 27 June) and My Chemical Romance (30 June). No 2027 shows were listed on Visit Liverpool at the time of writing, but announcements usually come months ahead. ## Where to stay for a big event

### City centre

Best if you want to walk everywhere and go out late. The downside is price and availability on big dates, and very limited, expensive parking. Groups usually need several rooms, sometimes in different hotels.

### Near the venue

For Aintree, Anfield or Everton, staying close sounds ideal, but options right by the grounds are limited and fill first. Streets around both football stadiums have matchday parking restrictions.

### A house on a train line

This is the middle ground. Zermatt Lodge is about **15 minutes by train from Liverpool Central**, and Sandhills (for Everton and the Anfield Soccerbus) is on the Merseyrail Northern line. You get space, parking and a quieter street, and you don't need to drive into the city.

The honest trade-off: you can't walk back from the city centre, so plan your last train or book a taxi after late finishes.

## Tips for event weekends

1. **Book early, then confirm the date.** Event dates can move. Read the cancellation terms before you pay.
2. **Check minimum stays.** Many hosts ask for two nights or more on big weekends.
3. **Plan your trip home.** Trains run less often late at night and on Sundays, and some events change train patterns. During the Grand National, the Jockey Club's travel plan says Kirkby-line trains start and end at Sandhills between about 11:00 and 14:00 and 17:00 and 21:00, so you change there for the city.
4. **Use the car for days out, not the city.** Park at your base and take the train for events.
5. **Keep it calm at the house.** Event weekends are lively in town, but residential streets aren't.
