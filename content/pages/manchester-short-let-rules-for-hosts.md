Thinking of letting your home in Manchester, Salford or Prestwich to guests for short stays? This guide sets out the rules that apply in England in plain English, with links to the official pages. It is general information, not legal or tax advice, so check your own case with your council, lender and an adviser.

## 1. Planning

There is **no national 90-night cap outside London**. Instead, GOV.UK says "your local planning authority will decide whether you need planning permission", based on how the home is used for short-term letting and its impact on neighbours and the local area.

In Greater Manchester, the planning authority is the council for the borough the home is in:

- **Manchester City Council** for areas such as Crumpsall and the city centre.
- **Salford City Council** for Salford.
- **Bury Council** for Prestwich and Whitefield.

If you are not sure, use the GOV.UK "find your local council" tool and ask before you start. In 2024 the government said it planned a new use class for short-term lets and a right for homeowners to let their own main home for up to 90 nights a year. Check with your council whether any of this is in force before you rely on it.

## 2. The national registration scheme

England is getting a national register of short-term lets. It is **not in force yet**. GOV.UK guidance updated on 2 October 2026 says it "is now expected to begin in March 2027". Law firm Clyde & Co noted in October 2026 that the operational details, such as what you must submit, are still being developed.

What to do now: keep your safety certificates, insurance documents and EPC in one place, so you are ready when the register opens.

## 3. Council tax or business rates?

A home in England is rated as self-catering (business rates) only if all of these apply:

- you let it commercially for short periods of 28 nights or less,
- it was available to let for **at least 140 nights** in the last 12 months,
- it was actually let for **at least 70 nights** in the last 12 months, and
- you plan to make it available for at least 140 nights in the next 12 months.

If it does not meet these tests, it stays in council tax. Small business rate relief may apply if it is your only property and its rateable value is under £15,000. You apply to the Valuation Office, and you confirm each year that you still qualify.

## 4. Fire and safety

GOV.UK says hosts must follow the Home Office guide **"making your small paying guest accommodation safe from fire"** for simpler homes (no more than 10 people or 2 floors), or the national sleeping accommodation fire safety guidance for larger or more complex homes. The Home Office guide includes a fire risk assessment checklist.

GOV.UK also points hosts to the HSE guidance on **gas safety** and **electrical safety**, and to the smoke and carbon monoxide alarm guidance for landlords. Read them and keep your records.

You must also check whether you need an **EPC**, and you may need a TV Licence and a music licence if you play music outside the domestic exemption.

## 5. Insurance, leases and mortgages

GOV.UK says you should have dedicated holiday let insurance, public liability cover and buildings and contents cover suitable for short-term letting. A normal home policy usually does not cover paying guests, so tell your insurer.

If you have a mortgage or a lease, read it and ask your lender or freeholder before you start. Many leases and mortgages have rules about letting.

## 6. Tax

The Furnished Holiday Let tax regime was **abolished on 6 April 2025**. From the 2025 to 2026 tax year, income from short-term lets is taxed under the usual residential landlord rules. Keep records of income and costs, and speak to an accountant if you are unsure.

## 8. A starter checklist

1. Ask your council whether you need planning permission.
2. Check your lease, mortgage and insurance.
3. Work through the Home Office fire safety guide and keep the checklist.
4. Check your gas and electrical safety, alarms and EPC.
5. Decide whether you will reach the 140 and 70 night business rates tests.
6. Keep your paperwork ready for the national register due in March 2027.
7. Write clear house rules and tell your neighbours how to reach you.
