Nightly rates are easy to compare and also misleading. A crew pays for rooms, food, parking and laundry, and the costs add up differently in a hotel and a house. This guide gives you a simple way to compare, with no made-up prices: use real quotes for your own dates.

## The hotel side

Work out:

1. **Rooms.** Room rate x number of rooms x nights. Count how many rooms the crew really needs: one each is common on longer jobs.
2. **Meals.** Breakfast if not included, plus evening meals out, per person per night.
3. **Parking.** Many city hotels charge for parking, per vehicle per night. Check whether vans fit.
4. **Laundry.** Hotel laundry or a launderette, per week.
5. **Fees.** Any booking or service fee on the site you book through.

## The house side

Work out:

1. **The total for the stay.** On Book Homes, the price at checkout is the total, including cleaning, with **no guest booking fee**. Any weekly (7+ nights) or monthly (28+ nights) discount the host sets is already taken off.
2. **Groceries.** A weekly shop for the crew. Our [self-catering guide](/guides/self-catering-for-work-crews) has tips for feeding a team cheaply.
3. **Parking.** Contractor-friendly homes on Book Homes list free parking. Check space for your vans.
4. **Laundry.** Contractor-friendly homes list a washing machine, so this is usually zero.

## Put them side by side

| Cost | Hotel | Whole house |
|---|---|---|
| Beds | Rate x rooms x nights | One total for the stay |
| Cleaning | Included | Included in the Book Homes total |
| Meals | Eating out, per person | Groceries for the crew |
| Parking | Often charged per vehicle | Free parking listed |
| Laundry | Charged or launderette | Washing machine in the home |
| Booking fee | Depends where you book | None on Book Homes |
| Evenings | Bedroom only | Living room and kitchen |

As a rule of thumb, the more people and the more nights, the better a house looks, because you pay for one home instead of a row of rooms and you cut the cost of meals.

## Tax and VAT points to check

- **Temporary workplace.** HMRC says a workplace is not temporary if the employee spends 40% or more of their working time there over a period that lasts, or is likely to last, more than 24 months. Inside that, travel and subsistence for the job are usually treated differently from ordinary commuting.
- **VAT on long stays.** HMRC's VAT Notice 709/3 says hotels and similar places charge VAT only on the non-accommodation part of the price from the 29th day of a stay. It also says this reduced value rule does not apply to holiday accommodation.
- **Not every host charges VAT.** A business must register for VAT if its taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in 12 months. Many smaller hosts are not registered, so their prices have no VAT to reclaim. Ask the host.

None of this is tax advice: check your own case with your accountant.
