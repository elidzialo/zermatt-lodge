Before you book accommodation for a work crew, check twelve things: real beds for everyone, parking for your vans, a full kitchen, laundry, cleaning during long stays, quiet hours, Wi-Fi, check-in times that suit shifts, the cancellation policy, how payment works, whether you'll get a proper invoice, and the house rules. Getting these right avoids the most common problems: tired workers, parking tickets and expense claims that bounce. This checklist works for any provider. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, sleeping up to 7, with free parking for 2 cars, about 15 minutes by train from Liverpool city centre and minutes from the M57 and M58.

## The 12 questions

### 1. Does every worker get a real bed?

Ask exactly how many beds there are and what size. "Sleeps 8" can mean six beds and a sofa bed. After a 10-hour shift, nobody wants the sofa.

**Good answer:** a bedroom-by-bedroom list, with bed sizes.

### 2. How many people per room?

Some crews are happy to share; many aren't. Ask whether any rooms are shared and whether there's a single room for a supervisor.

### 3. Where do the vans go?

This catches out more crews than anything else.

- How many vehicles can park **off-street**?
- Is there **height clearance** for high-top vans?
- Are there **residents' parking zones** nearby?
- Is the area safe to leave **tools** in vans overnight, or is there space to bring them inside?

### 4. Is there a full kitchen?

Eating out every night is expensive and gets old fast. Ask about:

- A proper **oven and hob**, not just a microwave
- A **fridge-freezer** big enough for a crew's food
- A **dining table** that seats everyone
- Where the nearest **supermarket** is

### 5. Is there a washing machine and dryer?

Work clothes need washing, often daily. A **washing machine and tumble dryer** in the property is close to essential for stays over a few nights.

### 6. How does cleaning work on long stays?

For stays of a week or more, ask:

- Is there a **mid-stay clean** or linen change, and does it cost extra?
- Can you get **fresh towels** during the stay?
- Who takes the **bins** out and when is collection day?

### 7. What are the house rules and quiet hours?

Crews often keep different hours to neighbours. Ask about quiet hours, smoking (usually banned inside), and whether there are rules on visitors. A property that's clear about rules is usually well managed.

### 8. Does check-in work with your shifts?

If your crew arrives at 9pm after a long drive, a 3pm reception check-in that closes at 6pm won't work. **Self check-in** with a lockbox or keypad is easier.

Also ask about **checkout time** and whether a late checkout is possible on your last day.

### 9. Is the Wi-Fi good enough?

Supervisors need to send reports, and everyone wants to stream in the evening. Ask whether there's Wi-Fi and roughly what speed.

### 10. What's the cancellation policy?

Jobs move. Ask:

- How much notice do you need to give for a **full refund**?
- Can you **shorten or extend** the stay if the job changes?
- What happens if the **start date slips**?

### 11. How do you pay, and is it protected?

- Pay by **card on a secure checkout**, or on a **company account with an invoice**
- Avoid **bank transfers** to people you don't know. MoneySavingExpert warns that bank transfers give you very little protection if something goes wrong
- Ask whether there's a **damage deposit or waiver**, and how it's handled

### 12. Can you get a proper invoice?

Your accounts team will need one. Ask for:

- An invoice in your **company's name**
- The **dates, address and number of guests**
- A breakdown of the **total**
- **VAT details** if your provider is VAT-registered

## A note on expenses and tax

If your workers are travelling to a **temporary workplace**, accommodation costs are often allowable. The Low Incomes Tax Reform Group (LITRG) explains:

- Travel expenses can include **accommodation and subsistence** where there's an overnight stay
- A workplace stops being temporary if someone works there continuously for **more than 24 months**
- HMRC treats duties as significant at a workplace if someone spends **40% or more** of their time there

HMRC's manual also says the 24-month rule is overridden for **fixed-term appointments** where someone works at one site for all, or almost all, of their employment. This is general information, not tax advice. Speak to your accountant about your crew's position.

## Red flags

- **No address until after payment**, or photos that don't match street views
- **Pressure to pay by bank transfer**
- **No written house rules** or terms
- **"Sleeps 10" with four bedrooms**
- **No phone number**, or a host who won't speak on the phone
