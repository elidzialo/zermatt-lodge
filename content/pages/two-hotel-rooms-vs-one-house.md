The headline price of a hotel room and the headline price of a house are not the same thing, so comparing them directly will mislead you. A fair comparison adds up **the whole trip**: every room, every night, every breakfast, every parking ticket and every fee. This guide gives you a simple worksheet to do that for Liverpool, without making up prices. Use real quotes for your own dates.

For the general pros and cons (space, privacy, food), see our [house vs hotel guide for groups](/guides/house-vs-hotel-liverpool-groups). This page is just the maths.

## Step 1: work out how many rooms you really need

Start with the people, not the property.

- **Who will share a bed?** Couples usually will. Friends, colleagues and older children usually won't.
- **Does the hotel offer triples or family rooms?** If not, a group of seven may need four rooms.
- **Is there a single traveller?** They may need a room of their own at full price.

Write down the room mix you would actually book, for example "2 doubles and 1 twin" or "4 twins".

## Step 2: the hotel worksheet

| Line | How to work it out |
|---|---|
| Rooms | Room rate x number of rooms x nights |
| City centre room charge | £2 x rooms x nights, if the hotel is in the Liverpool Accommodation BID area |
| Breakfast | Price per person x people x mornings (if not included) |
| Parking | Daily rate x cars x days, if you drive |
| Booking fees | Any card or platform fees |
| **Hotel total** | Add the lines up |

### About the £2 charge

The Liverpool Accommodation BID company says guests staying in paid accommodation inside the Liverpool Accommodation BID area pay **£2 per room or unit per night**, added to the final bill. It is **per room, not per person**. Three rooms for two nights is £12 on top of the room rates. Check your booking confirmation to see whether it applies to your hotel.

Separately, Liverpool City Council has consulted on how a possible future **Overnight Visitor Levy** would sit alongside the BID. That levy is proposed, not in force, and no amount has been set.

## Step 3: the house worksheet

| Line | How to work it out |
|---|---|
| Nightly rate | Nightly rate x nights (weekends are often priced higher) |
| Cleaning fee | One-off, not per night |
| Platform service fee | Only if you book through a platform. Check the checkout total |
| Food shop | A big shop for breakfasts and a dinner or two |
| Parking | Free if the house has off-street parking |
| **House total** | Add the lines up |

The cleaning fee matters most on short stays. Spread over two nights it adds a lot per night; over four nights much less. That's one reason houses usually win on longer stays and hotels can win on a single night.

## Step 4: compare per person, per night

Divide each total by the number of people and the number of nights. That gives a fair figure to compare, and it's the easiest number to share with your group when you're collecting money.

## Costs people forget

- **Breakfast for seven, every morning.** In a house, one supermarket shop covers it. Remember that large shops in England can only open for six hours on a Sunday, between 10am and 6pm, so shop on Saturday if you arrive late.
- **Parking.** City centre car parks charge by the day. A house with a drive avoids that.
- **Taxis home.** Staying outside the centre can add a taxi on a big night out, but the train often covers it. From Zermatt Lodge, trains reach Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes.
- **Dinners out.** A kitchen and a big dining table mean you can eat in for one night and save the restaurant budget for the other.

## A quick worked example (with blanks)

For seven friends, two nights, driving in two cars:

- **Hotel:** 4 rooms x 2 nights x your room rate, plus £2 x 4 rooms x 2 nights (£16), plus 7 breakfasts x 2 mornings, plus 2 cars x 2 days of parking.
- **House:** 2 nights x the nightly rate, plus the cleaning fee, plus one food shop. Parking free.

Fill in the blanks with real quotes and you'll have your answer in five minutes.

## When the hotel still wins

Be honest about your trip. A hotel is often better for **one night**, for **couples**, for anyone who wants **daily housekeeping**, or if the whole group will be out in the city centre every night without a car.
