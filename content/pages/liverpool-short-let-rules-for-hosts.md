If you want to let your Liverpool home to guests, here's the short version. **Liverpool has no night cap** on short lets. The 90-night rule is **London only**. Instead, whole-home lets are judged **case by case under planning**, and the council's **draft Local Plan** includes a policy on short-term lets. A **national registration scheme for England** is expected to start in **March 2027**. You'll also need proper **fire safety**, and your home moves from council tax to **business rates** only if it's available for 140 days and actually let for 70.

This is a plain guide for new hosts, not legal or tax advice. Rules are changing, so check with Liverpool City Council, gov.uk and a professional before you start.

## 1. Planning

### No night cap, but planning still matters

A short-let management firm's guide to Liverpool, updated in September 2025, puts it simply: Liverpool has no citywide night cap, and "Liverpool relies on planning control". Letting a **whole home** to short-stay guests "can be treated as a material change of use, case by case", depending on how intensively it's used and the effect on neighbours. The council has a planning enforcement route for suspected breaches.

The same guide notes that Liverpool uses **Article 4 Directions** in most conservation areas and for **HMOs in 11 wards** since June 2021, but that no Article 4 specifically for short lets was in force when it was written.

### What's coming

- **Draft Local Plan policy.** Liverpool's draft Local Plan includes a policy, **TC7**, on the development and management of short-term lets. The draft says it would consider the concentration of short lets in a neighbourhood, loss of family homes, noise and antisocial behaviour, parking, and **waste storage and management**. It's a draft, so the wording may change.
- **A new use class.** The government has proposed a new planning use class for short-term lets. Councils could later use Article 4 to remove permitted development rights in particular areas.
- **Local pressure.** Liverpool councillors have previously backed calls for a separate use class, a licensing regime and action on "party houses".

**Our advice:** if you're letting a whole house, especially in a terrace or a block of flats, speak to the council's planning team or a planning consultant first.

## 2. The national registration scheme

England is getting a **national register of short-term lets**.

- **When:** briefings from law firms Clyde & Co and Wedlake Bell in October 2026 said government guidance now expects the scheme to **start in March 2027**.
- **Who:** Wedlake Bell says anyone letting **self-catering property** or offering short-term accommodation for leisure or holiday purposes will need to register. It may also cover serviced apartments.
- **How:** the government says it intends the scheme to be "light-touch, low-cost and primarily digital".
- **Enforcement:** the law allows regulations to ban providing or advertising unregistered properties and to set civil penalties. The details haven't been published yet.

Scotland and Northern Ireland already have registration schemes, and Wales has been introducing one.

## 3. Council tax or business rates?

Since **1 April 2023**, a self-catering property in England is assessed for **business rates** rather than council tax only if the owner can show it:

- **Will be available** to let commercially, for short periods, for at least **140 days** in the coming year
- **Was available** for at least **140 days** in the previous year
- **Was actually let** for at least **70 days** in the previous year

If you don't meet all three, the property **stays on council tax**. Rateable values are set by the Valuation Office Agency, and small business rate relief may apply depending on the year and your circumstances.

## 4. Fire, gas and electrical safety

Paying-guest accommodation has to comply with **fire safety law**. The Home Office publishes a guide called *Making your small paying-guest accommodation safe from fire*. Key steps include:

- A written **fire risk assessment**, reviewed regularly
- Suitable **smoke detection and alarms**, and **carbon monoxide alarms** where required
- **Clear escape routes**, with no locked doors that need a key from inside
- **Regular testing and maintenance**, with records

Wedlake Bell also reminds hosts that **gas and electrical safety** obligations, insurance and record-keeping apply.

## 5. Leases, mortgages and insurance

Before your first booking, check:

- **Your lease or title.** Many leasehold flats ban short-term or holiday letting, or need the freeholder's consent.
- **Your mortgage.** Check whether your lender needs to consent before you let to paying guests.
- **Your insurance.** Check that your policy covers paying guests. Standard home insurance may not.

## 6. Tax

- **Income tax.** The **furnished holiday lettings tax regime was abolished in April 2025**. Ask an accountant how your short-let income is now treated.
- **VAT.** If your business's taxable turnover passes **£90,000** in a rolling 12 months, you must register for VAT. HMRC treats holiday accommodation as standard-rated, and there's **no 28-day reduced-value rule** for holiday accommodation, unlike hotels.

## 7. Being a good neighbour

Council proposals on short lets in Liverpool focus on **noise, waste, antisocial behaviour and party houses**. Good hosts head these off:

- **Set clear house rules** and enforce them. On Book Homes, many hosts state: "Strictly no parties or events - bookings found to be for parties are cancelled."
- **Vet bookings.** Be wary of one-night weekend stays for large groups of young adults with no clear reason for the trip.
- **Sort bins properly.** Know your collection day and make sure guests do too.
- **Give neighbours your number**, so they call you before they call the council.
- **Use a noise monitor** that measures decibels, not recordings, and tell guests it's there.

## 8. A starter checklist

- Check planning, and ask the council if unsure
- Check your lease, mortgage and insurance
- Complete a fire risk assessment and fit alarms
- Get gas and electrical safety checks done
- Decide council tax vs business rates, based on availability and lets
- Speak to an accountant about income tax and VAT
- Watch for the national registration scheme opening, expected March 2027
- Write clear house rules, including a no-parties rule
