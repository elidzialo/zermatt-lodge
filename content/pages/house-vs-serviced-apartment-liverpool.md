For a group of 5 to 7, a whole house usually gives you more space, a proper kitchen, parking and one shared living area, while serviced apartments give you a city-centre location and a walk to everything. If you're mainly in Liverpool for nights out or events in town, a serviced apartment can make sense. If you're a family, a work crew or a group that wants to spend time together, a house is usually the better fit. ## What "serviced accommodation" means in Liverpool

"Serviced accommodation" is a loose term. In Liverpool it usually covers:

- **Aparthotels:** apartment-style rooms in a hotel-like building, often with a reception
- **Serviced apartment blocks:** flats in a city-centre building, run by an operator, with remote check-in and cleaning between guests
- **Individual flats** let on short stays by a management company
- **Whole houses** run as short-term lets, sometimes also marketed as serviced accommodation

Most of the stock is in and around the city centre, the Baltic Triangle and the waterfront. Houses are mostly in the suburbs.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | City-centre serviced apartment | Whole house in the suburbs |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Walk to bars, shops, waterfront | Train or drive into town |
| Sleeping 6 to 7 | Often needs 2+ units | One home, everyone together |
| Living space | Usually one open-plan room per unit | Separate lounge, dining and often a garden |
| Kitchen | Often a compact kitchenette | Usually a full kitchen |
| Parking | Often paid, nearby car parks | Often free on site |
| Noise | Busy streets, especially weekends | Quieter residential streets |
| Visitor charge | £2 a night at larger city-centre hotels and serviced blocks | Not a city-centre block |
| Check-in | Reception or code | Usually self check-in |

## Liverpool's £2 a night visitor charge

From **1 June 2025**, Liverpool's **Accommodation BID** introduced a **£2 a night charge** at **larger hotels and serviced accommodation blocks in the city centre**. It was challenged by Whitbread, and the council said the extra money would be held and refunded if the ballot result was voided. It's a small amount per night, but for a group in several apartments over several nights it's worth knowing. Check your booking terms to see whether it applies.

## When a serviced apartment is the better choice

- **You're 2 to 4 people.** A single apartment is often enough
- **You want to walk everywhere.** Gigs, restaurants and the waterfront are on the doorstep
- **You're not driving.** No need for parking
- **You have late nights planned.** No worrying about the last train
- **You're working in the city centre** on a short business trip

## When a whole house is the better choice

- **You're 5 to 7 people** and want to stay together
- **You're a family** with children who need bedtimes, a garden and space to play
- **You're a work crew** with vans and tools, early starts and shift patterns
- **You want to cook.** A full kitchen and a dining table make longer stays cheaper and healthier
- **You're driving.** Free parking saves daily car park costs
- **You're visiting a hospital** in north Liverpool, such as Aintree University Hospital or The Walton Centre
- **You want quiet.** Residential streets are calmer at night than the city centre

## The location question

The honest downside of a house is distance. Zermatt Lodge is in Fazakerley, north Liverpool. It's not walking distance to the waterfront or Bold Street. What you get instead:

- **Fazakerley station nearby**, with direct Merseyrail trains to **Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes**, according to Trainline
- **Moorfields** on the same line for the business district
- **M57 and M58** minutes away for driving around Merseyside
- **Aintree Shopping Park**, with an M&S Foodhall and free parking, a short drive away for supplies

For most groups, a 15-minute train ride is a fair trade for space and parking. For a group planning to be out until 2am every night, it probably isn't.

National Rail notes that Fazakerley station has step-free access to its platforms, but not between platform 1 and the exit, so check before you travel if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair.

## Cost: how to compare properly

We don't publish example prices here, because rates change with dates and demand. To compare fairly:

1. **Get total prices** for your exact dates, including cleaning fees and any visitor charge.
2. **Count the units.** If a group of 7 needs two apartments, add both together.
3. **Add parking.** A week of city-centre parking for two cars can be a significant cost.
4. **Add eating out.** A kitchenette usually means more meals out than a full kitchen.
5. **Add travel.** Train fares into town from a suburban house are modest, but count them.

## Questions to ask any serviced accommodation provider

- How many people does each unit genuinely sleep, and are any beds sofa beds?
- Is parking included, and where is it?
- Is there a full oven and hob, or just a microwave?
- Are units next to each other if we book more than one?
- What are the noise rules and quiet hours?
- Does the £2 visitor charge apply?
- Can you issue a single invoice in a company name?
