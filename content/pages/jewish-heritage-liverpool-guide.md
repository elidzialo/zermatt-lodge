The best way to see Jewish Liverpool is in two parts. First, book a **tour of Princes Road Synagogue** in Toxteth. It lasts **about 45 minutes** and must be **pre-booked** by email or phone. Then follow the **Jewish Community Trail at the Museum of Liverpool**, which includes the life-size **Galkoff's kosher butcher's shopfront**. With more time, you can arrange a visit to the **historic Deane Road and Long Lane cemeteries**.

Liverpool has one of the oldest Jewish communities in Britain. Local press reports it once had more than 10,000 members, the largest outside London, and around 2,000 today.

## Princes Road Synagogue

### Why it's special

Princes Road Synagogue, home of the **Liverpool Old Hebrew Congregation**, is often described as one of the finest synagogues in Britain. The synagogue describes the building as **Grade I listed**, and Jewish heritage guides praise its **late Victorian interior**. It is about 150 years old and still a working synagogue, used for regular services and for big occasions such as bar and bat mitzvahs and weddings.

### Booking a tour

- **How:** email **office@princesroad.org** or call **0151 709 3431**
- **Who:** pre-booked tours for **adult groups and school groups**
- **How long:** about **45 minutes**
- **Cost:** pre-booked tours carry a charge, but the price isn't published online, so ask when you book

The synagogue doesn't list fixed tour days on its tours page, so contact it well ahead.

**Before you go:** the synagogue is a working place of worship. Dress modestly, ask before taking photos, and check whether you need to bring photo ID.

### Attending a service

Princes Road lists Shabbat morning services. It asks visitors to **email the office in advance for security reasons**, which is common practice at UK synagogues. If you're observant, remember that Liverpool's other Orthodox congregations, in **Childwall and Allerton**, are a long walk from Toxteth. Plan where you'll stay accordingly. ## The Jewish Community Trail at the Museum of Liverpool

National Museums Liverpool publishes a free **Liverpool Jewish Community Trail** for the Museum of Liverpool on the waterfront. Look for the **menorah symbol** next to trail objects. Highlights include:

**Floor one**

- **The first synagogue.** The museum's timeline marks the first purpose-built synagogue, on **Seel Street in 1807**, which seated 300 people.
- **Brian Epstein's autobiography,** *A Cellarful of Noise* (1964). The Beatles' manager writes about his Jewish upbringing in Liverpool.

**Floor two**

- **Yentl on the Mersey.** Barbra Streisand's 1983 film shot scenes on a Mersey ferry, and local Jewish community members, many descended from Russian migrants, were cast as extras.
- **Frankie Vaughan.** A signed photo and one of his straw boaters. Vaughan, born to a Jewish immigrant family in Liverpool, became a world-famous singer.
- **Sarah Levy's identity book.** Her family arrived in the 1880s, fleeing persecution in Russia.
- **David Lewis's merchant token.** Lewis founded Lewis's department store in 1856, and his charity still helps young people.
- **Torah scroll decorations** used at Princes Road Synagogue.

### Galkoff's and the Secret Life of Pembroke Place

The trail's centrepiece is a **life-size reconstruction of Galkoff's**, a kosher butcher's shop from the old Jewish quarter around London Road and Pembroke Place. Its famous **green tiles were added in 1933** with Hebrew "kosher" lettering. The museum says **113 of the 855 tiles** were replaced during conservation, and the tiles were moved to the museum in **2018**. Nearby you'll find the butcher's coat and knife of Issy Silver, who worked there from the late 1940s, and a model of the shop made by a King David High School teacher.

The museum sometimes runs a ticketed talk, **"Beyond the label: Galkoff's tiles"**, listed at **£7**. No dates were listed when we checked, so look at the museum's events page before your trip.

## Cemeteries and other sites

Jewish heritage guides list several sites you can visit **by arrangement**:

- **Deane Road Cemetery** (Deane Road, L7). Its grounds have been fully restored. Contact Princes Road Synagogue for details.
- **Long Lane Cemetery** (Long Lane, L9). Visits by appointment only.
- **Greenbank Drive** (near Sefton Park). A former synagogue, closed in the late 2000s and not open to visitors.

The **Liverpool Record Office** at Central Library holds the Merseyside Jewish archives, which hosted an exhibition in 2025. Check with the library about current displays and research access.
