**Cammell Laird** is one of the best-known names in British shipbuilding, and its Birkenhead yard brings regular work for welders, fitters, electricians, painters and specialist marine contractors. This guide covers what the yard does now, how to cross the Mersey, and how to choose between a Wirral base and a Liverpool one.

We used public sources only, checked on 22 November 2026. Site access, inductions and parking are set by the yard, so confirm them with your contact.

## The yard today

- **Location:** Birkenhead, on the Wirral side of the Mersey
- **History:** the yard dates to **1828**. Famous builds include the aircraft carrier **Ark Royal** (commissioned 1955) and the polar research ship **RRS Sir David Attenborough**
- **New owner:** in **June 2026**, Maritime Executive reported that **Balaena**, a Cornwall-based maritime engineering group, had bought **APCL Group**, the Peel Group subsidiary that held Cammell Laird, A&P Tyne and two Falmouth yards. The combined group has more than 2,000 employees and 12 dry docks
- **Current work:** blocks for **Royal Navy aircraft carriers and submarines**, work on **Type 26 frigates**, and **repair services** to the Ministry of Defence, according to the same report
- **Workforce:** Wikipedia lists more than 550 employees

Defence and repair work often comes in peaks, with extra crews for a dry dock period or a build phase. That's when short-notice, flexible accommodation matters most.

## Crossing the Mersey

### By road

- The **Kingsway Tunnel** runs between **Liverpool and Wallasey** and carries the A59. It's the **only Mersey tunnel that takes HGVs**
- The tunnels are **tolled**. Merseytravel takes cash or card at the booths and is moving drivers from **Fast Tag to T-FLOW**, an account linked to your number plate. Check the current toll for your vehicle class on Merseytravel's site
- A daily crossing for several vans adds up over a long job. Factor it into the budget

### By train

- Merseyrail's **Wirral line** runs under the river from Liverpool city centre stations to Birkenhead
- From the Liverpool side, take the train into the city and change to the Wirral line at **Moorfields** or **Liverpool Central**, which interchange with both lines. Check the full journey in a planner

## Wirral base or Liverpool base?

| Base | Best for |
|---|---|
| Birkenhead or the Wirral | Crews working every shift at the yard, no tunnel tolls |
| Liverpool | Crews who drive, also work Liverpool or Merseyside sites, or want a whole house |

Be realistic: if your team's only job is at the yard, a Wirral base will usually mean a shorter commute. If the crew moves between the yard, the Liverpool docks and sites off the M57 and M58, a Liverpool base can work well.
