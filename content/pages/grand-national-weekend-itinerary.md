The 2027 Grand National Festival runs from Thursday 8 to Saturday 10 April, with the big race at 4pm on the Saturday. The best weekend plan mixes one or two race days with a day in Liverpool city centre and a slow Sunday before the drive home. This itinerary assumes you're staying in north Liverpool, close to Aintree: Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Fazakerley, next to Aintree, sleeping up to 7, with free parking for 2 cars and trains to Liverpool city centre in about 15 minutes.

You don't need to go racing every day. Most groups pick one or two days and spend the rest exploring.

## Key 2027 timings

From the Jockey Club's event schedule:

| Day | Date | Gates open | First race | Highlight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening Day | Thursday 8 April | 11:00am | 1:45pm | Festival opener |
| Ladies Day | Friday 9 April | 10:30am | 1:45pm | The most dressed-up day |
| Grand National Day | Saturday 10 April | 10:00am | 12:45pm | Randox Grand National at 4:00pm |

Two things worth knowing for 2027:

- **It's in the Easter holidays.** Liverpool City Council's 2027 Easter school holiday runs from 26 March to 9 April, so the city and the trains will be busier than usual, and families may be travelling too.
- **Car parks open at 6am** on race days, according to the Jockey Club, but you can't enter the course until the gates open.

## Before you go: a checklist

- **Racing tickets** for the day or days you want. Choose your enclosure early
- **Accommodation** booked well ahead. Festival week is one of the busiest of the year in north Liverpool
- **Parking** booked online if you're driving to the course. The Jockey Club says car parking must be booked in advance
- **Small bag only:** nothing bigger than 30cm x 10cm x 20cm
- **A card or phone to pay.** The racecourse is cashless, though bookmakers take cash
- **Layers and a waterproof.** April weather on an open racecourse can turn quickly

## Thursday: arrive and settle in (or go to Opening Day)

**Option A: arrive and settle in**

- Arrive from **3pm** (Zermatt Lodge check-in time) and unpack
- Do a big food shop for the weekend. Aintree Shopping Park, with an M&S Foodhall, is a short drive away
- Cook together, plan who's going on which day and sort outfits for Friday

**Option B: go straight to Opening Day**

If you can get in early or arrive the night before, Thursday is usually the calmest day of the festival. The first race is at **1:45pm**, so there's time for a relaxed lunch first. It's a good choice for groups who care more about the racing than the crowds.

## Friday: Ladies Day

Ladies Day is the social highlight for many groups. See our [Ladies Day group stay guide](/guides/grand-national-ladies-day-group-stay) for more.

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| 9:00am | Breakfast at the house, start getting ready |
| 11:00am | Leave for the course |
| 11:30am | Through the gates (they open at 10:30am) and find your spot |
| 1:45pm | First race |
| After the last race | Head home or wait for the queues to thin |
| Evening | Dinner back at the house, or a table booked nearby |

### Getting to the course

From the Fazakerley area, you can drive (with pre-booked parking) or take a taxi to a drop-off point. By train, Aintree station is on Merseyrail's Ormskirk branch, while Fazakerley is on the Kirkby branch, so you'd need to change. The Jockey Club's festival travel plan says Kirkby-line trains start and end at Sandhills at certain times on festival days, and Aintree trains run more often than usual. Check Merseyrail's live planner on the day. Our guide to [getting to Aintree from Fazakerley](/guides/getting-to-aintree-racecourse-from-fazakerley) has the details.

## Saturday: Grand National Day

The big one. Gates open at **10:00am**, the first race is at **12:45pm** and the **Randox Grand National is at 4:00pm**.

- **Leave early.** Roads and trains around Aintree are at their busiest on Saturday
- **Eat before you go.** Queues for food are long on National day
- **Agree a meeting point** in case phones die or signal drops in the crowds
- **Plan your exit.** Leaving straight after the National means joining the biggest crowds. Many people stay for the later races and leave once queues ease

### Not going racing on Saturday?

Plenty of groups only buy tickets for one day. If some of you aren't at the course on Saturday:

- **Watch the race on TV** at the house (there's a 55" Smart TV)
- Run a **sweepstake** for the National
- Go into the city in the morning and be back before 4pm

## Sunday: city day and slow recovery

After two days of racing, Sunday is for a relaxed lie-in and a gentle look around Liverpool.

- **Late breakfast** at the house
- **Train into the city** from Fazakerley: about 15 minutes to Liverpool Central
- **Waterfront walk** from Liverpool ONE to the Royal Albert Dock and Pier Head
- **Free museums** on the waterfront for a rainy afternoon
- **Sunday roast** in the city or back at the house

If you're driving home on Monday, Sunday evening is a good time for a game of pool and an early night.

## Monday: checkout and home

Checkout is by **11am**. Leave the M57 and M58 traffic to settle after the weekend and you'll be on the motorway in minutes.

## Budgeting the weekend

Costs vary a lot by enclosure, transport and how many days you race, so we won't guess at figures. The biggest variables are:

- **Racing tickets**, which differ hugely by enclosure and day
- **Parking or taxis** on race days
- **Food and drink** at the course
- **Accommodation**, which rises sharply during festival week

Sharing a whole house can keep the accommodation cost per person down compared with several hotel rooms, and cooking a couple of meals at home saves money too. See our comparison of a [house vs hotel for groups](/guides/house-vs-hotel-liverpool-groups).
