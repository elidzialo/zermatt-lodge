Booking a crew into one home is simple once you have the right details in front of you. This guide walks through it in order. For homes that already meet the basics, see [contractor and work crew accommodation](/contractors).

## 1. Get the headcount and the room plan

Write down how many people are going, and how many need their own room. A home that "sleeps 8" may have two double beds that colleagues will not want to share. Ask the host for the **bed list**: how many bedrooms, and which beds are doubles, twins, singles or sofa beds.

## 2. Count the vehicles

List every van and car, with rough height and length. Then ask the host:

- How many vehicles fit on the drive, and is the rest on the street?
- Are there permit zones, height barriers or narrow turns?
- Where should vans park so neighbours are not blocked?

Our guide to [van parking for contractor stays in Greater Manchester](/guides/van-parking-contractor-stays-greater-manchester) covers the parking rules worth knowing.

## 3. Shortlist homes that fit how a crew lives

For a stay of more than a couple of nights, look for:

- A **kitchen** and a fridge big enough for a weekly shop.
- A **washing machine**, ideally a dryer too, for workwear.
- **Wi-Fi** for timesheets, drawings and calls home.
- **Self check-in** if the team arrives late from another job.

On Book Homes, the [contractor-friendly filter](/?for=contractors) shows homes with at least 2 bedrooms that list free parking, a washing machine, a kitchen and Wi-Fi.

## 4. Agree the paperwork before you pay

Decide who pays and what finance needs. On most Book Homes listings you can tick a box at checkout for a **company receipt (PDF)** with your company name. If you need a VAT invoice, ask the host first, because only VAT-registered hosts can charge VAT. Our [invoices and VAT guide](/guides/invoice-and-vat-for-business-stays) explains what to ask for.

If staff are working at a **temporary workplace**, HMRC's 24-month rule sets out when travel and subsistence count as business costs. HMRC says a workplace stops being temporary when someone spends 40% or more of their working time there over a period that lasts, or is likely to last, more than 24 months. Check with your accountant for your own case.

## 5. Book the full job, with room to extend

Book the whole expected length of the job. Some hosts set a **weekly discount** (7 nights or more) or a **monthly discount** (28 nights or more), and it shows in the total before you pay. Ask the host whether the following weeks are free, in case the job runs over.

## 6. Share the house rules with the crew

These are homes on residential streets. Send the team the host's rules before they arrive: quiet hours, smoking, bins, and where to park. Our [house share etiquette guide](/guides/trades-team-house-share-etiquette) is a good one to forward.

## 7. Keep one contact

Give the host one name and number for the crew, and tell them about any changes to dates or headcount straight away.
