Airbnb now charges most hosts one fee: a 15.5% host-only service fee, taken from your payout. The old split model, where hosts paid about 3% and guests paid a separate service fee on top, has been phased out for hosts outside the EU during 2026. If you host in the UK and your payouts dropped this year, this is usually why.

## What changed in 2026

Airbnb's own page for hosts says it is "combining both fees into a single 15.5% service fee paid by hosts", and that hosts using property management software who were not yet on the single fee moved on 13 October 2026. Hostfully reports that independent hosts outside the EU, which includes the UK, moved to the host-only fee on 15 September 2026, and that bookings confirmed before a host's switch date keep their old fee terms.

Airbnb notes that exceptions may apply, and that listings in Brazil and Mexico stay at 16%.

## What the 15.5% means for your payout

| Booking subtotal | Airbnb fee at 15.5% | You receive |
|---|---|---|
| £500 | £77.50 | £422.50 |
| £1,000 | £155 | £845 |
| £2,000 | £310 | £1,690 |

The guest pays the price you set, with no separate Airbnb guest fee on host-only fee listings. That is why many hosts build the fee into their nightly rate.

## How other channels compare

- **Booking.com:** commission varies. Guesty puts the average at around 15%, with a range of 10% to 25% depending on market and property type.
- **Book Homes:** 15% commission per booking, or 3% for kosher hosts. No guest booking fee, no sign-up or monthly fee. Once payouts are connected, Stripe takes its card fee from your share, which on Stripe's UK pricing is 1.5% + 20p for standard UK cards.

To be straight with you: on a £1,000 booking with a UK card, a standard Book Homes host keeps about £834.80, against about £845 on Airbnb. Kosher hosts keep about £954.80. For most hosts the case for adding Book Homes is a second source of bookings, with calendars kept in sync, rather than a lower fee.
