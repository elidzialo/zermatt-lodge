Chanukah 2026 begins on the evening of **Friday 4 December** and ends at nightfall on **Saturday 12 December**. That timing matters if you're away from home: the **first and the eighth candles are both lit on a Friday**, before Shabbat candles, and both Saturdays of the festival are Shabbat. If you're spending the festival in Liverpool, plan your candles, your food and your base before you travel.

This guide uses published sources only. For anything halachic, ask your own rabbi. For homes where hosts have declared kosher kitchens or Shabbat features, see our [kosher homes page](/kosher-homes).

## Friday and Saturday lighting

Both Fridays need a little planning. Chabad.org's Chanukah guide says:

- **Light the Chanukah candles before the Shabbat candles** on Friday
- **Use larger candles or extra oil**, so they burn until **half an hour after nightfall**. Standard 30-minute Chanukah candles aren't suitable on Friday
- **Don't relight, move or prepare the menorah** from Shabbat candle lighting until havdalah
- **On Saturday night**, light only after Shabbat ends after nightfall

Liverpool's winter afternoons are short. Sunset is at its earliest on **13 December, at 3.54pm**, so on both Fridays you'll be lighting mid afternoon. Get the exact Shabbat time from a Jewish calendar.

## Lighting a menorah in a rental

Most holiday homes have smoke alarms, and many have rules on candles. Before you book:

1. **Ask the host** whether you can light candles, and where
2. **Find a safe spot.** Chabad.org advises a sturdy, fireproof surface, out of reach of children, away from curtains and anything flammable
3. **Windowsills and doorways** are the traditional places. Check the sill is deep enough and clear of blinds
4. **Ask where the smoke alarms are**, and never disable one
5. **Bring a foil tray** to sit the menorah on, and long candles for the Fridays

If open flames aren't allowed, ask your rabbi what to do before you travel.
