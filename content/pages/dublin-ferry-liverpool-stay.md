There's currently no passenger ferry between Dublin and Liverpool. P&O Ferries closed its Dublin to Liverpool route at the end of 2023, and the Stena Line service that started between Dublin and Birkenhead in 2024 was launched as a freight route. Most people travelling from Dublin to Liverpool with a car now sail to Holyhead and drive across North Wales, or fly.

Plenty of websites still list the old route, so this guide sets out what actually runs, how to plan the journey, and where Irish groups can stay once they arrive.

## What happened to the Liverpool to Dublin ferry?

- **August 2023:** P&O Ferries announced it would close its Dublin to Liverpool route, saying it had no Liverpool berth for 2024 after talks with the port owner failed. Afloat reported the ships would be moved elsewhere in P&O's network.
- **December 2023:** Freightlink reported the last P&O sailing from Dublin on 21 December 2023, with the service closing for good at the end of the month.
- **February 2024:** Stena Line began a Dublin to Liverpool (Birkenhead) route from the 12 Quays terminal. Peel Ports described it as a **freight route**.
- **Now:** Direct Ferries says there are **no ferries currently running between Liverpool Birkenhead and Dublin** for its customers.

Routes do change. Check Stena Line and other operators directly in case passenger sailings return.

## Your options from Ireland to Liverpool

### Dublin to Holyhead, then drive

This is the most popular option for Irish drivers.

- **Operators:** Irish Ferries and Stena Line
- **Sailings:** Direct Ferries lists about **58 a week**
- **Crossing time:** roughly 3 to 3.5 hours
- **Foot passengers:** accepted by both operators

From Holyhead, the A55 runs along the North Wales coast towards Chester and Merseyside. Journey times vary a lot with traffic and roadworks, so check a live route on the day you sail.

### Belfast to Birkenhead

If you're coming from Northern Ireland, Stena Line sails between Belfast and Birkenhead.

- **Sailings:** about 2 a day, 14 a week
- **Crossing time:** around 8 hours
- **Liverpool terminal:** 12 Quays Terminal, Tower Road, Birkenhead CH41 1FE
- **Foot passengers:** accepted. Direct Ferries advises checking in between 1 hour 45 minutes and 30 minutes before departure.

Birkenhead is on the Wirral, across the river from Liverpool. You reach Liverpool through the Mersey tunnels or by Merseyrail. Our [Belfast ferry guide](/guides/belfast-ferry-birkenhead-stay) covers this route in more detail.

### Flying

Dublin to Liverpool John Lennon Airport is a short flight. Our [Liverpool airport guide](/guides/liverpool-airport-to-fazakerley) explains the buses and trains from the airport.

## Planning tips for Irish visitors

- **Sailing times dictate your arrival.** An overnight or late sailing may mean arriving in Liverpool in the early hours. Book accommodation from the night you land, or check you can arrive late.
- **Motorway access matters.** If you're driving from Holyhead or the Wirral, a base near the motorway network saves time.
- **Parking is the hidden cost.** City-centre car parks charge by the day. Free parking at your accommodation adds up over a long weekend.
- **Big events book up early.** The Grand National Festival (8 to 10 April 2027) and big matches at Anfield or Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium attract thousands of Irish fans. See our guide to [the Grand National from Ireland](/guides/grand-national-from-ireland).

## Choosing between the routes

Which route suits you depends on where you start and what you're bringing.

- **From Dublin or the south, with a car:** Holyhead is usually the practical choice. The crossing is short and there are several sailings a day, so you can pick one that gets you to Liverpool at a sensible hour.
- **From Belfast or the north, with a car:** the Belfast to Birkenhead sailing lands you right on Merseyside, with no long drive at the end.
- **Without a car:** flying is normally quickest. Foot passengers can use both ferry routes, but getting from Holyhead or the 12 Quays terminal onwards takes more planning.
- **With a lot of luggage or kids:** a longer crossing with a cabin can be easier than an early flight and a hire car.

Whichever you choose, keep a little slack in your plans. Ferries can be delayed or cancelled in bad weather on the Irish Sea, especially in winter.

## Why Irish visitors come to Liverpool

Liverpool has deep Irish roots, and it shows in the visitors: football supporters for Liverpool and Everton, racegoers for Aintree, families visiting relatives, and groups on weekends away. Many come by car because it lets them bring more and travel on their own schedule.
