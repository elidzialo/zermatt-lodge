You do not need to leave Airbnb to stop relying on it. Most UK hosts who list elsewhere keep Airbnb and add one or two more channels, then connect the calendars so a booking anywhere closes the dates everywhere. This guide covers the main options. We run Book Homes, so we are not neutral, but the facts below are sourced.

## Why hosts add a second channel

- **Fees changed.** Airbnb now charges most hosts a single 15.5% host-only fee. See [Airbnb host fees UK 2026 explained](/guides/airbnb-host-fees-uk-2026-explained).
- **One platform is a single point of failure.** A paused listing, a search ranking change or a bad month leaves you with nothing else.
- **Different guests search in different places.** Families, contractors and observant guests often look beyond the biggest sites.

## The main options

| Channel | Who books | Fee (check your own terms) | Calendar sync |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booking.com | Large audience, many short city stays | Varies; Guesty puts the average around 15% (10% to 25%) | iCal or channel manager |
| Vrbo | Families and whole-home holidays | Check Vrbo's current host terms | iCal or channel manager |
| Your own booking site | Repeat guests, people who find you by name | Website and card fees only | Depends on your software |
| Book Homes | Whole homes for families, groups, work stays and kosher-friendly guests in our areas | 15% per booking, 3% for kosher hosts, plus Stripe's card fee | iCal in and out, every 15 minutes on our side |

## How to choose

1. **Start with where your guests already are.** If many of your guests are work crews or big families, a marketplace that filters for those helps more than another general site.
2. **Keep it manageable.** Two or three channels with synced calendars is plenty for a single home.
3. **Compare the take-home, not the headline fee.** Include card fees and any guest fee that makes your total look higher.
4. **Keep your listing details the same everywhere.** Same photos, same house rules, same cancellation terms, so nothing contradicts.
