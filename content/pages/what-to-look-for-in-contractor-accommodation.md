Good contractor accommodation is less about style and more about whether the crew can sleep, eat, wash their gear and get to site without hassle. These are the things that matter most, in order.

## Beds, not "sleeps"

The guest number on a listing tells you the most people allowed, not how they sleep. Ask for the **bed list**. Most crews want **one bed per person**, and many want **one room per person** on longer jobs. Twin rooms and singles are worth more to a crew than one big double.

## Parking that fits your vans

Ask how many vehicles fit off the street, and give the host your van heights and lengths. Street parking may be fine, but check for permit zones. The Highway Code says you must not park on a road at night facing against the direction of traffic unless you are in a recognised parking space, and vehicles over 7.5 tonnes laden must not be parked on a verge or pavement without police permission.

## Laundry

A **washing machine** is close to essential for a stay of a week or more. A dryer, or at least somewhere to hang wet gear, makes a big difference in winter. See [laundry on a work stay](/guides/laundry-for-workers-liverpool).

## A kitchen the crew can actually use

A full kitchen, a big fridge and enough pans and plates for everyone. Cooking at the house is one of the biggest savings over eating out every night. Our [self-catering guide for crews](/guides/self-catering-for-work-crews) has meal ideas.

## Wi-Fi and somewhere to sit

For timesheets, drawings, video calls and evenings off. If someone needs to work at a desk, check for a workspace.

## Check-in that suits shift work

**Self check-in** helps when the crew arrives late, or in shifts. Ask what time check-in opens and how keys work.

## Paperwork

Decide what finance needs: a receipt with the company name, or a full VAT invoice. On most Book Homes listings you can tick a box at checkout for a **company receipt (PDF)**. Only a VAT-registered host can charge VAT and give a VAT invoice. HMRC's VAT Notice 709/3 explains how VAT works for hotels and holiday accommodation. See our [invoices and VAT guide](/guides/invoice-and-vat-for-business-stays).

## Long-stay terms

Ask about weekly or monthly discounts, a mid-stay clean, fresh linen and towels, and what happens if the job runs over and you need to extend.

## House rules

A crew in a residential street needs to be a good neighbour: quiet late at night, considerate parking and bins out on the right day. No parties.
