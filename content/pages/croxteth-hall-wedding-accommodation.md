If you're going to a wedding at Croxteth Hall, the easiest place to stay is somewhere with parking and enough room for the family, so you aren't hunting for a taxi from the centre late at night. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Liverpool, sleeping up to 7, with free parking for two cars and two bathrooms for getting ready. It's ideal for the bride's or groom's family, or a group of guests travelling together.

This page covers the venue, the practical timings of a wedding day there and how to plan your stay.

## About Croxteth Hall as a wedding venue

Croxteth Hall is the former ancestral home of the Earls of Sefton, set in a large country park in the Croxteth and West Derby area. The address is **Muirhead Avenue East, Liverpool, L11 1EH**.

- **Licensed for ceremonies:** Liverpool City Council lists Croxteth Hall as an approved venue for marriages and civil partnerships.
- **Rooms:** the hall describes rooms with Tudor, Georgian and Queen Anne features, and ceremonies can be held in a choice of them.
- **Receptions:** it offers a wedding breakfast overlooking the grounds and drinks receptions outside, with tailor-made packages.
- **Grounds:** the Victorian walled garden is often used for photographs.

The hall's own wedding page notes that **room hire is currently limited because of maintenance**, and some rooms may not be ready for events. If you're the couple, check which rooms are available for your date. If you're a guest, the couple will have this in hand, but it's worth knowing that the layout might differ from older photos online.

### For couples: the legal bit

Booking the venue is only part of it. Liverpool City Council says couples marrying at an approved venue still need to give notice and book and pay for a registrar. Government guidance says notice must be given **at least 29 days before the ceremony**, and the ceremony must take place within 12 months of giving notice.

## Planning a wedding day at Croxteth

### Morning: getting ready

For the couple's family, the morning is the busiest part of the day. A whole house helps:

- **Bathroom rota.** With 2 bathrooms, including an en-suite, the bride or groom can have one to themselves.
- **Hair and make-up.** Choose the room with the best light, and let your stylist know there's parking outside.
- **Breakfast.** A big kitchen means everyone eats properly before a long day.
- **Outfits.** Hang everything up the night before so nothing creases.

Tell your hair and make-up artist the arrival time and confirm they're happy to travel to the house.

### Getting to the venue

Croxteth Hall is in north Liverpool, and journey times depend on traffic. Always:

- Check a live route the day before, at the same time of day
- Book wedding cars and taxis well in advance
- Leave extra time on a Saturday, when local roads can be busy

The country park is large, so follow the signs to the hall and the car park rather than relying on the postcode alone.

### Evening: getting back

This is where many guests get caught out. Taxis are hard to find late on a Saturday night from a country park. Pre-book a taxi or minibus for the end of the evening, and agree a time as a group. Having somewhere nearby to go back to makes a big difference.

## Things to do if you're staying for the weekend

Many wedding guests come from out of town and stay two or three nights. While you're here:

- **Croxteth Country Park** has woodland walks, a working farm and the walled garden. See our [Croxteth Hall visitor guide](/guides/croxteth-hall-visitor-guide).
- **West Derby village** has cafes and pubs for a lazy lunch the day after. See the [West Derby village guide](/guides/west-derby-village-guide).
- **Liverpool city centre** is about 15 minutes by train, for the waterfront, museums and shops.

## Hotel or house for wedding guests?

A hotel works if you're a couple or travelling alone. A house is often better when:

- You're the couple's immediate family and want to get ready together
- Several relatives are travelling from the same place
- You have children who need their own space and an early night
- You want to cook breakfast the morning after, rather than queue for a hotel buffet
