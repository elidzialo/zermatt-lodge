If your Liverpool home is having a major renovation, such as a new kitchen, a rewire, an extension or structural work, moving into a furnished house nearby for the duration can save your sanity. It lets a family live normally while the builders get on with it.

This guide covers how to decide whether to move out, how to time the stay, and how to plan for the delays that almost every building job brings.

## Should you move out?

Many families try to live through a renovation and regret it. Moving out usually makes sense if the work involves:

- **No kitchen** for more than a week or two
- **No bathroom**, or only one bathroom for a large family
- **No heating or hot water**, especially in winter
- **Heavy dust**, such as knocking through walls, sanding floors or replastering
- **Structural work** where the builder would rather you weren't on site
- **Young children, older relatives or anyone with breathing problems** in the household

If it's a single room, a repaint or a small bathroom, staying put is usually fine.

## Timing your stay

### Start from the builder's programme

Ask your builder for a written schedule showing when each trade will be on site, and when the "no kitchen" and "no bathroom" periods fall. You don't have to move out for the whole project. Some families only leave for the messiest few weeks.

### Allow for slippage

Building projects overrun. The usual reasons are:

- **Weather.** Liverpool gets plenty of wet and windy weather, which can delay roofing, groundwork and external work. Our [Liverpool weather by month guide](/guides/liverpool-weather-by-month) shows what to expect in each season
- **Supply delays** on windows, kitchens and specialist items
- **Surprises** once walls, floors or ceilings are opened up
- **Sign-offs and inspections** that need to happen before the next stage

Practical ways to handle this:

1. Book a little longer than the estimate
2. Ask the host whether you can extend if needed, and how much notice they need
3. Check the cancellation policy. Zermatt Lodge uses a Moderate policy: a full refund up to 5 days before check-in, 50% after that, which helps if the builder's start date moves

### Party wall notices

If your project involves a shared wall or excavating near a neighbour's foundations, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 may apply. According to RICS:

- Notice must be given **one or two months** before starting, depending on the type of work
- If the neighbour hasn't agreed in writing within **14 days**, the surveyor process begins
- **Work can't start** until there's written agreement or a party wall award

This can push your start date back, so don't lock in accommodation until the party wall position is clear.

## Practical planning

### Storage

Your furniture and belongings need to go somewhere while the work happens. Options include:

- One room at home that the builders won't touch, sealed with dust sheets
- A self-storage unit for the duration
- Family or friends with a spare garage

A furnished rental means you only need to bring clothes, essentials and the things the children can't live without.

### Post and deliveries

Set up a Royal Mail redirection if you'll be away for weeks. Keep building deliveries going to the work site, not the rental.

### School and work routines

Choose a base that keeps school runs and commutes manageable. If you live in north Liverpool, Sefton or Knowsley, staying nearby means very little changes. From Fazakerley, trains reach Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes for anyone working in town.

### Health

If you're staying away from your usual area for a while, you can still see your own GP. If you need to, NHS guidance says you can register as a temporary patient elsewhere for up to 3 months.

## Visiting the site

Plan regular site visits with your builder, at your home rather than at the rental. Take photos at each stage, keep a list of questions and agree any changes in writing. Living a short drive away makes it easy to drop in after school or work.
