The best things to do in Liverpool with a group mix the big sights with activities you can share: a Mersey ferry cruise, the waterfront and its free museums, a stadium tour, a food hall where everyone picks their own dinner, and a games night. Below is a two-day plan for a group of five to seven, based in north Liverpool with a train into town. Swap pieces in and out to suit your group.

## Day 1: The waterfront and the city

### Morning: Pier Head and a Mersey ferry

Take the Merseyrail Northern line from Fazakerley to **Liverpool Central** (about 15 minutes), then walk down to the **Pier Head**. Start with the **Three Graces**: the Royal Liver Building, the Cunard Building and the Port of Liverpool Building.

Then hop on the **Mersey Ferries River Explorer cruise**. It takes about 50 minutes from the Pier Head, calls at Seacombe and Woodside on the Wirral, and has commentary throughout. It's a great way for a group to see the skyline together and get your bearings. See our [Mersey ferry cruise guide](/guides/mersey-ferry-cruise-guide).

### Late morning: Museum of Liverpool

Right by the ferry terminal, the **Museum of Liverpool** is free and has something for every age, from the city's music and football to its people and history. Allow an hour or two.

### Lunch: Royal Albert Dock

Walk south to the **Royal Albert Dock** for lunch. Groups can usually find space at one of the many places around the dock, but book ahead at weekends.

### Afternoon: choose your own adventure

Split up for a couple of hours, then meet up again:

- **Music fans:** The Beatles Story at the Albert Dock, then Mathew Street and the Cavern Club
- **History fans:** the Merseyside Maritime Museum and International Slavery Museum, in the same building on the dock
- **Shoppers:** Liverpool ONE, a short walk from the dock

### Evening: food hall and games

Food halls work brilliantly for groups, because everyone chooses their own food and you still eat together. The **Baltic Triangle**, just south of the centre, is known for them. See our [Baltic Market guide](/guides/baltic-market-guide).

Then try something competitive: **crazy golf, darts, shuffleboard or bowling**. Liverpool has plenty of competitive socialising venues. Book a slot for your whole group. See [competitive socialising in Liverpool](/guides/competitive-socialising-liverpool).

Take the train back to Fazakerley, or a taxi if it's late.

## Day 2: Football, views or the coast

### Option A: Stadium tour

Football fans can tour **Anfield** or Everton's new stadium at **Bramley-Moore Dock**. From Fazakerley, both are easy to reach via **Sandhills** station. Book tours ahead, especially around match days. See our [Anfield Stadium Tour guide](/guides/anfield-stadium-tour-guide).

### Option B: Cathedrals and views

Climb the **Liverpool Cathedral** tower for views across the city and river. Then walk along Hope Street to the **Metropolitan Cathedral**. Stop for lunch on **Bold Street**, Liverpool's best-known street for independent food.

### Option C: Crosby Beach

Drive to **Crosby Beach** via Switch Island to see Antony Gormley's **Another Place**: 100 cast-iron figures along more than 3km of beach. Sefton Council advises staying near the promenade and not walking out to the furthest statues, where the sand is very soft and muddy. Check tide times before you go.

### Afternoon: back to base

After two busy days, most groups love a relaxed afternoon: pool in the games room, a board game, a walk, and a big home-cooked dinner around the dining table.

## Ideas by type of group

- **Family groups with children:** the Museum of Liverpool and World Museum (both free), the ferry, Croxteth Hall, Knowsley Safari
- **Football fans:** a stadium tour, Stanley Park between the two old grounds, the Museum of Liverpool's football displays
- **Music fans:** the Beatles Story, Mathew Street and the Cavern Club, then a gig at one of the city's venues
- **Foodies:** Bold Street, the Baltic Triangle food halls and a home-cooked feast on the last night
- **Mixed ages:** the ferry and waterfront work for everyone, then split up for the afternoon and meet for dinner

## Tips for planning group activities

1. **Book anything time-slotted** (tours, escape rooms, games venues, restaurants) as soon as you know numbers.
2. **Check group sizes.** Some activities have a maximum per booking, so you might need two slots side by side.
3. **Build in free time.** Not everyone wants to do everything.
4. **Plan the weather back-up.** See [rainy day things to do in Liverpool](/guides/rainy-day-liverpool).
5. **Think about transport.** Use the train for the city centre and cars for the coast.
6. **Set a group kitty** for shared costs. See [how to split costs fairly](/guides/how-to-split-costs-group-trip).
7. **Decide in advance whether it's a big-night-out trip or a relaxed one**, and choose accommodation to match.
