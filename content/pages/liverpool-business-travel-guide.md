Business travel to Liverpool is straightforward: the main office district, the conference venues on the waterfront and the Knowledge Quarter are all within a compact city centre, linked by Merseyrail trains you can pay for with a contactless card. The main decisions are where to base yourself and how to get between sites if you're also visiting business parks on the outskirts. Solo travellers often stay in town; teams and crews often prefer a house with parking. ## Where business happens in Liverpool

### The Commercial District

Liverpool's main office district is in the north-west of the city centre, around **Old Hall Street**, Tithebarn Street and the waterfront. It's home to insurers, law firms, public sector offices and professional services. Old Hall Street buildings include **New Hall Place**, used by the Home Office and RSA, and the **Cotton Exchange**.

- **Nearest station: Moorfields**, which has an entrance on Old Hall Street
- **Coming up:** **Pall Mall**, described by the city as Liverpool's first major Grade A office scheme in a decade

### The Knowledge Quarter and Paddington Village

**KQ Liverpool** is the innovation district around the universities and hospitals to the east of the centre. At its eastern gateway is **Paddington Village**, a 30-acre, £1 billion development:

- **The Spine**, a 14-storey Grade A office building where the **Royal College of Physicians** occupies seven floors
- A **221-bed Novotel** and a **1,200-space multi-storey car park**
- The University of Liverpool International College and a community diagnostic centre
- **HEMISPHERE**, a planned office and laboratory building

If your work is in life sciences, health or the universities, this is likely where you'll be.

### The waterfront and conference campus

The convention and exhibition halls on Kings Dock are the city's main venue for conferences and trade shows. See our guides for [conference delegates](/guides/acc-liverpool-conference-accommodation) and [exhibitors](/guides/exhibition-centre-liverpool-exhibitor-stay).

### The north docks

The north docks are the city's biggest regeneration area. The proposed **North Docks Mayoral Development Corporation** covers about 174 hectares, with potential for more than 17,500 homes and 5 million sq ft of commercial space over 15 years, according to Liverpool Express. Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium sits at Liverpool Waters here.

## Getting around

### Merseyrail

Merseyrail is the quickest way around the city centre and out to the suburbs. The underground stations in the centre are **Moorfields**, **Liverpool Central**, **Lime Street** (Wirral line) and **James Street**.

**Tap and Go:** the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority's scheme lets you pay with a **contactless card or digital wallet**, with **daily and weekly fare capping**. It was piloted on the Headbolt Lane line from December 2025, with full service later in 2026. For business travellers, that means no paper tickets to keep for expenses. Download statements from your card provider.

### Driving

Driving into the centre is fine outside peak hours, but parking costs add up over a week. If you're visiting business parks or several sites in a day, a car is often essential. A base near the **M57 and M58** saves time compared with crossing the city centre.

### Lime Street for mainline trains

**Lime Street** is the mainline station for trains from London, Manchester and Birmingham. Liverpool Central is a short walk away for connections.

Since **1 June 2025**, Liverpool's Accommodation BID has applied a **£2 a night** visitor charge at larger hotels and serviced accommodation blocks in the city centre. It's minor for one night, more noticeable for a team over a week.

From Fazakerley, Trainline lists direct trains to Liverpool Central in **about 15 minutes**, calling at **Moorfields** for the Commercial District.

## Expenses and tax: the basics

If your employer is paying, or you're claiming expenses, the rules on **temporary workplaces** matter. The Low Incomes Tax Reform Group (LITRG) explains:

- A **temporary workplace** is one you attend for a limited time or irregularly, for example being sent to work at another site for a year
- Travel between home and a temporary workplace may be allowable; travel to a permanent workplace isn't
- Travel expenses can include **subsistence and accommodation** where there's an overnight stay
- A workplace stops being temporary if you work there continuously for **more than 24 months**, and HMRC treats your duties as significant there if you spend **40% or more** of your time at that place

This is general information, not tax advice. Check HMRC's guidance or ask your finance team about your situation.

### Practical expenses tips

1. **Ask for an invoice** in your company's name when you book.
2. **Keep contactless statements** for train journeys.
3. **Note mileage** if you're driving your own car between sites.
4. **Check your company's policy** on house rentals vs hotels. Many now accept both.

## Tips for a smooth work trip

- **Book early for big event weeks.** The Grand National in April, major concerts and big conferences push up prices across the city
- **Check match days.** Liverpool and Everton home games affect traffic and trains in north Liverpool
- **Plan meetings around Moorfields or Central.** Both are easy to reach by train
- **Bring layers and an umbrella.** The waterfront is windy
