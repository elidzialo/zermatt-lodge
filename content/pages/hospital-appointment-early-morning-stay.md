If your hospital letter says to arrive at 7am, staying in Liverpool the night before is usually the calmest option, especially if you'd otherwise be driving in from North Wales, Lancashire or further afield. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, near Aintree University Hospital and about 15 minutes by train from the city centre, where you and a companion can check in at 3pm, have a quiet evening and get a proper night's sleep.

This guide walks through the evening before and the morning of an early admission, whichever Liverpool hospital you're heading to.

## Why the night before matters

Early admissions are scheduled tightly. Arriving late can mean your slot goes to someone else, and a long, stressful drive is not ideal preparation for surgery or a procedure. Staying nearby helps you:

- Avoid rush-hour traffic on the M62, M57 and M58
- Follow fasting instructions without a 4am breakfast in the car
- Arrive rested, with time to find the right entrance
- Bring a companion who can collect you later

## Which hospital, and how far?

Liverpool's main hospitals are spread across the city. North Liverpool is a good base for some and a reasonable drive or train ride for others.

If you're having treatment at Aintree or The Walton Centre, staying in north Liverpool means a very short trip in the morning. For the Royal or Clatterbridge, it's still easier than a long motorway drive, but leave extra time.

## The afternoon before

### Check in at 3pm

Check-in at Zermatt Lodge is from 3pm. That leaves time to unpack, have an early dinner and settle in.

### Rehearse the route

This is the best tip we can give. In the late afternoon:

1. Drive or walk the route to the hospital entrance you've been given.
2. Find the car park and the pay machines. Aintree's visitor car park uses number plate recognition and you pay before leaving the site.
3. Note the drop-off point. Aintree has drop-off and pick-up points outside all its main entrances.
4. Check how long it took, then add a margin for morning traffic.

The hospitals warn that roads around their sites get congested at peak times. A dry run takes the guesswork out of the morning.

### Plan your evening meal around your fasting instructions

Your hospital will tell you when to stop eating and drinking. Follow its instructions exactly, because they can vary by procedure and by patient.

Many UK hospitals now use **SipTilSend**, published by the Centre for Perioperative Care. Under that approach:

- **No solid food for at least 6 hours** before surgery (this includes sweets and chewing gum)
- **Clear fluids freely** until 2 hours before
- **Small sips of clear fluid** (up to 170ml an hour) after that, until you're called to theatre
- Clear fluids include water, black tea or coffee, and squash or juice without bits

Some patients can't use SipTilSend for medical reasons. If your letter says "nil by mouth from midnight", do that. Having a kitchen means you can eat a sensible meal at the right time instead of whatever's open late.

### Pack your hospital bag

- Appointment letter and any pre-op paperwork
- List of your current medicines
- Phone and charger
- Loose, comfortable clothes for going home
- Slippers and a dressing gown if you might be admitted overnight
- Something to read while you wait

## The morning of your appointment

### If you're driving

Leave earlier than your rehearsal suggested. Have your registration number to hand for the car park. If a companion is driving you, they can drop you at the entrance and park afterwards.

### If you're taking the train

Trainline shows the first weekday train from Fazakerley to Liverpool Central at **04:46**, with the journey taking about 15 minutes. That's useful if your companion needs to get into the city for the Royal or Clatterbridge without a car. Check the timetable for your exact date, as early services differ at weekends.

### Taxis

Book a taxi the night before for an early pick-up. Don't rely on finding one at short notice before 7am.

## The companion's role

After many procedures, you won't be allowed to drive or go home alone. Your companion will:

- Take you to the hospital and know where to collect you
- Keep their phone on for a call from the ward
- Drive you back, and stay with you if the hospital asks for someone to be with you overnight

A whole house means your companion has their own bedroom and doesn't have to wait in a car park all day. They can go back, rest and return when called.

## After discharge

If you're discharged the same day but feel too tired for a long journey home, a second night can make sense. Bookings are by the night, so ask when you book whether you can extend.
