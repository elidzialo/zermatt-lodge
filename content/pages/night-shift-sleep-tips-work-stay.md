Night work is hard enough at home. Away from home, in a strange bed with daylight leaking round the curtains and a day shift crew clattering in the kitchen, it's harder. The good news is that most of what helps is simple. This guide uses the **HSE's hints and tips for shift workers**, plus practical points for anyone sharing a work house.

## Why it matters

The HSE says more than **3.5 million people** work shifts in the UK. It notes that accident and injury rates are higher on night shifts, after a run of consecutive shifts and on long shifts, and that night workers' daytime sleep tends to be **lighter, shorter and more easily disrupted**. Fatigue has been implicated in **20% of accidents on major roads**.

## Setting up the bedroom

The HSE recommends:

- **Darkness:** heavy curtains, blackout blinds or an eye mask
- **Quiet:** earplugs, white noise or background music to mask outside noise
- **Cool:** a comfortable temperature, as cool conditions improve sleep
- **Phone off:** disconnect it or turn the ringer down
- **Bed for sleep only:** not for TV, eating or work

When you book, ask the host whether bedrooms have **blackout blinds or curtains**. If not, pack an eye mask and some travel blackout film.

## Your sleep routine

From the HSE's tips:

1. **Aim for 7 to 8 hours a day.** If you can't, resting still helps.
2. **Pick a schedule and stick to it,** either going to bed soon after you get in or sleeping before your next shift.
3. **Take a short sleep before your first night shift.**
4. **After your last night,** have a short nap and go to bed earlier that night.
5. **Wind down first:** a short walk, a book, music or a warm bath.
6. **Avoid caffeine and energy drinks** for a few hours before bed, and **avoid alcohol** as a sleep aid.
7. **Don't go to bed hungry,** but avoid fatty, spicy or heavy meals.

## Food on nights

The HSE suggests regular light meals and snacks rather than one big meal, with easy-to-digest food such as pasta, rice, bread, salad, fruit, vegetables and milk products. A house kitchen makes this easy: batch-cook before the first night and take a packed meal to site.

## Getting to and from site

The HSE's travel tips:

- Consider **public transport or a taxi** instead of driving
- **Share the driving** if you can
- Do some **brief exercise** before you set off
- If you feel sleepy, **stop for a short nap** if it's safe

The shorter the drive between site and bed, the better. Book a base close to the job.

## Sharing a house with the day shift

Mixed day and night crews can share a house well with some ground rules:

- **Give night workers the quietest bedrooms,** away from the kitchen and front door
- **Agree quiet hours** for the daytime sleepers
- **Keep doors closed** and phones on silent in shared areas
- **Use the washer and dryer** at agreed times

## For employers and crew managers

HSE good practice for shift design includes capping shifts at **12 hours**, or **8 hours** for night or safety-critical work, keeping night shifts to **2 or 3 in a row** and allowing **two nights of uninterrupted sleep** when switching between days and nights. The HSE also says that complying with the Working Time Regulations alone is not enough to manage fatigue.
