"Near the job" is the first thing builders search for, and it is worth getting right. A good base saves time every single day of the job. A bad one costs it.

## Measure by drive time, not distance

Check the route at the time the crew actually leaves, and at the time you come back. A house five miles away on the wrong side of a busy junction can take longer than one ten miles away beside the motorway. Run the route in a maps app for your real start time on a weekday.

## Think about the roads you will use every day

- In **Liverpool**, the M62 comes in from Manchester and the east, and the M57 and M58 serve the north of the city.
- In **Greater Manchester**, the M60 ring road circles the city, with the M62, M61 and M56 joining it.

A base near the motorway you use most is often the best choice. See our [contractor guides for Liverpool](/guides/contractor-accommodation-liverpool) and [Manchester](/guides/contractor-accommodation-manchester).

## Use the tram or train for city-centre sites

If the site is in a city centre with little parking, a house near a Metrolink stop or train station lets the crew leave the vans at the house. In Manchester, for example, the Metrolink Bury line runs through Prestwich to the city centre. See [contractor accommodation in Prestwich](/guides/contractor-accommodation-prestwich).

## Make sure the house works for builders

- **A bed each.** Ask for the bed list.
- **Free parking** that fits your vans. Give the host heights and lengths. See [what to look for in contractor accommodation](/guides/what-to-look-for-in-contractor-accommodation).
- **A washing machine** for workwear.
- **A kitchen** and a big fridge.
- **Wi-Fi.**
- **Self check-in** for late arrivals.

## Mind the neighbours

Most homes are on residential streets. Leave quietly in the morning, do not block drives, and follow the house rules. The Highway Code also says you must not park on a road at night facing against the traffic unless you are in a recognised parking space.
