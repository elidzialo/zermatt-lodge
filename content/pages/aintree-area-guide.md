Aintree is a village in north Liverpool, best known for the Grand National, but it also has a canal towpath, a historic motor racing circuit, a large shopping park with free parking, and fast trains into the city. Aintree is about 6 miles from Liverpool city centre, with the M57 and M58 close by.

This guide covers what Aintree is like, what there is to do beyond race week, and how to get around.

## Where Aintree is

Aintree is a village and civil parish in the **Metropolitan Borough of Sefton**, historically part of Lancashire. It sits on the **A59** between Walton and Maghull, about **6 miles (10 km) north-east of Liverpool city centre**. The 2011 census counted 6,689 residents.

People use "Aintree" loosely. Locals might mean the village, the racecourse, or the wider district that runs into Fazakerley, Orrell Park and Walton. You'll also see "Aintree" on hospital signs, but **Aintree University Hospital is actually in Fazakerley**, next to Fazakerley station.

### Roads

Aintree is close to **Switch Island**, the junction between Aintree and Maghull where the **M57, M58, A59 and A5036** meet. That makes it a handy base if you're driving:

- The **M57** heads south towards the M62 and Knowsley
- The **M58** heads north-east towards Skelmersdale and the M6
- The **A5036** runs west to Bootle, Crosby and the docks
- The **A59** runs south into the city centre

Our guide to [accommodation near the M57 and M58](/guides/accommodation-near-m57-m58-liverpool) has more on driving routes.

### Trains

Aintree has two Merseyrail stations on the **Northern line (Ormskirk branch)**:

- **Aintree station** serves the racecourse
- **Old Roan station** is closer to the village and the shopping park

Both run direct to **Liverpool Central** via Sandhills and Moorfields. Fazakerley is on the **Kirkby branch** of the same line. The two branches split north of the city, so travelling between Fazakerley and Aintree by train means changing at a shared station nearer town (check the Merseyrail journey planner for the best change). By road it's a short drive.

## Aintree's Formula One history

Few visitors realise Aintree was once a Grand Prix venue. In the 1950s and 1960s a three-mile circuit ran around the racecourse, using its grandstands. It hosted the **British Grand Prix five times: 1955, 1957, 1959, 1961 and 1962**. **Stirling Moss** won his first British Grand Prix there in 1955, driving a Mercedes.

The full circuit closed in 1964, but a **1.535-mile club circuit** is still in use:

- **Liverpool Motor Club** runs car sprints (typically May, June and September) and track days (typically April and August)
- **Aintree Motor Cycle Racing Club** has run motorcycle race meetings there since 1982, around six a year

If you're into motorsport, check the clubs' calendars for 2027 dates before you book.

## The Leeds and Liverpool Canal

The **Leeds and Liverpool Canal** runs right through Aintree. The towpath is flat and easy, good for:

- A morning walk or run if you're in the area for work
- A gentle stroll with grandparents or a buggy
- A cycle ride, with National Cycle Route 62 and the Liverpool Loop Line close by

It's a quieter side of Aintree that race-day visitors rarely see.

## Aintree Shopping Park

**Aintree Shopping Park**, on Ormskirk Road, is a big out-of-town retail park managed by The Crown Estate. It's useful if you're self-catering or forgot something at home. Shops include:

- **M&S with a Foodhall**, Boots, Next and Pets at Home
- **B&Q**, Halfords and Hobbycraft
- Furniture stores including DFS and Furniture Village
- Food and drink from Greggs, Costa, Tim Hortons, McDonald's and Nando's

Practical details from The Crown Estate:

- **Parking is free all day**, with Blue Badge spaces and EV charging bays near Next
- **Core opening hours** are 7am to 11pm Monday to Friday, 9am to 8pm Saturday and 9am to 11pm Sunday (individual stores vary)
- It's about a **10-minute walk from Old Roan station**
- Buses **300, 310 and 345** stop within 150 metres
- By car it's off **M57 junction 7** or **M58 junction 0**, via the A59

## Who stays in the Aintree area

- **Racegoers** for the Grand National and other race meetings
- **Hospital visitors** for Aintree University Hospital and The Walton Centre in Fazakerley. See our [Aintree hospital accommodation guide](/guides/accommodation-near-aintree-university-hospital)
- **Contractors and work crews** who need quick motorway access
- **Football fans** who want a base on the north side of the city, a short train ride from Anfield and Everton's stadium
- **Families** who want a quieter base with parking and space

### Aintree vs the city centre

| | Aintree and Fazakerley | City centre |
|---|---|---|
| Parking | Easier, often free | Paid car parks |
| Noise at night | Quieter residential streets | Busier, especially weekends |
| Getting to the waterfront | Train, about 15-20 minutes | Walk |
| Grand National | Close by | Train out |
| Motorways | Minutes away | Through city traffic |

The trade-off is simple: you give up walking to bars and the waterfront, and in return get space, parking and a quieter night's sleep.

## Tips for visiting Aintree

1. **Check race-day road closures** if you're driving anywhere near the racecourse during a meeting.
2. **Use Old Roan** for the shopping park and village, and **Aintree** for the racecourse.
3. **Book early for April.** The Grand National festival fills accommodation across the city.

4. **Pack for the weather.** The canal towpath and racecourse are exposed in wind and rain.
