Maghull, Merseyside: A Practical Area Guide

Maghull guide for visitors, workers and relocators: the canal, Frank Hornby's town, two Merseyrail stations, Switch Island for the M57 and M58, and nearby stays.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Maghull is a canal town in Sefton, north of Liverpool and west of Kirkby, with about 20,000 people (20,444 at the 2011 Census). It has two Merseyrail Northern line stations, Maghull and Maghull North (opened June 2018), and sits next to Switch Island, where the M57 and M58 begin. It's a good base for Aintree, Ormskirk and the Sefton coast, and close to north Liverpool.

Maghull is a canal town on Liverpool's northern edge, in the borough of Sefton. It's mostly residential, with about 20,000 people, two Merseyrail stations and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal running through the old centre. For visitors, its big advantage is location: it sits right by Switch Island, where the M57 and M58 begin, so Aintree, Ormskirk, the Sefton coast and the motorway network are all close.

Key facts

  • Where: a town and civil parish in the Metropolitan Borough of Sefton, Merseyside, north of Liverpool and west of Kirkby
  • Population: 20,444 at the 2011 Census
  • History: recorded as "Magele" in the Domesday Survey of 1086
  • Canal: the Leeds and Liverpool Canal reached Maghull in 1774
  • Railway: arrived in 1849, on the Liverpool, Ormskirk and Preston Railway
  • Main road: Northway (the A59), built in 1933 and dualled in the early 1960s, runs through the town

Things to see

The canal

The Leeds and Liverpool Canal runs through the centre of old Maghull. The towpath is a flat walk, good for a morning stroll or a run, and the Red Lion pub was originally built to serve canal trade.

St Andrew's Old Church

The small chapel in the churchyard of the Victorian St Andrew's is described as the oldest church building in Merseyside still in regular use for worship.

Frank Hornby's town

Frank Hornby, founder of Meccano, Dinky Toys and Hornby Railways, lived in Maghull, first at "The Hollies" and later at "Quarry Brook". He's buried in St Andrew's churchyard. The Hollies was the first building outside London to get a blue plaque.

Maghull Town Hall

The town hall had an £8.2 million extension in 2009, adding a library and sports facilities.

Getting around

By train

Maghull has two stations on Merseyrail's Northern line:

  • Maghull (opened 1849)
  • Maghull North (opened 18 June 2018) at the north end of the town

Trains run south through Aintree towards Liverpool city centre and north to Ormskirk. Check Merseyrail for current times.

By road

The M57 and M58 both start at Switch Island, between Aintree and Maghull. The M58 runs east towards Skelmersdale and the M6, and the M57 runs south around the east of Liverpool to the M62. The A59 Northway runs through Maghull towards Ormskirk to the north and Aintree and Liverpool to the south. See our M57 and M58 accommodation guide.

Who visits Maghull

  • Families visiting Ashworth Hospital, a high-security hospital that is part of Mersey Care's Maghull Health Park. See our Ashworth visitor guide.
  • Race-goers heading to nearby Aintree Racecourse
  • Workers on sites near Switch Island, the M58 corridor and Kirkby
  • Relocators looking at Sefton's commuter towns. See our house-hunting guide.

Where to stay

Maghull has few large holiday houses. If you need a whole house for a family, group or crew, look just south at Aintree and Fazakerley, on the Liverpool side of Switch Island. Both have Merseyrail stations and quick access to the same motorways.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where is Maghull?

Maghull is a town and civil parish in the Metropolitan Borough of Sefton, Merseyside, north of Liverpool and west of Kirkby. Switch Island, where the M57 and M58 start, lies between Aintree and Maghull.

Does Maghull have a train station?

It has two on Merseyrail's Northern line: Maghull, which opened in 1849, and Maghull North, which opened on 18 June 2018 to serve the north end of the town.

What is Maghull known for?

The Leeds and Liverpool Canal, which reached the town in 1774; St Andrew's Old Church, described as the oldest church building in Merseyside still in regular use; and Frank Hornby, the inventor of Meccano, who lived and is buried in Maghull.

Is there anywhere to stay in Maghull for a group?

There are few large holiday houses in Maghull itself. Nearby Fazakerley, on the other side of Switch Island and Aintree, has whole-house options such as Zermatt Lodge, which sleeps 7 with free parking for 2 cars.
