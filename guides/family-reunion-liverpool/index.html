Planning a Family Reunion in Liverpool

Bringing the family together in Liverpool? How to choose dates, a house that sleeps everyone, meals, free days out for all ages and the house rules to agree.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

For a family reunion in Liverpool, book a whole house for two or three nights so everyone has a shared kitchen and dining table, then plan one or two easy outings that suit all ages, such as the free national museums, Liverpool Cathedral or a 50-minute Mersey Ferry cruise. Avoid big event weekends like the Grand National (8 to 10 April 2027) unless you book very early.

Getting the whole family in one place doesn't happen often. Whether it's cousins flying in from abroad, a big birthday or simply time together, Liverpool works well: it's easy to reach, has plenty to do for every age, and many of the best things are free.

We checked the details below on 23 November 2026.

1. Pick the right dates

  • Avoid the busiest weekends. The Grand National Festival runs 8 to 10 April 2027, and big match days at Anfield and Hill Dickinson Stadium also push prices up
  • Think about school holidays. Families with school-age children can only travel in holidays, which are also the busiest times
  • Two or three nights is usually right. One night is rushed, and longer is hard to fit around everyone's work
  • Check our monthly guides for December, January and February

2. Choose a house, not hotel rooms

A reunion is about being together. In a hotel, everyone disappears to separate rooms. In a whole house, you share breakfast, sit round one table at dinner and stay up talking.

Things to check:

  1. Beds: how many real beds, and who needs their own room (grandparents, young families)
  2. Bathrooms: two bathrooms make a big difference for 6 or 7 people
  3. Stairs: check bedroom layout if anyone has mobility needs. See our accessible group stay questions
  4. Parking: family groups often arrive in several cars
  5. Kitchen and dining table: big enough for everyone to eat together

If the family is bigger than one house sleeps, book two homes near each other. Our group booking checklist has more.

3. Plan meals

  • One big home-cooked dinner is often the highlight. Our meal plan for 7 makes it easy
  • Get a supermarket delivery on arrival day. See our grocery delivery guide
  • Book one meal out for the big night, and book early on weekends

4. Outings for every age

Keep it simple: one outing a day, then home.

  • The free national museums: World Museum, the Walker Art Gallery and the Museum of Liverpool. See our museums guide
  • Liverpool Cathedral: free to visit, with a paid Tower Experience
  • The Mersey Ferry: a 50-minute River Explorer Cruise with commentary, good for grandparents and children alike. See our ferry guide
  • A show: from November to January there are pantos and Christmas shows across the city

More ideas are in our free things to do guide.

5. Agree house rules up front

Share the host's rules with everyone before you arrive: check-in time, quiet hours, parking and who's sleeping where. It saves arguments on the day.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What's the best accommodation for a family reunion?

A whole house with a big kitchen and dining table, so everyone can eat together. If the family is bigger than one house sleeps, book two homes close together.

What can all ages do together in Liverpool?

The national museums are free, Liverpool Cathedral is free to visit, and the Mersey Ferry River Explorer Cruise takes about 50 minutes with commentary.

Which weekends should we avoid?

Big event weekends fill up fast and cost more, especially the Grand National Festival (8 to 10 April 2027) and big match days. Check our events calendar guides before you pick a date.

Can we hold a party at the house?

No. Zermatt Lodge has a strict no parties or events rule. For a big celebration, book a restaurant or a hired venue, and use the house for the family who are staying.

7, in 4 bedrooms: a king master with en-suite, a second king, a queen and a single. There are 2 bathrooms.
