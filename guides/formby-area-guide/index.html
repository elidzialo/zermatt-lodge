Formby Area Guide: Pinewoods, Dunes and Red Squirrels

Planning a day at Formby? The National Trust pinewoods and beach, red squirrels, walking trails, car parks that fill fast, trains and tips from Liverpool.

Where Formby is

Formby is in the borough of Sefton, on the coast between Crosby and Southport. The National Trust looks after a large stretch of pinewoods, dunes and beach here, often just called "Formby" by locals.

The town itself has a village centre with shops and cafes, but most visitors come for the coast.

What makes Formby special

The pinewoods and red squirrels

The National Trust says Formby's woodlands provide important habitat for a wide variety of wildlife, including rare red squirrels. Seeing one isn't guaranteed, but your chances are better if you:

  • Walk quietly through the woodland, away from the busiest paths
  • Look up into the pine trees rather than at the ground
  • Visit on a quieter day, outside summer weekends

The Trust carries out conservation work in the woodlands in autumn and winter, so you may see areas being managed.

The dunes and beach

Behind the beach are high sand dunes, and in front is a wide, flat beach facing the Irish Sea. It's a great place for a long walk, kite flying or letting children run off energy.

Things to know from the National Trust:

  • No fires or barbecues anywhere along the Sefton Coast
  • No shade on the beach, so bring hats and sun cream in summer
  • Bins are only in the car parks, so bring a bag and take your litter home
  • Facilities are basic
  • Child wristbands are available from the welcome team, so you can add a phone number in case a child wanders off
  • Dogs are welcome; the Trust rates Formby as a "three pawprint" place

Walking trails

The National Trust lists three waymarked trails:

Trail Length
Lost Resort Trail 1.25 miles (2 km)
Devil's Hole Trail 1.5 miles (2.4 km)
Formby Asparagus Trail 1.75 miles (2.8 km)

Some woodland paths and the Asparagus Trail are described as accessible, but expect sand, dunes and uneven ground elsewhere. The Trust calls Formby a "wild and natural space".

Parking: the thing that catches people out

Formby's popularity is its biggest problem on sunny days.

The two National Trust car parks

  • Lifeboat Road (L37 2EB): a hard-standing path and boardwalk to the beach, with benches. RNLI lifeguards are on duty here from 10am to 6pm in the spring and summer school holidays
  • Victoria Road (L37 1LJ): the car park is now inland, so getting to the beach means walking through woodland and over the dunes, on uneven ground

Both fill up very quickly on busy days. The National Trust suggests:

  • Checking the National Trust Formby Facebook page for live parking updates
  • Watching for the "car parks full" signs on the Formby bypass
  • Using the JustPark app to pay quickly

Don't park on residential streets

The streets around Formby have parking restrictions and fines set by Sefton Council. If the car parks are full, it's better to come back another time or take the train.

November 2026 note

The National Trust says the Lifeboat Road boardwalk is closed on 20, 24 and 27 November for repairs, with diversions over the dunes. Check before you go.

Getting to Formby by train

The National Trust recommends travelling by Merseyrail to Formby or Freshfield stations "if you can". Both are on the Northern line towards Southport.

From Fazakerley, you're on the Kirkby branch of the same line, so you'd change onto a Southport train nearer the city. Check the Merseyrail journey planner for the best connection. From the stations, allow time for the walk to the woods and beach.

When to visit

  • Spring and autumn weekdays: quieter car parks, cooler walking weather, and a better chance of a peaceful woodland walk
  • Summer weekends and school holidays: busiest, with lifeguards and refreshment vans but full car parks. Liverpool's 2027 summer school holiday runs from 22 July to 31 August
  • Winter: bracing beach walks, fewer people, but shorter days and woodland conservation work

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you see red squirrels at Formby?

Formby's pinewoods are an important habitat for rare red squirrels, according to the National Trust. Sightings aren't guaranteed, so walk quietly through the woodland and keep your eyes on the trees.

Where do you park for Formby beach?

The National Trust has two car parks, Lifeboat Road (L37 2EB) and Victoria Road (L37 1LJ). Both fill up very quickly on sunny days. Victoria Road's car park is now inland, so you walk through woodland and over dunes to reach the beach.

Can you get to Formby by train?

Yes. Formby and Freshfield stations are on Merseyrail's Northern line towards Southport, and the National Trust recommends the train on busy days.

Are barbecues allowed on Formby beach?

No. The National Trust says fires and barbecues are not permitted anywhere along the Sefton Coast.

Are there lifeguards at Formby?

The National Trust says RNLI lifeguards are on duty at Lifeboat Road from 10am to 6pm during the spring and summer school holidays.
