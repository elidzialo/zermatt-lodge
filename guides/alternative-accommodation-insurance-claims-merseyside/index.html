Alternative Accommodation for Insurance Claims on Merseyside: A Guide for Bookers

For loss adjusters and claims handlers: placing a displaced household on Merseyside. Typical cover limits, stay lengths, what to check and a 4-bed house.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

When a flood, fire or escape of water leaves a Merseyside home unliveable, the insurer or its loss adjuster usually arranges alternative accommodation for the household. According to ABI guidance, most buildings and contents policies allow up to 20% of the total insured value for alternative accommodation and extra costs, and drying alone can take weeks to months. So the right booking is one that fits the family's daily life and can be extended without a fuss.

This guide is written for the people who place families: loss adjusters, claims handlers and alternative accommodation teams. If you are the displaced householder, our insurance alternative accommodation guide for families is written for you.

The numbers that shape the booking

Factor What the sources say Why it matters

Contents-only cover The limit is based on the contents sum insured (ABI) Tenants' budgets are often much tighter
First contact The loss adjuster should normally contact the household within 24 hours of the claim (ABI) The first booking is often urgent
Drying A few weeks to several months; moisture can stay in walls and floors after surfaces look dry (ABI) Initial bookings are frequently extended

What a displaced household actually needs

Bookers know the policy. The household knows its routine. The placements that work best meet both.

Stay near normal life. The single biggest stress for families is the school run. A home within a sensible drive or train ride of the children's school and the adults' work keeps life stable. Our guide to school places when relocating covers the Liverpool, Sefton and Knowsley systems if a move is longer than expected.

Keep the family together. Two hotel rooms split a family. A whole house with separate bedrooms, a kitchen and a living room keeps routines going.

A kitchen. Self-catering lets the family cook normally, which matters for children, special diets and budgets.

Parking. Many families still need the car for work and school. Free private parking avoids daily charges.

Laundry. Weeks of hotel laundry is expensive and stressful. A washing machine helps.

A quiet, residential street. Families recovering from a loss want a normal home, not a city-centre flat above a bar.

A booking checklist for claims teams

Before you confirm:

  1. Claim reference and billing details. Company name, billing address and whether a VAT invoice is needed
  2. Who is staying. Adults, children, ages, and any mobility needs
  3. Pets. Many providers, including Zermatt Lodge, don't accept them. Check whether the policy covers kennels or catteries
  4. Start date and likely length. Book the first block, then agree how extensions will work
  5. Notice to extend or shorten. Ask the provider how much notice they need
  6. Location. Map the route to school and work at rush hour
  7. Parking. How many spaces, and are they free?
  8. Bedding layout. Who sleeps where? Children may want to share at first
  9. Utilities and Wi-Fi. Included? Is the connection good enough for home working or schoolwork?
  10. Key handover. Can the family check in at short notice?

Where on Merseyside?

Displaced households usually want to stay close to home. North Liverpool, for instance, gives reasonable reach to Sefton (Bootle, Maghull, Crosby), Knowsley (Kirkby) and the north of the city. Motorway access matters for longer commutes: the M57 and M58 meet at Switch Island, and the M57 links to the M62 for Manchester and Warrington.

If someone in the household is also in hospital or attending appointments at Aintree University Hospital or the Walton Centre, both in Fazakerley, a temporary home nearby with free parking can take a lot of pressure off.

When a house is not the right answer

Be honest with the household. A house may not suit:

  • A one-night emergency at 2am, when a hotel is quicker
  • A family with pets that can't go into boarding
  • Someone who needs a ground-floor bedroom and level access throughout. Check layout and steps with the host first

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who arranges alternative accommodation after a home insurance claim?

Usually the insurer, often through a loss adjuster or an accommodation provider it appoints. Comparethemarket notes that arranging your own accommodation or starting repairs without telling your insurer could invalidate a claim, so households should always speak to the insurer first.

How much will insurance pay for alternative accommodation?

It depends on the policy. ABI flood guidance says most buildings and contents policies include up to 20% of the total insured value for alternative accommodation and extra costs. Some insurers set a fixed maximum instead.

How long do alternative accommodation stays last?

It varies with the damage. The ABI says drying alone can take a few weeks to several months, and returning home can take from a few weeks to a year or more. Book with a provider that can extend the stay.

Can the family bring pets to Zermatt Lodge?

No. Zermatt Lodge does not accept pets. Some policies cover kennel or cattery fees when pets can't go to the temporary home, so check the policy.

Can a loss adjuster or insurer pay the invoice directly?

Ask when you book. Give the company name, claim reference and billing address so the invoice can be made out correctly, and say if you need a VAT invoice.
