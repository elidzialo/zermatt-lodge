Here's how to plan a family Christmas in Liverpool, from festive days out to the realities of Christmas Day transport.

Festive things to do in Liverpool

Liverpool City Council's 2026 festive programme includes:

  • Skate St George's: an outdoor ice rink at St George's Hall, run by Underbelly
  • Liverpool Christmas Wonderland @ Pier Head: a covered ice rink with rides, games and experiences, in front of the Three Graces
  • Pop-up seasonal markets with local and independent businesses at city-centre sites, including Basnett Street and the Cunard Building, on selected dates
  • Liverpool City Halls events, including Story Time with Santa, Carols by Lantern Light and A Christmas Carol

Dates and ticketing are announced by each organiser, so check their websites and book popular slots early, especially Santa experiences. See our Liverpool Christmas markets guide and ice skating guide.

For racing fans

Aintree's Becher Chase Day, run over the Grand National fences, falls in early December. In 2026 it's on Saturday 5 December. Aintree is next door to Fazakerley. See our Becher Chase weekend guide.

Getting into town

Take the Merseyrail Northern line from Fazakerley to Liverpool Central (about 15 minutes). St George's Hall is a short walk from Central and Lime Street, and the Pier Head is about 15 minutes' walk through Liverpool ONE. Leave the car at the house, because city-centre car parks get busy in December.

Christmas Day and Boxing Day: plan your transport

This catches many visitors out:

  • Merseyrail doesn't normally run on Christmas Day. Plan to be where you need to be by Christmas Eve, or drive.
  • Boxing Day: Merseyrail has run Boxing Day services in recent years, but the timetable can differ from normal.
  • The Liverpool City Region Combined Authority publishes Christmas and New Year timetables for buses, trains, ferries and tunnels each year. Check them before you finalise plans.
  • Taxis are in high demand on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. Pre-book if you need one.

If family are visiting you on Christmas Day, make sure they have a way to get there and home. Free parking for two cars at the house helps.

Cooking Christmas dinner for 7

Shop early

Large shops in England are closed on Christmas Day, and supermarkets get very busy in the days before. Order your turkey or centrepiece in advance. Do the big shop by the 23rd, and pick up fresh bits on Christmas Eve. There's a Lidl near the house.

Plan the oven

Christmas dinner is mostly about timing. Write a schedule backwards from when you want to eat, and use the hob for veg and gravy to free up oven space. If you've got specific equipment questions, such as a large roasting tin, ask us before you arrive.

Share the jobs

With seven people, make a rota: one on veg, one on pigs in blankets, one laying the table, and a washing-up team afterwards. Our self-catering meal plan for a group of 7 has more ideas.

Bring the essentials

Crackers, decorations, any favourite serving dishes, and something for leftovers.

Christmas at home

Some of the best bits of a family Christmas happen indoors:

  • Board games after dinner
  • A pool tournament in the games room
  • A Christmas film on the 55" Smart TV with Netflix
  • A frosty walk on Boxing Day at Croxteth Country Park or along Crosby Beach

A sample Christmas week

  • 23 December: arrive from 3pm, unpack, big food shop, decorate
  • 24 December: morning in the city by train for skating or the markets, back in good time, as Christmas Eve services can finish earlier than usual, then a film night
  • 25 December: presents, Christmas dinner at the dining table, board games
  • 26 December: a frosty walk at Croxteth Country Park or Crosby Beach, leftovers for lunch, a pool tournament
  • 27 December: checkout by 11am, or stay on for New Year

Adjust to suit your family, and always check the festive transport timetables for the year you're visiting.

Planning tips

  1. Book accommodation early. Christmas and New Year are popular for family gatherings.
  2. Check arrival and departure days. Check-in is from 3pm and checkout is 11am, so plan travel around Christmas Eve and Boxing Day.
  3. Pack for cold, wet weather.
  4. Book festive events as soon as dates are released.
  5. Agree who's cooking what before you arrive.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What's on in Liverpool at Christmas?

Liverpool's 2026 festive programme includes Skate St George's, an outdoor ice rink at St George's Hall, and Liverpool Christmas Wonderland at the Pier Head, with a covered ice rink and rides. There are also pop-up markets on selected dates and family events at the city's halls, such as Story Time with Santa and Carols by Lantern Light.

Do trains run in Liverpool on Christmas Day?

Merseyrail doesn't normally run on Christmas Day, and Boxing Day services have varied from year to year. The Liverpool City Region Combined Authority publishes festive bus, train, ferry and tunnel timetables each year, so check before you plan journeys.

Are supermarkets open on Christmas Day?

Large shops in England are closed on Christmas Day, so do your big food shop before Christmas Eve evening. There's a Lidl near Zermatt Lodge.

Is a house better than a hotel for Christmas?

For a family, usually yes. You can cook your own Christmas dinner, open presents round the tree and eat together at one table, without restaurant set menus or booking deadlines.

How many people can stay at Zermatt Lodge for Christmas?
