A Winter Family Weekend in Manchester: 2-Day Plan

A simple two-day winter itinerary for families in Greater Manchester, with a wet-weather plan, free museums, a park or garden morning, a show, and tips on trams, parking and where to stay.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

For a winter family weekend in Manchester, spend day one in the city centre: Manchester Museum (free, closed Mondays) in the morning, then the National Football Museum or People's History Museum, and a matinee show in December. On day two, get outdoors early at Heaton Park, Dunham Massey or RHS Garden Bridgewater before the light goes, then finish indoors at The Snow Centre or the Trafford Centre. Stay in a whole home on a tram line so you're not driving into town.

Two days is plenty for a family weekend in Manchester in winter, as long as you plan around short daylight and the odd downpour. This plan mixes free museums, one outdoor morning and one treat. We put it together on 25 November 2026 from venue details checked in our other guides. Christmas and New Year hours differ, so check each place before you go.

Before you go

  • Daylight: in December it's dark by about 4pm. Put outdoor plans in the morning.
  • Mondays: many museums are closed, including Manchester Museum. Aim for a Saturday and Sunday, or a Friday and Saturday.
  • Book ahead: shows, light trails and snow sessions sell out at weekends.

Day one: the city centre

Morning: Manchester Museum (free) On Oxford Road. Dinosaurs including Stan the T. rex, and a life-sized moose in Living Worlds. Closed on Mondays. Its Vivarium is closed until May 2027.

Lunch Somewhere central, or a packed lunch from home to keep costs down.

Afternoon: pick one - National Football Museum (paid), about two minutes' walk from Manchester Victoria. - People's History Museum (free), closed on Tuesdays.

Late afternoon or evening - December: a matinee panto or family show. See our pantos guide. - Late November to early January: a light trail. See our light trails guide.

Day two: get outside, then warm up

Morning outdoors: pick one - Heaton Park, Prestwich: open from 8am to dusk, with tram stops close by. See our Heaton Park guide. - Dunham Massey, Altrincham: deer park walks; parking is included in the ticket. See our Dunham Massey guide. - RHS Garden Bridgewater, Worsley: closes at 3.30pm in December. See our Bridgewater guide.

Afternoon indoors: pick one - The Snow Centre Manchester (formerly Chill Factore) for real snow, or LEGOLAND Discovery Centre and SEA LIFE. See our Snow Centre and Trafford Centre guide. - IWM North at Salford Quays, free. See our Salford Quays guide.

Wet weather swaps

  • Swap the park for Salford Museum and Art Gallery and its Victorian street. See our Salford guide.
  • More ideas in our rainy day guide.

Getting around

  • Trams are the easiest way in with children. The Bury line runs from Prestwich and Crumpsall into Manchester Victoria.
  • Bus fares are £2 for adults and £1 for children in 2026, as hopper fares. See our transport guide.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What can families do in Manchester in winter?

Free museums such as Manchester Museum, the People's History Museum and IWM North; panto and Christmas shows in December; light trails at Heaton Park, Dunham Massey and RHS Bridgewater; and indoor snow at The Snow Centre Manchester.

Is two days enough for Manchester with kids?

Yes, for a first visit. One day in the city centre and one day out at a park, garden or TraffordCity works well, with time to rest in between.

What if it rains all weekend?

Swap the outdoor morning for IWM North at Salford Quays, Salford Museum and Art Gallery, or The Snow Centre Manchester, which is indoors.

Should we drive into Manchester city centre?

It is usually easier to park at your accommodation and take the tram. Homes in Prestwich and Crumpsall are on the Metrolink Bury line into Manchester Victoria.
