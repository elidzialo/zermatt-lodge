House-Hunting in Liverpool: How to Plan a Viewing Trip

Planning a house-hunting trip to Liverpool? A three-day viewing plan by area, what to check at viewings, council tax, commutes and where to stay.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

A house-hunting trip to Liverpool works best as three focused days: one loop of viewings in the north and along the coast, one loop in the south or east of the city, and a third day for second viewings, school visits and testing your commute at rush hour. Book viewings in clusters by area, visit at different times of day, and keep a simple scorecard for every property. Stay somewhere with parking and good road and rail links so you're not crossing the city every morning. Before you travel

Narrow down the areas

Liverpool's suburbs are very different from each other. Before you book viewings, decide what matters most:

  • Commute: where's the job, and will you drive or take the train?
  • Schools: which schools do you want to be near? (See our school admissions guide.)
  • Space: garden, parking, number of bedrooms
  • Green space and coast: parks, beaches, countryside
  • Budget: purchase price or rent, plus running costs

Check the council

The Liverpool area is split between several councils, including Liverpool, Sefton (Crosby, Formby, Maghull, Southport), Knowsley (Kirkby, Huyton, Prescot) and Wirral. They set their own council tax and run their own schools admissions.

For example, Liverpool City Council lists its 2026/27 Band D council tax as £2,673.59, including the police, fire and combined authority shares. Lower bands pay a fraction of this and higher bands a multiple. Check the band of any property you view on the government's council tax band checker.

Do your desk research

Before each viewing, look up:

  • The council tax band of the property
  • The energy performance certificate (EPC) on the government's register
  • Flood risk for the postcode
  • Broadband speeds on Ofcom's checker
  • Recent sale prices on the street
  • Street-level crime data on police.uk
  • Planning applications nearby on the council's planning portal

Book viewings in clusters

Ask agents for viewings close together in the same area, with about 45 minutes between each. Group them so you're not driving back and forth.

A three-day viewing plan

This plan suits a family looking at a mix of areas. Swap in your own shortlist.

Day 1: north Liverpool and the coast

  • Morning: Aintree, Orrell Park and Maghull. These are on Merseyrail's Ormskirk branch, so they suit city commuters, and they're close to the M58.
  • Lunch: in Crosby village.
  • Afternoon: Crosby, Blundellsands and Formby, on the Southport branch. Walk on the beach or in the pinewoods to see if coastal life suits you.
  • Evening: drive back and compare notes.

Day 2: south and east Liverpool

  • Morning: West Derby village and the streets around Croxteth Park.
  • Lunch: in Allerton Road or Woolton village.
  • Afternoon: Allerton, Woolton, Mossley Hill and Aigburth, near Sefton Park and Calderstones Park.
  • Evening: dinner on Lark Lane if you want to see the area at night.

Day 3: second viewings and real life

  • Morning rush hour: drive or take the train from your favourite area to your workplace. Note how long it really takes.
  • Late morning: second viewings of your top two or three.
  • Afternoon: walk past the schools at pick-up time, look round the local shops, and visit the GP surgery and nearest park.
  • Evening: visit your top street after dark. Is it quiet? Is it well lit? Is parking easy?

What to check at every viewing

Use the same scorecard for every property so you can compare them later.

Outside

  • Parking: drive, garage or street? Permit zones?
  • Roof, gutters, windows and walls: any obvious damage or damp?
  • Garden: size, privacy and which way it faces
  • Noise: main roads, railway lines, flight paths, pubs

Inside

  • Damp and condensation: check corners, window frames and behind furniture
  • Heating: boiler age and type
  • Windows: double glazed? Do they open?
  • Storage: lofts, cupboards, room for bikes and buggies
  • Mobile signal in every room
  • Water pressure: run a tap and flush a toilet

Questions for the agent

  • Why are the owners selling, or why is the tenant leaving?
  • How long has it been on the market?
  • What's included: carpets, curtains, appliances?
  • For rentals: what's the deposit, when is it available, and are pets allowed?

Testing the commute

By train

Merseyrail's Northern line has three branches heading north: to Southport (via Crosby and Formby), to Ormskirk (via Orrell Park, Aintree and Maghull) and to Kirkby and Headbolt Lane (via Fazakerley). They all meet at Sandhills and run into the city centre. As an example, Trainline puts Fazakerley to Liverpool Central at about 15 minutes.

Try the journey at the time you'll actually travel. Trains are busier, and roads slower, at 8am than at 11am.

By car

The M57 and M58 run from north Liverpool to the M62 and M6, which suits people working in Knowsley, Skelmersdale, Warrington or Manchester. Rush-hour traffic on main roads into the city can be slow, so test it.

Schools and GPs

If you have children, school places will shape where you can live. Councils allocate places based on your permanent address, and Sefton, for example, asks for a completed purchase or a tenancy of at least 6 months as evidence. Liverpool City Council says in-year applications should be processed within 15 school days, but not in the holidays.

Read our school admissions guide and our guide to registering with a GP as a temporary resident.

Where to stay

For a house-hunting trip, a base with these makes life easier:

  • Parking, as you'll be driving between viewings
  • A kitchen, so you're not eating out every night after a long day
  • Space for children, if they're coming
  • Wi-Fi and a table for comparing listings and emailing agents
  • A central location for the areas you're viewing

Why north Liverpool works

From north Liverpool, Aintree, Maghull, Crosby and Formby are a short drive, and the motorways take you east and south. The city centre is a short train ride away.

The honest trade-off: if you're mainly viewing in south Liverpool or the Wirral, a base in the south will save driving.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need for a house-hunting trip to Liverpool?

Three days works well for most buyers and renters: one day for each of two area loops, and a third for second viewings, school visits and checking commutes at rush hour.

How much is council tax in Liverpool?

Liverpool City Council lists the 2026/27 Band D total as £2,673.59, including the police, fire and combined authority shares. Lower bands pay less and higher bands more. Sefton, Knowsley and Wirral set their own rates.

Which areas should families look at around Liverpool?

Common choices include Crosby and Formby on the coast, Maghull and Aintree to the north, West Derby to the east, and Allerton, Woolton and Aigburth in the south. Each has a different balance of price, schools, green space and commute.

Where should I stay for a house-hunting trip?

Somewhere central to the areas you're viewing, with parking. A base in north Liverpool, near the M57 and M58 and on a train line, makes it easy to reach the north and coast, and the city centre is about 15 minutes away by train.
