Long Hospital Stays in Liverpool: Practical Tips for Families

Relative in a Liverpool hospital for weeks? Parking concessions compared, visiting rotas, meals, laundry and keeping children in a routine.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

When a relative is in a Liverpool hospital for weeks, sort the parking concession on day one, agree a visiting rota and stay somewhere you can cook, sleep and do laundry. Zermatt Lodge is a whole house near Aintree University Hospital with free parking for two cars.

When a relative is in a Liverpool hospital for weeks rather than days, the things that wear families down are the practical ones: parking costs, long drives, poor sleep, takeaway food and children's routines falling apart. The fixes are mostly simple: sort the parking concession on day one, agree a visiting rota, and stay somewhere you can cook, sleep and do laundry. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, near Aintree University Hospital, with free parking for two cars and room for up to 7.

This guide pulls together tips that apply across Liverpool's main hospitals.

Sort parking on the first day

Over several weeks, daily hospital parking adds up quickly. Each trust has its own rules, and the prices on their websites change, so check the signs and ask at reception. Here's what the hospitals' own pages said when we checked.

Hospital What families should ask about
Aintree University Hospital Weekly ticket (£17.50 on the hospital's page). Free parking for cancer treatment and for long-term outpatients (3+ visits in 30 days, over at least 3 months). Discretionary free parking for carers of inpatients and relatives called in at short notice
The Walton Centre (same site) 7-day pass (£22.50 on its page). Concession scheme for long-term patients and frequent visitors in designated car parks
Alder Hey Children's Hospital Free parking for inpatient overnight stays (ask the ward), free for Blue Badge holders, and a frequent-visitor concession for 3+ outpatient appointments in 30 days spanning 3 months
Clatterbridge Cancer Centre Liverpool Paddington Village Car Park is free for patients and visitors once validated at reception

Two warnings:

  • Aintree and The Walton Centre share a site but publish different tariffs. Go by the signs on the day
  • Only park in marked bays. Breach of contract notices are issued for verges, hatched areas and pavements

Travel cost help

NHS guidance says the Healthcare Travel Costs Scheme covers eligible patients on certain benefits or low incomes, and sometimes an escort the patient medically needs. It doesn't cover visitors. If the patient qualifies for appointments after discharge, keep receipts and ask the cash office.

Set up a visiting rota

When several relatives are helping, a rota stops everyone arriving at once and then nobody turning up the next day.

  1. Find out the ward's visiting times and any limits on numbers. See how to check Liverpool hospital visiting times
  2. Find out when the ward round happens, so the right person is there to hear updates
  3. Use a shared chat or calendar to show who's visiting when
  4. Nominate one main contact for the ward, so staff aren't repeating updates
  5. Build in rest days. Exhausted visitors aren't much help to anyone

Eat properly

Hospital cafes, vending machines and takeaways are fine for a few days. Over weeks, they leave people tired and run down.

  • Do one big shop at the start of the week
  • Batch-cook a couple of meals that can be reheated after late visits
  • Pack food for the hospital so you're not buying lunch every day
  • Check with the ward before bringing food in for the patient

Keep on top of laundry

Long stays mean the patient needs fresh clothes regularly, and so do you. Agree who collects and returns the patient's washing, and label everything with their name. If you're staying in rented accommodation, ask the host what laundry facilities are available before you arrive.

Keep children in a routine

Children cope better when some things stay the same.

  • Keep bedtimes and mealtimes as normal as you can
  • Plan short visits. Half an hour can be plenty for a young child
  • Give them time outside. A garden, a park or a walk between visits helps
  • Bring familiar things like favourite toys, books and their own pillow
  • Tell their school what's happening. Teachers can help if a child is upset or behind
  • Plan small treats. A trip to the beach or a film night gives them something to look forward to

If a child is the patient, our Alder Hey long-stay tips are written for parents in that situation.

Look after yourselves

  • Sleep in proper beds, not in the car or a hospital chair, whenever you can
  • Take short walks outside the hospital
  • Accept offers of help from friends and neighbours
  • Ask the ward about chaplaincy, patient advice (PALS) and family support services

When discharge comes

Discharge from a long stay is often followed by a recovery period, appointments and a careful journey home. See our guide to hospital discharge family support stays.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I get cheaper parking if I'm visiting a Liverpool hospital every day?

Usually, yes. Aintree University Hospital lists a weekly ticket at £17.50, The Walton Centre lists a 7-day pass at £22.50, and several hospitals offer concessions for long-term patients or discretionary free parking for relatives. Ask on the first day.

Is parking free at Clatterbridge Cancer Centre Liverpool?

The centre says Paddington Village Car Park is free for patients and visitors if you validate your ticket or give your registration at a reception desk.

Can visitors claim back travel costs from the NHS?

Generally not. NHS guidance says the Healthcare Travel Costs Scheme doesn't cover visiting someone in hospital. It covers eligible patients and, in some cases, an escort the patient medically needs.

How do we keep children in a routine during a long hospital stay?

Keep bedtimes and mealtimes as normal as you can, plan short hospital visits rather than long ones, and give children time outdoors and with their own things. A base with a garden and separate bedrooms makes this much easier.
