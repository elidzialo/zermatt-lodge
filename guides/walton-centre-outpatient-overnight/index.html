Travelling to a Walton Centre Appointment: When and How to Stay Overnight

Travelling far for a Walton Centre clinic or scan? Check where it is, plan the night before, parking, trains and a quiet house near the Fazakerley site.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

If you've got an early appointment at The Walton Centre and you live more than a couple of hours away, it's worth staying in Liverpool the night before. The main hospital is in Fazakerley, north Liverpool, and it serves a huge area, so many patients travel from Lancashire, Cheshire, North Wales and the Isle of Man for clinics, scans and tests that start first thing.

This guide is for outpatients and the person coming with them. If you're supporting someone who's been admitted, see our Walton Centre family accommodation guide instead.

First, check where your appointment actually is

The Walton Centre describes itself as the only specialist hospital trust in the UK dedicated to neurology and neurosurgery. It says it:

  • serves around 3.5 million people across Merseyside, Cheshire, Lancashire, Greater Manchester, the Isle of Man, North Wales and beyond
  • treats about 158,000 outpatients a year
  • runs clinics at 19 hospitals and health centres in partnership with 12 NHS trusts

So your appointment may be at a hospital nearer home. Neurosurgery, highly specialised assessments and inpatient care take place at the main site in Fazakerley. Read your letter carefully before booking travel.

The main site in Fazakerley

The Walton Centre shares a campus with Aintree University Hospital on Lower Lane.

The switchboard is 0151 525 3611 if you need to check anything.

Who should stay overnight?

Staying the night before makes sense if:

  • your appointment is before about 10am and you're more than two hours away
  • you're coming from the Isle of Man, where ferry or flight times don't fit an early clinic (see our guide for Isle of Man patients)
  • you're travelling from North Wales or Cumbria and don't want to face the motorway at rush hour (see North Wales patients)
  • you've been told you may be tired, dizzy or unable to drive afterwards, for example after certain tests
  • you have two appointments on consecutive days

If you're not sure whether you'll be fit to drive home, ask the department before you travel. Some tests and treatments mean you shouldn't drive for a while, and you'll need someone with you.

Getting there

By train

The Walton Centre says Fazakerley station is about a 10-minute walk away. It's on the Merseyrail Northern line towards Kirkby and Headbolt Lane, about 15 minutes from Liverpool Central. If you arrive at Lime Street on a mainline train, walk to Central and change.

By car

The site is near the end of the M57 and M58, so drivers from the north and east can usually avoid the city centre. Aintree University Hospital warns that roads around the site are congested at peak times. For an early appointment, that's another reason to be close by the night before.

Parking

Parking for the whole site is managed by Aintree University Hospital and uses number plate recognition, so note your registration. The two hospital pages list different tariffs:

  • Walton Centre page: under 30 minutes free, £4.50 up to 1 hour, £5 up to 2 hours, £6 up to 4 hours, £7 up to 6 hours, £8.50 over 6 hours, 7-day pass £22.50
  • Aintree University Hospital page: under 30 minutes free, £3 up to 1.5 hours, rising to £5.50 over 4.5 hours, weekly ticket £17.50

Check the signs on the day. Blue Badge holders park free in marked bays. Our Aintree hospital parking guide covers concessions.

The night before: a simple checklist

  • Re-read your letter for the building, time and any preparation instructions (fasting, stopping medication, what to wear for a scan).
  • Pack your medication list, glasses, hearing aids and anything the clinic asked you to bring.
  • Plan the journey for the morning, including where you'll park or which train you'll catch.
  • Eat a sensible meal, unless you've been told not to.
  • Get an early night. Clinics can involve a lot of waiting, and some tests are tiring.

Help with travel costs

Some patients can claim help with travel costs to NHS appointments, depending on their circumstances and benefits. Ask the Walton Centre or your referring team whether you're eligible before you travel, and keep receipts.

Coming with someone

It's a good idea to bring a companion for neurology appointments. They can take notes, ask questions you forget and drive if you can't. If several family members want to come, a house can work out simpler than separate hotel rooms.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is my Walton Centre appointment in Liverpool?

Not necessarily. The Walton Centre says it runs clinics at 19 hospitals and health centres across the region, so many outpatient appointments and tests happen closer to home. Neurosurgery, highly specialised assessments and inpatient care are at the main site in Fazakerley, Liverpool. Your letter says where to go.

Which Walton Centre building is my appointment in?

The main site has two buildings on Lower Lane, Fazakerley: the Main Building (L9 7LJ) and the Sid Watkins Building (L9 7BB). Your appointment letter tells you which one.

What's the nearest station to the Walton Centre?

Fazakerley, on the Merseyrail Northern line. The Walton Centre says it's about a 10-minute walk. Trains run from Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes.

Should I stay overnight before a Walton Centre appointment?

If your appointment is early in the morning and you live more than a couple of hours away, or you're coming from the Isle of Man or North Wales, staying the night before removes the risk of being late and lets you arrive rested.

How much is parking at the Walton Centre?

The Walton Centre's page lists under 30 minutes free, then £4.50 up to an hour, rising to £8.50 over 6 hours, with a 7-day pass at £22.50. The Aintree University Hospital page for the same site shows different figures, so check the signs on the day.
