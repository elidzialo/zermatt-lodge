Anfield Stadium Tour and Museum: A Practical Guide for Visitors

Planning the Anfield Stadium Tour? Tour types, how long it takes, what you see, quoted prices, getting there by bus or train and planning a day around it.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

The Anfield Stadium Tour takes about 70 to 90 minutes, plus time in the LFC Museum, so allow two to three hours. Tours usually run daily from 10am to 3pm; book ahead and check times around match days.

The Anfield Stadium Tour takes about 70 to 90 minutes, plus time in the LFC Museum, so allow two to three hours for the full visit. Tours run daily, usually from 10am to 3pm, and take you through the home dressing room, down the tunnel past the This Is Anfield sign and into the dugout. Book ahead, especially in school holidays, and check the schedule around match days, when times change.

Here's what to expect, the different tour types and how to fit the visit into a day in Liverpool.

What you see on the standard tour

According to Football Ground Guide, the standard LFC Stadium Tour & Museum covers:

  • the home dressing room
  • the players' tunnel and the This Is Anfield sign
  • the press conference room
  • the manager's dugout
  • pitchside viewing areas (you don't walk on the pitch)
  • entry to the LFC Museum, with trophies including the six European Cups and the Liverpool FC Story

Anfield holds over 61,000 people, according to Wikipedia, and seeing it empty is a different experience from a match day.

Opening times

Football Ground Guide, citing Liverpool FC's help centre, lists:

  • Stadium tours: seven days a week, 10am to 3pm
  • LFC Museum: daily, 9am to 5pm

Both can change because of matches, concerts and other events. On match days and concert days the area around the stadium is extremely busy, so normal tours may not run.

Accessibility

Football Ground Guide notes wheelchair-accessible routes, pram access, personal assistant tickets and guide dog access. If anyone in your group has specific needs, contact Liverpool FC before booking to confirm.

Getting to Anfield on a non-match day

On a normal day, Anfield is much easier to reach than on match days.

  • Bus: Liverpool FC lists routes including the 26 from Liverpool ONE bus station and the 17 from Queen Square. Check current timetables with Merseytravel.
  • Train and walk: Kirkdale station, on the Merseyrail Northern line Kirkby branch, is about a mile's walk from the stadium. Liverpool FC signposts walking routes from Kirkdale, Sandhills and Lime Street.
  • Taxi: a short ride from the city centre. Liverpool City Council says black cabs must use the meter for journeys within the city.
  • Car: check parking arrangements for your date. Remember that Anfield is a residents' parking area, with strict rules on match days and concert days.

Making a day of it

Stanley Park

Stanley Park sits between Anfield and Goodison Park. It's a good place for a walk or a picnic after the tour. See our Stanley Park guide.

Goodison Park and Everton's new stadium

Goodison Park, across the park, is now home to Everton Women. Everton's men's team plays at the Hill Dickinson Stadium on the waterfront at Bramley-Moore Dock, which runs its own tours. Our Everton stadium tour guide covers it, and our Anfield to Everton heritage walk links the sites on foot.

A sample day

  • 9.30am: travel to Anfield
  • 10am to 1pm: tour and museum
  • 1pm: lunch near the ground
  • 2pm: walk through Stanley Park
  • Afternoon: into the city centre or the waterfront

Tips

  • Book a timed slot online. Walking up on a busy day can mean a long wait or no space.
  • Combine with a match weekend by touring on the day before or after the game.
  • Children: the tour suits most ages, but younger kids may find 90 minutes long. See our guide to a football trip to Liverpool with kids.
  • Photos: bring a charged phone. The tunnel, dugout and This Is Anfield sign are the shots everyone wants.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does the Anfield stadium tour take?

Football Ground Guide says the standard tour takes about 70 to 90 minutes, not counting the museum. Allow two to three hours for the full visit.

What do you see on the Anfield tour?

The standard tour includes the home dressing room, the players' tunnel and the This Is Anfield sign, the press conference room, the manager's dugout and pitchside viewing areas. It doesn't include walking on the pitch. Museum entry is included.

How much is the Anfield stadium tour?

Football Ground Guide quotes £26 for an adult, £21 for seniors and students, £17 for under-16s and £77 for a family of two adults and two children, with museum-only tickets from £14. Prices change, so check the official LFC booking site.

Can you do the Anfield tour on a match day?

Liverpool FC runs a Matchday tour on selected dates, but normal tour times can change because of matches and events. Check the schedule for your date before you book travel.

What time does the LFC Museum open?

Football Ground Guide, citing Liverpool FC's help centre, lists stadium tours seven days a week from 10am to 3pm and the museum daily from 9am to 5pm.
