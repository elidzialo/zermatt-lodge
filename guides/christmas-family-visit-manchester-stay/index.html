Visiting Family in Manchester at Christmas

Coming home to Greater Manchester for Christmas 2026? How Christmas Day and New Year transport worked last year, what's open, parking, and why a whole house near your relatives can beat squeezing in.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

If you're visiting relatives in Greater Manchester over Christmas 2026, plan for no trams on Christmas Day: last year Metrolink did not run on 25 December, and Bee Network buses ran special timetables on selected routes on Boxing Day and New Year's Day. On New Year's Eve 2025 trams ran every 12 minutes, with the last leaving the city centre at about 1am. A whole house with parking near your family, rather than sleeping on a sofa, gives everyone space. Check TfGM's Christmas page for 2026 times.

Lots of people come back to Greater Manchester at Christmas to see parents, grandparents and cousins. The days are lovely; the logistics are harder. Where does everyone sleep, how do you get about on Christmas Day, and what's open? This guide covers the practical side. We checked these details on 25 November 2026. Transport plans for Christmas 2026 hadn't been published when we checked, so we describe last year's pattern.

Transport over Christmas: last year's pattern

For Christmas 2025 into New Year 2026, reports of the TfGM plans said:

Day Metrolink trams Bee Network buses
Christmas Eve At least every 15 minutes Normal weekday timetable, some finishing earlier
Christmas Day No trams Not stated in the report
Boxing Day Not stated in the report Special timetables on selected routes
New Year's Eve Every 12 minutes, last trams from the city centre about 1am Saturday timetable, plus some late journeys
New Year's Day At least every 15 minutes Special timetables on selected routes

What this means for you: if you're staying a tram ride away from your family, plan how you'll get there on Christmas Day. Most people drive, walk or book a taxi well in advance. Check TfGM's Christmas operating times for 2026 nearer the date.

Why a nearby house helps

  • Space: your own bedrooms, so nobody sleeps on an airbed for a week.
  • A second kitchen: handy for cooking part of Christmas dinner, or making breakfast without getting in the way.
  • Parking: on Christmas Day you'll probably drive, so a driveway or free parking matters.
  • A quiet base: small children can nap, and grandparents' homes don't get overcrowded.

What's open and what isn't

  • Christmas Day: almost everything closes, including most museums and The Snow Centre. Large shops and supermarkets in England must close by law, so shop on Christmas Eve.
  • Boxing Day: sales and some attractions reopen; many museums stay shut. Check before you go.
  • Between Christmas and New Year: pantos, light trails and The Snow Centre are good options. See our December events guide and pantos guide.
  • New Year's Eve: see our New Year's Eve guide.

Booking tips

  • Book early. Larger homes go first over Christmas and New Year.
  • Check the minimum stay. Some homes set a minimum number of nights over the holidays.
  • Arrive before Christmas Eve if you can, while shops and transport are on normal hours.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do Manchester trams run on Christmas Day?

Last year Metrolink did not run on Christmas Day. Check TfGM's Christmas operating times for 2026 before you travel.

Do buses run on Boxing Day in Greater Manchester?

For Christmas 2025, Bee Network buses ran special timetables on a selection of routes on Boxing Day and New Year's Day. Check the 2026 plans nearer the time.

What time is the last tram on New Year's Eve?

On New Year's Eve 2025 trams ran every 12 minutes and the last trams left Manchester city centre at about 1am. The 2026 times may differ.

Is it better to rent a house than stay with relatives at Christmas?

For a bigger family it often is. You get your own bedrooms and kitchen nearby, the hosts get their home back at night, and you can still spend the days together.
