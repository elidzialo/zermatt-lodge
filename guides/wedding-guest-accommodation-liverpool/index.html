Wedding Guest Accommodation in Liverpool: Staying Together Instead of Scattered Hotel Rooms

Guests coming to a Liverpool wedding? How to keep your group together, getting ready, taxis to the venue, St George's Hall parking and a house for up to 7.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

A group of guests going to a Liverpool wedding can book one house instead of several hotel rooms, so you get ready together and share transport. A city-centre ceremony suits a base on a direct train line; venues on the edge of the city suit a base with parking.

If you're a group of guests travelling to a Liverpool wedding, the simplest option is often to book one house together rather than several hotel rooms. You get ready in one place, share lifts or a pre-booked taxi to the venue, and wake up together the next morning. The right location depends on where the wedding is: a city-centre ceremony suits somewhere on a direct train line, while venues on the edge of the city suit a base with parking.

This guide is for guests, not couples. It covers the practical side of getting a group to a Liverpool wedding on time and home safely.

Start with the venue

Before you book anywhere, find out three things from the couple or the invitation:

  1. Where's the ceremony, and is the reception somewhere else? Many Liverpool weddings have a ceremony in one place and a reception in another.
  2. What time does it finish? A midnight finish needs a different plan from a 6pm one.
  3. Is there transport between venues? Some couples book a coach. Most don't.

City-centre venues

Liverpool's register office ceremonies take place at St George's Hall, opposite Lime Street station. The city's ceremonies team says:

  • There's no onsite parking for guests.
  • Nearby car parks include Lime Street Station Long Stay (Lord Nelson Street, L3 5QB), St John's Shopping Centre (St George's Place, L1 1LQ) and Queen Square (Whitechapel, L1 1RH).
  • Blue Badge bays are spread around the city centre.
  • Wedding cars can book access to St George's Plateau to drop the couple near the south steps. There's a fee, 48 hours' notice is needed and access isn't guaranteed if other events are on.

For city-centre weddings, the train is your friend. If you're staying somewhere on a Merseyrail line, you can arrive without worrying about parking at all. See our guide for Liverpool Town Hall wedding guests too.

Venues on the edge of the city

Country-house and park venues around north and east Liverpool, such as Croxteth Hall in West Derby or Knowsley Hall, are much easier to reach by car or taxi than by train. Our guides to Croxteth Hall weddings and Knowsley Hall events go into those in detail.

Getting ready as a group

This is where a shared house really earns its keep.

  • Bathrooms: count them. Two bathrooms for seven people is workable if you plan a rota. One isn't.
  • Mirrors and light: a big kitchen table near a window makes a better make-up station than a hotel desk.
  • Ironing: ask whether there's an iron and board. Don't assume.
  • Timing: work backwards from when you need to leave. Allow at least 20 minutes per person in the bathroom, and more for anyone having hair done.
  • Food: eat a proper breakfast or brunch. Wedding breakfasts are often served late afternoon.

Taxis: book them before the day

Liverpool City Council's advice is worth knowing:

  • Private hire cars must be pre-booked. Don't get into one that hasn't been booked for you.
  • Black cabs (hackney carriages) must use the meter for journeys within Liverpool, and can only charge what it shows.

On a Saturday night, especially if there's a match or a concert in the city, taxis are in demand. Book your return trip in advance and agree a pick-up point that's easy for a driver to find. If the venue is out of town, ask the venue which local firms they recommend.

Designate a driver if you're driving. If your group plans to drink, one car can stay at the accommodation and you take a taxi both ways.

The morning after

The day after a wedding is when a shared house is at its best. A slow breakfast around one table, swapping photos and stories, beats queuing for a hotel buffet in separate groups. Check the checkout time so nobody's rushing; 11am is common for houses.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where should wedding guests stay in Liverpool?

It depends on the venue. For city-centre ceremonies at St George's Hall or the Town Hall, a central hotel or somewhere on a direct train line works well. For venues on the edge of the city, such as Croxteth Hall or Knowsley Hall, a house with parking in north or east Liverpool is often closer and easier.

Is there parking at St George's Hall for weddings?

No onsite parking for guests. Liverpool's ceremonies team lists nearby car parks including Lime Street Station Long Stay, St John's Shopping Centre and Queen Square. Wedding cars can book a drop-off on St George's Plateau with 48 hours' notice, for a fee.

How do we get a taxi after a Liverpool wedding?

Pre-book. Liverpool City Council says private hire cars must be booked in advance, and black cabs must use the meter for journeys within Liverpool. Book your return before the day, especially for late finishes on Saturdays.

Can we have an after-party at the house?

No. Zermatt Lodge has a strict no parties or events rule. It's a place to get ready, sleep and have a quiet breakfast together the next morning, not a venue.

How many wedding guests can stay at Zermatt Lodge?
