Pier Head and the Three Graces: What to See

Visiting Liverpool's Pier Head? The Three Graces explained, the Royal Liver Building 360 tour, the Mersey Ferry, the free Museum of Liverpool and tips for a winter visit.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

The Pier Head is Liverpool's riverfront, home to the Three Graces: the Royal Liver Building (1911), the Cunard Building (1916) and the Port of Liverpool Building (1907). You can go up the Royal Liver Building on the 70-minute RLB360 tour (£16 adults, £11 children 5 to 15, no under-5s), catch the 50-minute Mersey Ferry River Explorer Cruise, and visit the free Museum of Liverpool, all within a few minutes' walk.

The Pier Head is the Liverpool skyline you see on postcards: three grand buildings on the river, with the Liver Birds on top. It's free to wander and sits right next to the Royal Albert Dock, so most visitors pass through at some point.

The Royal Liver Building has two clock towers, each topped by a Liver Bird, the city's best-known symbol.

There's a £1.75 admin charge per booking (not per ticket) for advance bookings. You can buy on the day, but peak tours sell out. Allow an extra 30 minutes for the free visitor centre.

The Mersey Ferry

Mersey Ferries sail from the Pier Head. The River Explorer Cruise takes about 50 minutes with commentary. Seacombe terminal is closed for refurbishment, so the cruise currently runs between the Pier Head and Woodside. Book timed tickets online. Our Mersey Ferry guide has times and tips.

Museum of Liverpool

The free Museum of Liverpool, opened in 2011, sits on the Pier Head next to the Three Graces. It's a good hour on the city's story, and a handy place to warm up in winter. See our museums guide.

A bit of history

  • The waterfront was at the heart of Liverpool Maritime Mercantile City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site from 2004 until it was removed in 2021
  • The Liverpool Canal Link, a 1.6-mile extension of the Leeds and Liverpool Canal, runs past the Pier Head to the South Docks. It opened in 2009
  • The current ferry terminal and landing stage opened in 2012

Combine it with

  • The Royal Albert Dock, a short walk south. See our Albert Dock guide
  • The city centre and Mathew Street, up the hill
  • A full plan: Liverpool in one day

Winter tips

  • It's windy on the river. Bring a warm coat
  • Daylight is short. Do the waterfront in the middle of the day and save indoor things for later
  • Photos: the Three Graces look great from the Albert Dock side and from the ferry

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What are the Three Graces in Liverpool?

The Royal Liver Building, the Cunard Building and the Port of Liverpool Building, three early 20th-century buildings on the Pier Head. The Royal Liver Building is Grade I listed and the other two are Grade II*.

Can you go inside the Royal Liver Building?

Yes, on the RLB360 tour. It lasts 70 minutes and costs £16 for adults (16+) and £11 for children aged 5 to 15. Under-5s aren't allowed. The tour climbs 124 stairs above the 10th floor.

What are the Liver Birds?

The two birds on top of the Royal Liver Building's clock towers. They're one of the best-known symbols of Liverpool.

Is the Museum of Liverpool free?

Yes. It's one of the free National Museums Liverpool venues, and it opened at the Pier Head in 2011.

Is Liverpool still a World Heritage Site?

No. Liverpool Maritime Mercantile City was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004 and removed from the list in 2021.
