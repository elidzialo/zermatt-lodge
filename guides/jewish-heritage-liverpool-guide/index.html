Jewish Liverpool: Princes Road Synagogue and the Heritage Trail

Jewish heritage in Liverpool for visitors: Princes Road Synagogue tours, the Museum of Liverpool's Jewish Community Trail and Galkoff's tiles.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Start with a pre-booked tour of Princes Road Synagogue, a Grade I listed Victorian building; book tours via its official website. Then follow the free Jewish Community Trail at the Museum of Liverpool, which includes the reconstructed Galkoff's kosher butcher's shopfront.

The best way to see Jewish Liverpool is in two parts. First, book a tour of Princes Road Synagogue, which must be pre-booked via its official website. Then follow the Jewish Community Trail at the Museum of Liverpool, which includes the life-size Galkoff's kosher butcher's shopfront.

Liverpool has one of the oldest Jewish communities in Britain.

Princes Road Synagogue

Why it's special

Princes Road Synagogue, home of the Liverpool Old Hebrew Congregation, is often described as one of the finest synagogues in Britain. The synagogue describes the building as Grade I listed, and heritage guides praise its late Victorian interior. It is about 150 years old.

Booking a tour

Tours are for pre-booked adult groups and school groups. Book tours via its official website, which has current details and prices.

Before you go: the synagogue is a working place of worship. Dress modestly and ask before taking photos.

The Jewish Community Trail at the Museum of Liverpool

National Museums Liverpool publishes a free Liverpool Jewish Community Trail for the Museum of Liverpool on the waterfront. Look for the menorah symbol next to trail objects. Highlights include:

Floor one

  • The first synagogue. The museum's timeline marks the first purpose-built synagogue, in 1807, which seated 300 people.
  • Brian Epstein's autobiography, A Cellarful of Noise (1964). The Beatles' manager writes about his Jewish upbringing in Liverpool.

Floor two

  • Yentl on the Mersey. Barbra Streisand's 1983 film shot scenes on a Mersey ferry, with local extras.
  • Frankie Vaughan. A signed photo and one of his straw boaters. Vaughan, born to a Jewish immigrant family in Liverpool, became a world-famous singer.
  • Sarah Levy's identity book. Her family arrived in the 1880s, fleeing persecution in Russia.
  • David Lewis's merchant token. Lewis founded Lewis's department store in 1856, and his charity still helps young people.
  • Torah scroll decorations from Princes Road Synagogue.

Galkoff's

The trail's centrepiece is a life-size reconstruction of Galkoff's, a former kosher butcher's shop. Its famous green tiles were added in 1933 with Hebrew "kosher" lettering. The museum says 113 of the 855 tiles were replaced during conservation, and the tiles were moved to the museum in 2018. Nearby you'll find the butcher's coat and knife of Issy Silver, who worked there from the late 1940s, and a model of the shop.

The museum sometimes runs a ticketed talk, "Beyond the label: Galkoff's tiles". Check the museum's events page before your trip.

Archives

The Liverpool Record Office at Central Library holds the Merseyside Jewish archives. Check with the library about current displays and research access.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you tour Princes Road Synagogue?

Yes. The synagogue offers pre-booked tours for adult and school groups. Book tours via its official website, which has current details and prices.

What is the Liverpool Jewish Community Trail?

A self-guided trail through the Museum of Liverpool, published by National Museums Liverpool. It picks out objects linked to the city's Jewish history, from the first purpose-built synagogue in 1807 to Brian Epstein's autobiography and the Galkoff's shopfront.

What are Galkoff's tiles?

The green-tiled front of Galkoff's, a former kosher butcher's. The tiles, with Hebrew 'kosher' lettering, were moved to the Museum of Liverpool in 2018, where a life-size reconstruction of the shopfront is on display.
