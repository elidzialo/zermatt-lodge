Liverpool Match Day Parking Guide: Anfield and Everton's New Stadium

Where to park for Anfield and Everton's new stadium. Residents' zones, why there's no stadium parking, pre-booked options and train-in alternatives.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

You cannot park at Anfield or Everton's stadium on match days, and the streets around both have parking restrictions. Leave the car somewhere you can stay all day and travel in by Merseyrail or the official matchday buses; Sandhills station serves both clubs.

The short answer: you can't park at either Liverpool football stadium on match days, and the streets around both are covered by parking restrictions. The easiest approach is to leave your car somewhere you're allowed to stay all day, then travel in by Merseyrail or the official matchday buses. Sandhills station serves both clubs. If you're staying over, a base on the Northern line with its own off-street parking, like Zermatt Lodge in Fazakerley, solves the problem completely.

This guide explains the rules at Anfield and at Everton's stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock, and your realistic options.

Anfield: the rules

According to Liverpool FC's matchday travel guidance:

  • There's no paid parking at Anfield on match days.
  • Anfield is in a residents' parking zone. Enforcement is active, and illegally parked vehicles will be fined.
  • Accessible drop-off points are on Gilman Street (near the Kop and Main Stand) and Oakfield Road.
  • There are designated taxi ranks outside the stadium and on main routes.
  • A free, attended Cycle Hub is at the top of Stanley Park Car Park, a few minutes from the Anfield Road end. Bring a lock and keep your receipt.

People who think they'll "find something on a side street" often find permit signs everywhere and very little legal space within walking distance.

Everton's stadium: the rules

Everton's stadium is on the waterfront at Bramley-Moore Dock, off Regent Road (A565).

  • Matchday parking is limited, and the club encourages public transport.
  • Liverpool City Council's plans set out a football match parking zone within roughly a 30-minute walk of the stadium. It includes the Ten Streets area and reaches into the city centre, with new residents' parking areas, restrictions and changed hours for existing zones around Great Homer Street, Ten Streets and Love Lane.
  • The council has since announced community-led changes to the scheme, so check its current rules before you travel.
  • Taxi ranks have been planned at Sandhills station, Boundary Street and Dublin Street.
  • Cycle stands are along the dock wall inside the stadium site.

Your realistic parking options

1. Leave the car where you're staying

If you're staying over, this is the easiest option. Choose accommodation with free off-street parking near a Northern line station. On match day, take the train to Sandhills (for either club) or Kirkdale (for Anfield on foot).

2. Pre-book a space outside the restricted zones

Operators such as Q-Park, JustPark and YourParkingSpace list event parking for Everton's stadium. Before you pay:

  • Check the space is outside any matchday restricted zone, or that the operator confirms it's valid on match days.
  • Check exit times. Some car parks have queues of an hour or more after the final whistle.
  • Make sure you can walk safely between the space and the ground.

3. Park in the city centre and take a shuttle

Liverpool city centre has plenty of multi-storey car parks. From there:

  • Anfield: the 917 express runs from Commutation Row about every 10 minutes on match days (£2.30 adult single, £4.60 return). The 26 runs from Liverpool ONE Bus Station and the 17 from Queen Square.
  • Everton: the 919 runs from Commutation Row/Lime Street and the 929 from Liverpool ONE Bus Station (£2 single), from two hours before kick-off.

Many city car parks charge more on event days, so compare prices in advance.

4. Get dropped off

If someone's driving you, agree a drop-off point well outside the zone, and walk the final stretch. Use the official accessible drop-off points only if someone in your group needs them.

The train-in approach: step by step

From a north Liverpool base on the Kirkby branch (Fazakerley, Rice Lane or Kirkdale):

For Anfield

  1. Take the Northern line to Sandhills.
  2. Catch the Soccerbus. It runs from two hours before kick-off until about 15 minutes before.
  3. After the match, return Soccerbuses run for about 90 minutes, with the last one leaving Walton Lane about 75 minutes after the final whistle.

Or get off at Kirkdale and follow the signposted walking route.

For Everton

  1. Take the Northern line to Sandhills.
  2. Walk about 15 minutes to the stadium.

Tips to make match day easier

  • Check the kick-off time before choosing your option. Evening and Sunday kick-offs affect train frequency.
  • Have a back-up plan, such as a taxi app or the 501 Peoplesbus from Walton Lane to Lime Street after Anfield games.
  • Don't block driveways or dropped kerbs. Residents deal with this every match day.
  • Factor in post-match queues wherever you park.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is there parking at Anfield on match days?

No. Liverpool FC says there's no paid parking at Anfield on match days, and the area is a residents' parking zone with active enforcement.

Can I park near Everton's new stadium?

Matchday parking is limited, and the council has introduced a football match parking zone covering a wide area around Bramley-Moore Dock. Everton encourages fans to use public transport. Some private operators sell pre-bookable spaces, but read the terms carefully.

What's the best way to avoid parking problems on match day?

Park somewhere you're allowed to stay all day, such as your accommodation or a pre-booked car park outside the restricted zones. Then travel in by Merseyrail or the official matchday buses.

Which station serves both Liverpool grounds?

Sandhills. It's the closest station to Everton's stadium, and Liverpool FC's Soccerbus runs from Sandhills to Anfield. Kirkdale, one stop away, also has signposted walking routes to Anfield.

Where are the accessible drop-off points at Anfield?

Liverpool FC lists Gilman Street (near the Kop and Main Stand) and Oakfield Road as accessible drop-off points.
