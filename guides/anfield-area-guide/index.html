Anfield Area Guide: What to See Beyond Match Day

Anfield away from match day: Stanley Park and the Isla Gladstone Conservatory, Homebaked, the area's regeneration and how to visit from north Liverpool.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Anfield is the north Liverpool district around Liverpool FC's stadium, and away from match day it's a quieter, residential area with one big asset: Stanley Park, a Victorian park with a restored glasshouse, the Isla Gladstone Conservatory. There's also Homebaked, a community bakery known for its pies, and the stadium itself for tours and summer concerts. The area has been through a long regeneration and parts of the high street are still quiet between games, so most visitors combine it with the city centre. Where Anfield is

Anfield is a district about 2 miles north-east of Liverpool city centre. It sits next to Everton and Walton, with Stanley Park between Anfield stadium and Goodison Park. Walton Breck Road and Oakfield Road are the main streets around the stadium.

Anfield stadium

Liverpool FC's ground dominates the area. After the Anfield Road Stand redevelopment, the club confirmed a capacity of 61,276 for the 2024-25 season. Away from match days you can:

  • Take a stadium tour and visit the museum. See our Anfield stadium tour guide
  • See summer concerts. Anfield hosted major stadium gigs in June 2026, including Foo Fighters. See Anfield concert accommodation
  • Visit the club shop

On match days the area changes completely, with road closures, a residents' parking zone and heavy crowds. Our Anfield match day guide covers that side.

Stanley Park

Stanley Park is the best reason to spend time in Anfield on a non-match day.

  • Opened: 14 May 1870
  • Designer: Edward Kemp
  • Size: about 45 hectares (110 acres)
  • Famous for: sitting between the home grounds of Liverpool and Everton

The Isla Gladstone Conservatory

The park's glasshouse was built in 1899 by Mackenzie & Moncur of Edinburgh. It's Grade II listed and was restored and renamed the Isla Gladstone Conservatory. It's worth a look even if you're only passing on the way to the stadium.

The stadium that never was

Plans to build a new stadium on part of Stanley Park were first announced in 2000. After a change of club ownership, the project was scrapped in October 2012 in favour of expanding Anfield instead, which is what you see today.

Using the park

  • A pre-match walk if you arrive early
  • A run or a kickabout on a quiet day
  • A walk between the two grounds, with Goodison Park on the far side

Goodison Park

Across Stanley Park is Goodison Park, Everton's home for well over a century. Everton's men's team moved to the Hill Dickinson Stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock, and Goodison became the permanent home of Everton Women from the 2025-26 season. If you're a football fan, seeing both grounds on one walk across the park is a classic Liverpool experience. For Everton's new ground, see our Everton stadium accommodation guide.

Homebaked

Homebaked is a bakery on the corner of Oakfield Road and Donaldson Road, a short walk from the stadium. It opened in 2012 on the site of the old Mitchell's bakery, starting as part of the Liverpool Biennial, and became a community land trust the same year. It sells bread, pies and buns, and has become one of the city's best-known social enterprises.

Practical tip: on match days expect queues, so go early or on a quieter day.

The regeneration story

Anfield's streets tell a long story. According to reporting on the area:

  • Victorian terraces were cleared from 2004 under the Housing Market Renewal Initiative, leading to hundreds of new and refurbished homes, a new primary school and work on Stanley Park
  • The programme was cancelled nationally in 2011, leaving gaps
  • In 2014 the council adopted a Strategic Regeneration Framework, with the council, Liverpool FC, Your Housing Group and Keepmoat Homes working on key sites
  • At the time of that reporting, about 60% of the regeneration was complete, with sites such as Anfield Square on Walton Breck Road still to be developed

The honest picture for visitors is that some shops and pubs on the main roads rely heavily on match days and can be quiet in between. That's changing slowly, but don't expect a busy high street on a Tuesday.

Getting to Anfield from north Liverpool

From Fazakerley, the usual route on match days is the Merseyrail Northern line to Sandhills, then the matchday Soccerbus to Anfield. On non-match days, a bus or a short drive is simplest, and parking is easier when there's no game. See our match day parking guide before driving on a game day.

A half-day in Anfield

  1. Morning: stadium tour (book ahead).
  2. Late morning: walk through Stanley Park and see the Isla Gladstone Conservatory.
  3. Lunch: pies from Homebaked.
  4. Afternoon: walk past Goodison Park, then head into the city or back to your base.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is there to do in Anfield apart from football?

Stanley Park, a Victorian park opened in 1870, and its restored Isla Gladstone Conservatory; Homebaked, the community bakery near the stadium; and stadium tours and concerts at Anfield itself.

How big is Anfield stadium now?

Liverpool FC confirmed a capacity of 61,276 for the 2024-25 season, after the Anfield Road Stand redevelopment.

Is Goodison Park still used?

Yes. Everton's men's team moved to the Hill Dickinson Stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock, and Goodison Park became the permanent home of Everton Women from the 2025-26 season.

Is Anfield a good place to stay?

It's very convenient for match days, but much of the high street is quiet between games and accommodation choice is limited. Many visitors stay elsewhere in north Liverpool or the city centre and travel in.
