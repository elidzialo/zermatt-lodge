Booking Direct vs Airbnb or Booking.com: An Honest Comparison

Book a holiday home direct or via Airbnb or Booking.com? How platform fees work, what protection you get and how to check a direct site is safe.

Quick answer

Booking a holiday home direct with the host is often cheaper than booking through Airbnb or Booking.com, because no platform commission has to be built into the price. The trade-off is that you rely on the host's own terms instead of the platform's, so you need to check the site is genuine and pay by card. If you do those checks, booking direct is a sensible way to save money, especially for longer or larger group stays. How platform fees work

Booking platforms make money by taking a cut of each booking. How that shows up for guests has changed.

Airbnb

Airbnb has used two fee models:

  • Split fee: the guest pays a service fee on top of the price, and the host pays a smaller fee
  • Single fee: the host pays the whole fee and the guest sees no separate service fee

From 27 October 2025, Airbnb moved hosts who use property or channel management software to the single-fee model, with a 15.5% service fee deducted from the host's price, according to Rentals United's summary of the change.

What this means for you as a guest: you may no longer see a separate service fee line, but the fee hasn't disappeared. Hosts usually raise their nightly rate to cover it. On a stay of several nights for a group, that adds up.

Booking.com and other platforms

Booking.com and similar sites also charge hosts commission. Exact rates vary by property and agreement, so we won't quote a figure, but the principle is the same: the commission is reflected somewhere in the price you pay.

Booking direct

When you book direct, the host keeps the whole payment, so there's room to charge less than on a platform and still earn the same. On Book Homes, the price you see is the total for the stay, including cleaning, with no guest booking fees.

Neither route is right for everyone. Platforms suit people who want a familiar system and a big choice of homes. Direct booking suits people who've found the place they want and would rather not pay extra.

The real risk: fake listings and scams

Holiday booking fraud is real. MoneySavingExpert, citing Action Fraud, reported that just over £11 million was lost to holiday booking scams in a year, with July the peak month for reports.

Common tricks include:

  • Cloned websites with addresses that look almost identical to the real one
  • Social media posts advertising homes that don't exist, or that belong to someone else
  • Hosts asking for a bank transfer to "secure" a booking or get a discount
  • Deals that seem too good to be true

How to book direct safely: a checklist

Use this for any direct booking site, not just ours.

  1. Check the web address. Make sure it matches the site you meant to visit.
  2. Look for a real business. A working phone number and an email address that gets answered are basic signs.
  3. Cross-check the property. Is the same home listed on a major platform with reviews? Do the photos match?
  4. Pay by card on a secure checkout. MoneySavingExpert says credit card purchases over £100 may be covered by Section 75, and debit card or smaller credit card payments may be covered by chargeback.
  5. Avoid bank transfers and money transfer services. You have very little protection if something goes wrong.
  6. Read the terms. Cancellation policy, damage deposit, house rules and what's included.
  7. Get written confirmation of your dates, guest numbers and total price.
  8. Call the host if anything feels off. A genuine host will be happy to talk.

When Airbnb might suit you better

Be honest with yourself about what you value:

  • You already have Airbnb credit or a gift card
  • You want everything in one app with your other trips
  • You're more comfortable with the platform's own processes

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to book a holiday home direct?

Often, because there's no platform commission to cover. Airbnb's single-fee model for many professional hosts deducts a 15.5% service fee from the host's price, and hosts usually build platform fees into their rates. Always compare the total price for the same dates.

Is it safe to book direct?

It can be, if you check the site properly: a real business with a working phone number, a secure card payment page and clear terms. Never pay a host by bank transfer for a booking you found on social media.

What protection do I get paying by card?

MoneySavingExpert explains that credit card purchases over £100 may be covered by Section 75, and debit card or lower-value credit card payments may be covered by chargeback. Bank transfers give you very little protection.

Can I still book Zermatt Lodge on Airbnb?

Yes. The same house is listed on Airbnb. Book direct on our site if you'd rather avoid platform fees, or use Airbnb if you prefer its system.
