Accessible Group Stays in Liverpool: What to Ask Before You Book

Booking a Liverpool group stay with a wheelchair user or access needs? Questions to ask any host, plus Blue Badge parking, trains and Changing Places.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

If someone in your group has access needs, the most important thing is to ask the host specific questions before you pay: how many steps there are to the door, whether there's a bedroom and bathroom on the same level, what the shower is like, and how close you can park. Words like "accessible" mean different things to different people, so get measurements and photos. In Liverpool itself, Blue Badge holders can park free in pay and display bays, Merseyrail's new trains have level boarding, and there are Changing Places toilets at several major attractions. This guide gives you a checklist to use with any host, including us.

Why groups need to check more carefully

Most holiday houses in the UK were built as family homes, not as accessible accommodation. Bedrooms are often upstairs, bathrooms can be small, and front doors may have a step. That doesn't rule a house out, but the details matter.

Groups also have a mix of needs. One person may use a wheelchair full-time, another may manage stairs slowly, and a third may need a quiet room. A single accessible hotel room solves one person's needs but splits the group up. A house keeps everyone together, if it works for the person with the most needs.

The checklist: questions to ask any host

Copy these into a message before you book.

Getting in

  1. How many steps are there from the parking space to the front door? How high is each one?
  2. How wide is the front door, and any internal doors on the route to the bedroom and bathroom?
  3. Is the path to the door level, sloped, or gravel?
  4. How does check-in work? Is there a key box or keypad, and at what height?

Parking

  1. How many cars can park, and how close to the door?
  2. Is there room to open a car door fully or unload a wheelchair beside the car?
  3. Is the parking on a drive, or on the street?

Bedrooms

  1. Is there a bedroom on the ground floor? If not, how many stairs are there, and is there a handrail on both sides?
  2. What size are the beds, and how high are they?
  3. Is there space beside the bed for a wheelchair or a hoist?

Bathrooms

  1. Is there a bathroom on the same floor as the accessible bedroom?
  2. Is it a bath, a shower over a bath, or a walk-in shower? Is there a step into the shower?
  3. Are there grab rails by the toilet or shower?
  4. Is there space to turn a wheelchair?

Living space

  1. Are the kitchen, living room and dining room on one level?
  2. Is the garden reachable without steps?

Sensory and other needs

  1. Is the street quiet at night? Are there any regular noises, such as trains or a busy road?
  2. Are there pets in the property normally? (Important for allergies.)
  3. Can lighting be dimmed, and are there blackout blinds?

Ask for evidence

  1. Can you send photos of the entrance, the stairs and the bathroom, and a few measurements?

A good host will answer all of these honestly. If a host is vague, take that as a warning.

Getting around Liverpool

Parking with a Blue Badge

Liverpool City Council says Blue Badge holders can park free of charge, all day, in Blue Badge bays and in other pay and display bays. Things to watch:

  • Some disabled bays have time limits
  • Some pay and display areas become taxi ranks in the evening
  • You can't park in business permit bays or anywhere that causes an obstruction
  • EU Blue Badges can be used as normal. Visitors with badges from outside the EU should email the council's parking services to check

Trains

Merseyrail's new trains have a sliding step that meets the platform edge to give level boarding, plus a priority area with tip-up seats near doors marked with a wheelchair symbol. Merseyrail advises wheelchair users to stay in that area.

To book help, Merseyrail lists its passenger assist line as 0800 0277 347 (Freephone) or 0151 555 1111, 7am to 7pm, Monday to Friday, with 0151 702 2704 outside those hours.

Stations vary. National Rail, for example, lists Fazakerley station in north Liverpool as having lifts and step-free category A, but also says step-free access isn't available between platform 1 and the exit, and there's no car park. Always check your exact stations before you travel.

Mobility equipment

Shopmobility at Liverpool ONE, on Level 3 of the Strand Street car park, hires manual and electric wheelchairs and scooters. Visit Liverpool says it's for anyone with reduced mobility because of age, disability or injury, not just registered disabled people. It's closed on Tuesdays. Call 0151 707 0877 to check hours and availability.

Changing Places and accessible toilets

Visit Liverpool lists Changing Places toilets at:

  • World Museum
  • Museum of Liverpool
  • Everyman Theatre
  • Liverpool ONE (5 Wall Street, in the Information Centre)
  • Tate Liverpool
  • Strawberry Field

It also lists the Maritime Museum, but National Museums Liverpool has closed the Maritime Museum for redevelopment until 2028, so don't plan on it. Liverpool ONE lists its Changing Places hours as 9am to 8pm Monday to Friday, 9am to 7pm on Saturday and 10.30am to 5pm on Sunday.

Accessible things to do

Visit Liverpool's accessibility guide lists attractions including World Museum, the Walker Art Gallery, Liverpool Cathedral, the Everyman Theatre, Tate Liverpool, The Beatles Story and Liverpool ONE. For detailed access information, such as door widths and lift sizes, look the venue up on AccessAble or Euan's Guide, or contact the venue directly.

If a house doesn't work

If the answers show that a house won't suit the person with the most access needs, consider:

  • An accessible hotel room for that person, with the rest of the group nearby. Visit Liverpool lists city-centre hotels with accessible rooms.
  • A specialist accessible holiday centre. Visit Liverpool mentions Revitalise Sandpipers in Southport, with fully accessible rooms and wheel-in showers.

It's better to change plan before booking than to arrive and find the bathroom is upstairs.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can Blue Badge holders park free in Liverpool?

Liverpool City Council says Blue Badge holders can park free all day in Blue Badge bays and in other pay and display bays. Some bays have time limits or become taxi ranks in the evening, and you can't park in business permit bays, so check the signs.

Are Merseyrail trains wheelchair accessible?

Merseyrail's new trains have a sliding step that meets the platform edge for level boarding, and a priority area for wheelchair users. Not every station is step-free, so check Merseyrail's accessibility map and book assistance if you need it.

Where are Changing Places toilets in Liverpool?

Visit Liverpool lists Changing Places toilets at World Museum, the Museum of Liverpool, the Everyman Theatre, Liverpool ONE, Tate Liverpool and Strawberry Field, among others. Check opening hours before you rely on one.

Is Zermatt Lodge wheelchair accessible?

We don't describe it as wheelchair accessible. If anyone in your group has mobility needs, please ask us about steps, stairs, bathrooms and parking before you book so you can decide if it works.
