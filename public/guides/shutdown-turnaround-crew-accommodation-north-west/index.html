Shutdown and Turnaround Crew Accommodation in the North West: A Planner's Guide

This guide is for crew managers, site leads and the people who book for them.

What a North West turnaround looks like

Take Stanlow as an example. EET Fuels describes the refinery in Ellesmere Port as a 770-hectare site supplying 16% of UK road transport fuel, supporting around 6,700 direct and indirect jobs.

Its 2025 turnaround, as set out in a letter to the local community:

  • Started: last week of January 2025
  • Length: about nine weeks, with units restarting towards the end of March
  • Hours: most work scheduled 8am to 6.30pm, with some work outside those hours
  • Traffic: local roads busier from 6.30am to 7.45am and 6.30pm to 7pm
  • Contractors: "additional contractors will be employed" for the duration

Maintenance contracts on sites like this are big. In 2021, Bilfinger UK won a six-year maintenance contract at Stanlow covering scaffolding, mechanical, electrical, instrumentation, insulation, painting and asbestos work, with around 200 staff dedicated to it.

Other North West industrial areas, such as Runcorn and Widnes, the Liverpool docks and the Knowsley and Kirkby business parks, see similar peaks in maintenance work. The detail varies by site, so always check the programme with the principal contractor.

Getting to site: plan for the river

Much of the North West's heavy industry sits on or near the Mersey. If your crew stays on one side and works on the other, the crossing becomes part of the daily commute.

  • Mersey Gateway and Silver Jubilee bridges (Runcorn and Widnes): tolled. From 1 April 2025, a car or van costs £2.15 (sticker registered), £2.30 (video registered) or £2.40 (unregistered) per crossing. A £110 monthly pass gives unlimited Class 2 crossings, which can make sense for a crew van over a nine-week job. Larger trucks pay more
  • Mersey Tunnels (Liverpool to Wirral): also tolled. The Liverpool City Region proposed cash tolls of £2.40 from April 2026, with lower rates for registered account holders. Check the current price before you budget

Drive the route once at shift-start time before you commit to a base. A 25-minute drive at noon can be very different at 6am on a shutdown morning when hundreds of contractors arrive at once.

Tax and expenses: get the basics right

HMRC guidance says travel to a temporary workplace can be deductible. A workplace is temporary when someone attends it for a task of limited duration. It stops being temporary if the employee spends 40% or more of their working time there over a period that lasts, or is likely to last, more than 24 months.

Most shutdowns are well under that. But the rules on accommodation and subsistence are a separate question, and arrangements differ for employees, agency workers and the self-employed. Speak to your accountant or payroll team.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long do refinery shutdowns last?

It varies by site and scope. As an example, the Stanlow refinery's 2025 turnaround was planned to start in the last week of January and last about nine weeks, with additional contractors employed for the duration.

What hours do shutdown crews work?

Long and early. Stanlow's 2025 community letter said most work was scheduled between 8am and 6.30pm, with some work outside those hours, and local roads busier from 6.30am. Many crews start earlier, so check the drive to site at that time.

Do crews crossing the Mersey pay tolls?

Yes. The Mersey Gateway and Silver Jubilee bridges are tolled. From April 2025, a car or van crossing costs £2.15 to £2.40 depending on registration, and there's a £110 monthly pass for cars and vans. The Mersey Tunnels are also tolled.

Are crew accommodation costs tax deductible?

It depends on the arrangement. HMRC treats a workplace as temporary if attendance is for a limited task or period, but not if the employee spends 40% or more of their working time there over more than 24 months. Ask your accountant about accommodation and subsistence.
