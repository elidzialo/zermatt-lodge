Liverpool with a Baby or Toddler: A Practical Checklist

Visiting Liverpool with a baby or toddler? What to ask your host, free travel for under-5s, buggy-friendly trains, days out and packing tips.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Liverpool is an easy city to visit with a baby or toddler: under-5s travel free on Merseyside's buses, trains and ferries, the newer Merseyrail trains have level boarding for buggies, and some of the best toddler days out, like the Museum of Liverpool, are free. The key is choosing accommodation that works for naps, bedtimes and mess, and asking the right questions before you book. Questions to ask before you book

Whatever you book, ask these before you pay.

Sleeping

  • Is there a cot or travel cot? Is it provided, or should you bring your own?
  • Can the baby sleep in a separate room from older children or other adults?
  • How dark are the bedrooms? Blackout blinds help with naps. If not, pack a travel blackout blind
  • Is the house on a busy road? Traffic noise matters at 7pm bedtime

Safety

  • Are there stairs, and are stair gates available?
  • Is the garden fully enclosed? A fenced garden means you can let a toddler play outside
  • Are there ponds, steps or drops outside?
  • Where's the nearest A&E or urgent care? It's worth knowing just in case

Practical

  • Is there a high chair?
  • Is there a bath or only a shower?
  • Is there a washing machine and dryer? With a toddler, you'll use it
  • Is there parking near the door? Carrying a sleeping child and a car seat from a distant car park is no fun
  • What are check-in and checkout times? A 3pm check-in may clash with nap time, so plan the journey around it

Getting around with a buggy

Free travel for under-5s

Merseytravel says children under 5 travel free on buses, trains and ferries in Merseyside. Young people aged 5 to 18 can get half-price tickets with Merseytravel's young people's products.

Merseyrail Family Ticket

If you have older children too, Merseyrail's Family Ticket costs £13 for up to five people (one or two adults with children aged 5 to 15). It gives unlimited travel outside the weekday morning peak (06:31 to 09:29), and all day at weekends and on bank holidays.

Level boarding

Merseyrail's new trains have a sliding step that meets the platform edge, creating level boarding. That makes getting a buggy on and off much easier than on older trains.

Stations

Check stations before you travel. For example, National Rail lists Fazakerley as having step-free access to all platforms, but not between platform 1 and the exit, and the platforms are below street level. It also lists baby changing facilities. Merseyrail's passenger assistance line is 0800 0277 347.

Toddler-friendly days out

Museum of Liverpool (free)

On the waterfront at Pier Head. Entry is free, and it's a strong choice for a rainy day:

  • Little Liverpool is a gallery designed with around 500 local children, with supervised public sessions running several times a day, each about 30 minutes
  • Two lifts serve all floors, so a buggy is fine
  • Accessible toilets on several floors

Croxteth Hall and Country Park

In west Liverpool, not far from Fazakerley by car:

  • The country park is open all year
  • The Victorian Walled Garden is open 10am to 3pm daily (last admission 2.30pm)
  • Home Farm is run separately, so check its hours before you go
  • Toilets open 9am to 5pm, and you can bring a picnic

Formby beach and pinewoods

A big open beach and woodland for a fresh-air day. Things to know from the National Trust:

  • Child wristbands are available from the welcome team, with space for your phone number
  • No shade on the beach
  • Paths from the Victoria Road car park go through woods and over dunes, so a carrier may be easier than a buggy
  • Car parks fill up very quickly on sunny days

Packing list

  • Travel cot and bedding (unless confirmed by your host)
  • Travel blackout blind
  • Portable high chair or booster (unless confirmed)
  • Socket covers and a few corner guards if your toddler is a climber
  • Buggy rain cover. Liverpool rain is reliable
  • Carrier or sling for beaches and busy places
  • Favourite toys, books and comforters
  • Calpol and a thermometer
  • Swim nappies if you're planning a pool or beach day

Why a house can be easier than a hotel

  • Separate rooms mean adults aren't sitting in the dark at 7pm
  • A kitchen for bottles, purees and fussy-eater meals
  • A washing machine for the inevitable accidents
  • A garden to burn off energy
  • Parking at the door for car seats and kit
  • Space for grandparents if they're coming too

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do under-5s pay on Liverpool trains and buses?

No. Merseytravel says children under 5 travel free on buses, trains and ferries in Merseyside.

Are Merseyrail trains buggy-friendly?

Merseyrail's new trains have a sliding step that meets the platform edge for level boarding, which makes getting on with a buggy much easier. Check the station you're using for lifts or stairs.

What's a good free day out in Liverpool for toddlers?

The Museum of Liverpool is free to enter and has Little Liverpool, a gallery designed with local children, with supervised sessions several times a day. It has lifts between floors.

Is there a family ticket on Merseyrail?

Yes. Merseyrail's Family Ticket costs £13 and covers up to five people (one or two adults with children aged 5 to 15) for unlimited off-peak travel on the Northern and Wirral lines and the City Line. Under-5s already travel free.
