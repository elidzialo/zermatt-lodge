Where to Stay When Visiting The Walton Centre in Liverpool

Family accommodation near The Walton Centre, Liverpool. Parking costs, weekly passes, both buildings explained, and a 4-bed house in Fazakerley nearby.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

The best place for families to stay when visiting The Walton Centre is close to its site in Fazakerley, north Liverpool. That way you can reach the ward quickly at any hour without paying city-centre parking. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Fazakerley, close to the Aintree hospital site and Fazakerley station, with free parking for two cars. It gives families supporting a neurology or neurosurgery patient a quiet base for a few nights or several weeks.

The Walton Centre is a specialist neurosciences hospital that serves a wide region, so many families travel a long way and don't know how long they'll be staying. This guide covers the practical side.

The Walton Centre site explained

The Walton Centre shares a campus with Aintree University Hospital. There are two Walton Centre buildings to know about:

  • Main Building: Lower Lane, Fazakerley, Liverpool, L9 7LJ
  • Sid Watkins Building: Lower Lane, Fazakerley, Liverpool, L9 7BB

The hospital asks you to check your appointment letter to see which building you need. Some patients are now seen closer to home, so check that too before you travel.

Because Aintree University Hospital manages parking across the whole site, the car parks, pay machines and rules are shared. If you're also visiting someone in the main Aintree hospital, see our guide to accommodation near Aintree University Hospital.

Getting there

By car

The site is in north Liverpool, close to the end of the M57 and M58 motorways. That makes it straightforward for families driving from North Wales, Lancashire, Cumbria or further afield, because you don't need to cross the city centre. The hospitals warn that roads around the site get congested at peak times, so leave extra time for morning arrivals.

By train

The Walton Centre says Fazakerley station is about a 10-minute walk away. It's on Merseyrail's Northern line, and direct trains reach Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes. Relatives arriving at Lime Street can walk to Central and be on the ward in well under an hour. See our guide to Fazakerley to Liverpool city centre by train.

By bus

Several bus routes serve the site. The Walton Centre points visitors to Merseytravel for current times, and to Traveline for journeys from Cheshire, Wales and national services.

Parking at The Walton Centre

The Walton Centre's page lists these visitor charges:

Stay Charge
Under 30 minutes Free
Up to 1 hour £4.50
Up to 2 hours £5
Up to 4 hours £6
Up to 6 hours £7
Over 6 hours £8.50
7-day pass £22.50

Things to know:

  • Most car parks are ticketless, pay-on-foot, with number plate recognition. Note your registration.
  • The machines take card payment only.
  • Blue Badge holders park free in marked bays. Tell reception when you arrive and leave, and give your registration and proof of your badge.
  • There's a concession scheme for long-term patients and frequent visitors, valid in designated car parks only.
  • Parking outside marked bays can get you a breach of contract notice.

Aintree University Hospital's own page lists a lower tariff for the same site. Charges get reviewed, so check the signs when you arrive. If you're visiting daily for more than a few days, the weekly or 7-day option or a concession nearly always works out cheaper. Ask about it on day one.

What families of neuro patients often need

Neurology and neurosurgery admissions can be long and unpredictable. Families often find:

  • Visiting happens in shifts. One person stays at the bedside while others rest, so a base with separate bedrooms really helps.
  • Updates come at set times. Find out when the consultant's ward round is, so the right family member is there to hear it.
  • Rehabilitation can take weeks. A stay in a hotel room soon becomes tiring. Having a kitchen, a garden and a living room matters more than you'd expect.
  • There's paperwork. Keep a notebook of names, ward numbers and questions for the medical team.
  • Children may be with you. Space to play and a normal evening routine help them cope.

For more ideas, see our tips for long hospital stays in Liverpool.

Checklist for your first visit

  1. Check your letter or the ward for the right building (Main or Sid Watkins).
  2. Save the ward's phone number.
  3. Ask about the Home from Home accommodation and the frequent-visitor parking concession.
  4. Find the nearest supermarket. There's a Lidl near Zermatt Lodge, which helps when you arrive late.
  5. Agree a rota with family members.
  6. Pack a phone charger, comfortable shoes and a reusable water bottle.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where is The Walton Centre?

The Walton Centre is on Lower Lane, Fazakerley, Liverpool, on the same site as Aintree University Hospital. The Main Building is L9 7LJ and the Sid Watkins Building is L9 7BB. Check the appointment letter to see which one you need.

Does The Walton Centre have accommodation for relatives?

The Walton Centre's website mentions 'Home from Home' accommodation for people visiting patients but gives no details online. Ask the ward or call the switchboard on 0151 525 3611. If there's no space, or your whole family is travelling, a house nearby in Fazakerley is a practical alternative.

How much does parking cost at The Walton Centre?

The Walton Centre's page lists free parking for under 30 minutes, then £4.50 up to 1 hour, £5 up to 2 hours, £6 up to 4 hours, £7 up to 6 hours and £8.50 over 6 hours. A 7-day pass is £22.50. The machines are card-only. Aintree University Hospital's own page shows different figures, so check on the day.

What's the nearest train station to The Walton Centre?

Fazakerley station, on Merseyrail's Northern line. The Walton Centre says it's about a 10-minute walk away, with regular trains to central Liverpool.

Is there a concession for families visiting long-term patients?

Yes. The Walton Centre says there's a concession scheme for long-term patients and frequent visitors, valid only in designated car parks. Ask at reception or on the ward.
