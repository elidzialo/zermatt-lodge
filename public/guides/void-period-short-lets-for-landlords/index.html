Void Period Short Lets: What Landlords Need to Know

Can you short-let a rental between tenants? Mortgage, insurance, the Renters' Rights Act 12-month rule, tax, furniture and safety, explained with GOV.UK sources.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

A furnished short let can cover part of a gap between tenants, but check first: your mortgage and insurance must allow it, you cannot market or re-let for 12 months after using the moving-in or selling grounds under the Renters' Rights Act, the property needs to be furnished and safe, and your council's rules apply.

An empty rental costs you money every week. A short let can cover some of that gap, for example while you look for a new tenant or finish works. It is not right for every property, and the rules changed in 2026, so check these points first.

1. The Renters' Rights Act 12-month rule

The new tenancy system in England started on 1 May 2026. GOV.UK's guide to the Renters' Rights Act says landlords will not be able to market or re-let their property for 12 months after using the moving-in or selling grounds for possession. If that is how the last tenancy ended, do not list the property as a short let in that period without legal advice.

2. Mortgage

Buy-to-let mortgages are written for tenancies. Many need the lender's consent for short lets or holiday lets. Ask in writing and keep the reply.

3. Insurance

Landlord insurance usually covers tenants, not paying guests. Get short-let or holiday let cover for the period, including public liability.

4. Furniture and safety

A short-stay guest expects a furnished, ready home: beds and linen, a working kitchen, towels, wifi and clear instructions. Safety basics:

  • a fire risk assessment (GOV.UK has a guide for small paying guest accommodation)
  • smoke alarms on every floor and carbon monoxide alarms where needed
  • a current gas safety record and electrical safety report

5. Tax and council tax

The special furnished holiday lettings tax rules ended in April 2025, so short-let income is now taxed much like other property income. Council tax and business rates can change if the property is available and let for short stays for long enough. Ask your accountant before you start.

6. Local rules

Ask your council about planning and licensing. In London, whole-home short lets are capped at 90 nights a year without planning permission. A national short-let register for England is expected from March 2027.

7. Make the gap easy to manage

  • Open only the dates you are sure about, and block the rest.
  • Set a minimum stay of several nights, so you are not turning the home round every day.
  • Offer weekly and monthly rates: contractors, relocating staff and insurance stays often book for weeks.
  • Pause the listing as soon as a new tenancy is agreed.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I short-let after evicting a tenant to sell or move in?

No, not for 12 months. GOV.UK's guide to the Renters' Rights Act says landlords will not be able to market or re-let the property for 12 months after using the moving or selling grounds. Take legal advice.

Does a buy-to-let mortgage allow short lets?

Many do not without consent. Ask your lender in writing before you take a booking.

Is a short stay a tenancy?

A short stay for a holiday or a work trip is normally a booking, not a tenancy. If a guest wants to live in the property for months, take advice on the right agreement.
