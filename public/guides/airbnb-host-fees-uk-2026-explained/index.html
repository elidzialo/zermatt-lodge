Airbnb Host Fees in the UK for 2026, Explained

Airbnb host fees in the UK for 2026: the 15.5% host-only service fee, what it means for your payout, and how Booking.com and Book Homes compare.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Airbnb now charges most hosts a single host-only service fee of 15.5%, taken from the payout, instead of the old split of about 3% for the host plus a guest fee. On a £1,000 booking that is £155. Guests on host-only fee listings pay no separate Airbnb service fee, so hosts often build the fee into their price.

Airbnb now charges most hosts one fee: a 15.5% host-only service fee, taken from your payout. The old split model, where hosts paid about 3% and guests paid a separate service fee on top, has been phased out for hosts outside the EU during 2026. If you host in the UK and your payouts dropped this year, this is usually why.

What changed in 2026

Airbnb's own page for hosts says it is "combining both fees into a single 15.5% service fee paid by hosts", and that hosts using property management software who were not yet on the single fee moved on 13 October 2026. Hostfully reports that independent hosts outside the EU, which includes the UK, moved to the host-only fee on 15 September 2026, and that bookings confirmed before a host's switch date keep their old fee terms.

Airbnb notes that exceptions may apply, and that listings in Brazil and Mexico stay at 16%.

What the 15.5% means for your payout

Booking subtotal Airbnb fee at 15.5% You receive
£500 £77.50 £422.50
£1,000 £155 £845
£2,000 £310 £1,690

The guest pays the price you set, with no separate Airbnb guest fee on host-only fee listings. That is why many hosts build the fee into their nightly rate.

How other channels compare

  • Booking.com: commission varies. Guesty puts the average at around 15%, with a range of 10% to 25% depending on market and property type.
  • Book Homes: 15% commission per booking, or 3% for kosher hosts. No guest booking fee, no sign-up or monthly fee. Once payouts are connected, Stripe takes its card fee from your share, which on Stripe's UK pricing is 1.5% + 20p for standard UK cards.

To be straight with you: on a £1,000 booking with a UK card, a standard Book Homes host keeps about £834.80, against about £845 on Airbnb. Kosher hosts keep about £954.80. For most hosts the case for adding Book Homes is a second source of bookings, with calendars kept in sync, rather than a lower fee.

Ways to protect your margin

  • Add channels, keep your calendar in one place. Sync calendars so that a booking on one site closes the dates everywhere. See how to sync your Airbnb calendar with another site.
  • Build repeat and direct guests from your own channels. See how to get direct bookings for your holiday let.
  • Check your cleaning fee. Airbnb's percentage applies to the booking subtotal, so a high cleaning fee is charged at the same rate.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What percentage does Airbnb take from UK hosts in 2026?

Airbnb's single host-only service fee is 15.5%, deducted from your payout. Airbnb says exceptions may apply, and listings in Brazil and Mexico stay at 16%.

Do guests still pay an Airbnb service fee?

Not on listings that use the host-only fee. The guest pays the price you set, and Airbnb takes its 15.5% from your side.

Is Book Homes cheaper than Airbnb?

For kosher hosts, yes: 3% commission plus Stripe's card fee. For other hosts the standard 15% plus Stripe's card fee comes out a little above Airbnb's 15.5% on a typical UK card booking, so the reason to add Book Homes is extra bookings, not a lower fee.

What does Booking.com charge hosts?

It varies by market and property. Guesty puts the global average at around 15%, in a range of 10% to 25%. Check your own Booking.com extranet for your rate.
