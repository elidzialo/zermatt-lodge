Keeping Kosher in a Holiday Rental Kitchen

How to keep kosher in an Airbnb or holiday house: ovens, hobs, microwaves, sinks, what to bring and what to ask the host. Sourced from kashrut guidance.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

In a non-kosher holiday rental, most people bring their own pots, utensils and dishes, double-wrap food in the oven and microwave, avoid the dishwasher and grill, and use the sink only with a basin and rack. A clean oven can be kashered on its highest setting, but always follow your own rabbi. Ask the host about the kitchen layout and appliances before you book.

You can keep kosher in a non-kosher holiday rental, but it takes planning. The common approach is to bring your own cookware, utensils and dishes, double-wrap food in the oven and microwave, leave the dishwasher and grill alone, and use the sink only with a basin and rack. Some appliances can be kashered. Others can't, or aren't worth it for a short stay.

This guide summarises published kashrut guidance, mainly an article by Rabbi Zvi Goldberg of STAR-K on renting an Airbnb. It's not a psak. Standards differ, so ask your own rabbi before you travel. If you'd rather skip all this, our kosher homes page lists homes where hosts have declared a kosher kitchen.

Appliance by appliance

Item Common approach in a rental
Oven Double-wrap food, or kasher a clean oven on its highest setting (see below)
Hob Keep pots clean and dry where they sit; wipe spills fast. On glass hobs, the area outside the rings isn't kashered
Microwave Food in two leakproof wrappings
Sink Cold or lukewarm water only, or a basin with a rack, separate for meat and dairy
Dishwasher Don't use
Grill Kashering is impractical; bring a portable grill if you need one
Worktops Cold items on clean surfaces; use a towel or trivet under hot pans
Fridge Fine to store food in, if clean
Kettle or hot tap Hot water may be used for drinks and cooking

The oven

STAR-K's guidance says a clean oven may be kashered by running it on high, at least 500F, for 40 minutes. In the UK that's about 260C, and not every domestic oven goes that high. Check the dial before you rely on it. A self-clean cycle may work on a dirty oven, but the article warns that many modern ovens use steam for self-cleaning, which isn't enough.

If kashering isn't possible, double-wrap everything that goes in the oven.

The host's kitchenware

STAR-K says some dishes and mugs may be used cold and temporarily if carefully inspected, but not with sharp foods, and knives shouldn't be used. If the owner is Jewish, items that need tevilah can't be assumed to have had it. In practice, most families bring their own.

What to pack

A packing list built from the STAR-K suggestions:

  • Pots, a frying pan and a baking tray, or foil trays
  • Your own knives, chopping board and utensils
  • Plates and cutlery, or disposables for weekdays
  • Two basins and racks for the sink, marked meat and dairy
  • Foil and oven bags for double-wrapping
  • A tabletop appliance such as a small grill or pizza maker
  • For Shabbat: a hot water urn, a hotplate, a slow cooker and timers. See our Shabbat-friendly holiday home checklist

What to ask the host before you book

  1. What's the oven's top temperature?
  2. Is the hob gas, electric rings or glass?
  3. Is there a second sink or space for two basins?
  4. How big is the fridge, and is there a freezer?
  5. Can I bring a hotplate or urn, and are there enough sockets?
  6. Can I light candles for Shabbat or Chanukah, and where?

A good host will answer plainly. If the answers are vague, look elsewhere.

Buying kosher food in and around Liverpool

Supply in Liverpool is limited, so most visitors bring supplies or order ahead. For licensed shops, use the official lists published by the Manchester Beth Din (MBD), KLBD or the Liverpool Kashrut Commission. Our kosher food in Liverpool guide has the details. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can you keep kosher in an Airbnb?

Many families do, with planning. Kashrut agencies such as STAR-K suggest bringing your own cookware and utensils, double-wrapping food in the oven and microwave, and not using the dishwasher. Ask your own rabbi for your standards.

How do you kasher an oven in a rental?

STAR-K's guidance says a clean oven may be kashered by running it on high, at least 500F, for 40 minutes. Most UK ovens show Celsius, and 500F is about 260C, and not every domestic oven goes that high. If yours doesn't, double-wrap food instead and ask your rabbi.

Can I use the sink in a non-kosher rental?

STAR-K advises against kashering a sink. Use it only for cold or lukewarm water, or put a basin with a rack inside it, with separate basins for meat and dairy.

Can I use the host's plates and cutlery?

STAR-K says some may be used cold and temporarily, after careful inspection, but not with sharp foods, and knives shouldn't be used. Most families bring their own or use disposables.
