Kosher and Shabbat-Friendly Stays in Liverpool: What Jewish Visitors Should Know

Visiting Liverpool and keeping kosher or Shabbat? Where the community lives, the synagogues, buying kosher food and what to ask a host before you book.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

If you keep kosher or Shabbat and you're visiting Liverpool, the key facts are these: the city's Jewish community, about 2,000 people, lives mainly in south Liverpool around Childwall and Allerton; there are active synagogues in Childwall, Allerton and Toxteth, plus a Reform synagogue in Wavertree; and kosher food is limited, so most visitors bring or order supplies. For Shabbat, the most important choice is staying within walking distance of the shul you plan to use. Liverpool's Jewish community today

Liverpool has one of the oldest Jewish communities in Britain. Jews may have settled here as early as the 17th century, and in the 19th century it was the largest community in northern England. Local press reports that it once had more than 10,000 members and now has around 2,000.

The Merseyside Jewish Representative Council is the umbrella body for Jewish communities in Liverpool, Wirral, Southport and Chester. It supports synagogues, schools, welfare services and a care home.

Where people live

Most of the community lives in Childwall and Allerton in south Liverpool. A recent community census, reported in the local press, also found growing numbers in Aigburth, Cressington and Wavertree, as younger families look for more affordable homes. If you want to be close to shul, communal life and the deli, this is the area to look at.

A few practical points:

  • Contact the shul before Shabbat. Princes Road asks visitors to email in advance for security reasons, and other congregations are likely to appreciate the same.
  • Check service times on the shul's own website. For example, Princes Road listed Shabbat for 20 to 21 November 2026 as beginning at 3.50pm and ending at 5.05pm. Winter Shabbat starts early in Liverpool.
  • Princes Road is worth seeing. Its Victorian interior is one of the finest in the country, and it celebrated 150 years in 2024. It offers tours; see its website.
  • Allerton runs synagogue visits of about an hour and a half for groups, including a Shabbat demonstration. Book by email through its website.

For students

Chabad at Liverpool Universities runs weekly Shabbat dinners, festival programmes and classes for Jewish students and staff at the University of Liverpool, Liverpool John Moores, Liverpool Hope, LIPA and Edge Hill. If you're a parent visiting a student, it's a good contact.

Kosher food

This is the area to plan carefully.

In Liverpool

Liverpool once had at least 16 kosher butchers. Local press now reports one remaining kosher deli, on Childwall Abbey Road, with someone employed to check that its produce meets kashrut requirements.

Kosher supervision in the city is the responsibility of the Liverpool Kashrut Commission, which incorporates the Liverpool Shechita Board. Supply has had difficult years: in 2019 the commission withdrew its approval for fresh products from the main deli at the time after breaches were found, and kosher meat had to be sourced from Manchester. Because of this history, we suggest you check the current supervision status of any shop with the local rabbi or kashrut commission before you buy.

There's no dedicated kosher restaurant listed in the visitor guides we checked. Don't count on eating out.

Bringing or ordering supplies from Manchester

Greater Manchester, along the M62 from Liverpool, has a much larger Jewish community. The Manchester Beth Din (MK) publishes a licensee list that includes butchers, bakers, groceries and restaurants, mostly in Prestwich, Salford and Whitefield. It lists no Liverpool licensees.

Many visitors either:

  • Stock up in Manchester on the way to Liverpool, with a cool bag for meat and dairy
  • Order ahead from a Manchester grocer or caterer and arrange collection
  • Bring sealed, packaged products with a reliable hechsher from home

Using a self-catering kitchen

Unless a host has declared a kosher kitchen, assume it isn't one. Visitors who keep kosher in an ordinary rental often:

  • Bring their own pans, a chopping board, cutlery and a small set of crockery
  • Use disposable foil trays, plates and cups
  • Cover worktops and the oven shelf with foil
  • Use a travel kettle or urn of their own

Ask your rabbi what's appropriate for your standard.

Shabbat in a rental home: what to ask the host

A home that suits Shabbat isn't just about the kitchen. Before you book, ask:

  1. How do I get in? Keypads and smart locks can be a problem. Is there a physical key, or a way to leave the door unlocked safely?
  2. Are there motion-sensor lights, inside or outside, and can they be switched off?
  3. Are any lights or heating on timers, and can I set them?
  4. Is there a fridge light I can disable, and a hot plate or urn I can use?
  5. How far is the nearest shul on foot, and is the route well lit?
  6. Is the home inside an eruv? We couldn't find a published eruv for Liverpool. Ask the local community rather than assuming.

Where to stay

For Shabbat

Stay in or near Childwall, Allerton or Wavertree so you can walk to shul. Check the walking route on a map before you book, as some main roads in south Liverpool are busy and long.

For weekday visits

If you're in Liverpool for a midweek event, a hospital visit or work, and you'll drive to shul and shops, you have more choice. North Liverpool, for example, has larger houses with parking and quick train links into town.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where does the Jewish community live in Liverpool?

Mostly in south Liverpool, especially Childwall and Allerton, with growing numbers in Aigburth, Cressington and Wavertree, according to a recent community census reported in the local press.

Which synagogues hold services in Liverpool?

There are Orthodox congregations in Childwall and Allerton, the historic Princes Road Synagogue in Toxteth, which lists weekly Shabbat morning services, and a Reform synagogue in Wavertree. Contact the shul before Shabbat, as most ask visitors to get in touch in advance for security.

Can I buy kosher food in Liverpool?

Choice is limited. Local press has reported one remaining kosher deli, on Childwall Abbey Road. Many visitors bring supplies or order from Manchester, where the Manchester Beth Din licenses shops in Prestwich, Salford and Whitefield. Check current supervision with the local kashrut authority.
