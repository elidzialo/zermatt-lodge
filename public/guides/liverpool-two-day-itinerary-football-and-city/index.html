Liverpool in 2 Days: A Football and City Weekend

A 2-day Liverpool football weekend: match day at Anfield, stadium tours, the waterfront and the Beatles, with real prices, transport and 2026-27 dates.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Day 1: an Anfield stadium tour or the LFC Museum in the morning, then the match, reached by Soccerbus from Sandhills or the 917 bus from near Lime Street. Day 2: the Royal Albert Dock, the Beatles Story and the Museum of Liverpool, plus Everton's new stadium tour if you have time. Book match tickets first, then a base on the Merseyrail Northern line.

Two days is enough for a great football weekend in Liverpool. On day one, do the Anfield stadium tour or LFC Museum in the morning, then the match, reached by Soccerbus from Sandhills or the 917 bus from near Lime Street. On day two, head to the Royal Albert Dock for the Beatles Story and the waterfront museums, and add Everton's new stadium tour if you have the energy. Get your match tickets first, then book a base on the Merseyrail Northern line.

Day 1: match day

Morning: stadium tour or museum

On match days, Anfield offers a Matchday Stadium Tour and Museum. LFC's booking site lists these advance prices:

Ticket Adult Under 16 Family (2+2)
The LFC Stadium Tour and Museum £26 £17 £77
Matchday Stadium Tour and Museum £28 £20
The LFC Museum only £14 £10 £40
Legends Q&A and Stadium Tour £60 £40

Prices apply to tickets booked in advance, and extra charges apply otherwise. See our Anfield stadium tour guide for what to expect.

Tip: on match days, book an early slot so you're done well before the crowds build.

Lunch

Eat early. Pubs near the ground fill up hours before kick-off. Many fans eat in the city centre or near their accommodation, then head up.

Afternoon: getting to the match

  • Soccerbus from Sandhills. Take any Northern line train to Sandhills and change to the Soccerbus. For Liverpool games it runs for up to three hours before kick-off, with the last bus about 15 minutes before. Merseyrail lists an adult return at £3.80 bought with a train ticket, or £4 on the bus.
  • 917 express bus. From Commutation Row, near Lime Street, to Anfield in about 15 minutes, according to LFC.
  • Matchday buses. The 926 runs from Liverpool ONE from three and a half hours before kick-off.
  • Taxi. LFC says a taxi from the city centre usually costs £8 to £10, but expect queues after the game.
  • Don't drive. Anfield has a residents' parking scheme and active enforcement.

Evening

After the final whistle, the Soccerbus returns to Sandhills for about 90 minutes, and the 927 runs from St Domingo Road for two and a half hours after the game. Then either head into town for dinner, which needs a booking on match nights, or back to your base to cook and watch the highlights.

Day 2: the city

Morning: Royal Albert Dock

Start at the waterfront. It's walkable from the city centre stations, and James Street is the closest Merseyrail stop.

The Beatles Story is on the Royal Albert Dock. It lists:

  • Adults (16+): £20
  • Concessions: £16
  • Children (5 to 15): £11
  • Open 9am to 5.30pm Sunday to Friday and until 6.30pm on Saturday

Book online, as the Beatles Story says cash isn't accepted for tickets bought on arrival.

Lunch at the docks

Plenty of cafes and restaurants line the dock. On a Sunday, book ahead if you're a group.

Afternoon: Museum of Liverpool or Everton

Option A: Museum of Liverpool. On the waterfront by the Pier Head, a short walk from the Albert Dock. It covers the city's history, music and football. Look for its themed trails, such as the Jewish Community Trail.

Option B: Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium tour. If you're a fan of football grounds, Everton's new waterfront stadium is a great contrast to Anfield. A football travel guide published in October 2026 lists:

  • About 90 minutes, covering the dressing room, tunnel, pitchside and press room
  • £26 adults, £23 seniors and under-22s, £16 under-14s, under-4s free
  • Selected dates only, with slots typically between 10.30am and 2.30pm. Book online.

The nearest station is Sandhills, about a 15-minute walk.

Evening

Head home, or stay on for dinner in the Ropewalks or the Baltic Triangle before your last train.

Getting around

  • Merseyrail covers most of what you'll need. Since February 2026 you can tap in and out with a contactless card, phone or watch.
  • Sandhills is the key match-day station for both clubs.
  • From Zermatt Lodge, trains reach Liverpool Central in about 15 minutes.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is 2 days enough for a football weekend in Liverpool?

Yes, for the highlights. Spend one day on the match and a stadium tour, and the second on the waterfront, the Beatles Story and the Museum of Liverpool. Add a third day if you want Everton's stadium tour or a second match.

How much is the Anfield stadium tour?

LFC's booking site lists The LFC Stadium Tour and Museum at £26 for adults, £17 for under-16s and £77 for a family of two adults and two children, when booked in advance. The museum alone is £14 for adults.

How do I get to Anfield on match day?

Take Merseyrail to Sandhills and the Soccerbus to the ground, or the 917 express bus from Commutation Row, near Lime Street, which LFC says takes about 15 minutes. Don't drive: there's a residents' parking scheme around the stadium.

Which Liverpool home games are on Saturdays this season?

The published list includes Leeds on 12 December and Tottenham on 19 December 2026, Coventry on 2 January, Crystal Palace on 16 January, Everton on 30 January, Hull on 20 February, Ipswich on 13 March, Newcastle on 10 April, Chelsea on 1 May and Brentford on 15 May 2027. Dates and times can change for TV.

How much is the Beatles Story?

The Beatles Story lists adult tickets at £20, concessions at £16 and children aged 5 to 15 at £11. It's open from 9am daily and asks visitors to book online, as cash isn't accepted on arrival.
