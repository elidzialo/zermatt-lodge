Where to Stay in Liverpool: Which Area Suits Your Trip?

Where to stay in Liverpool: city centre, Albert Dock, Baltic Triangle, the suburbs and the coast compared for holiday homes and groups.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Stay in the city centre or around the Albert Dock for a short break on foot, the Baltic Triangle for bars, and south Liverpool for leafy streets near Sefton Park. For families, groups, race goers and anyone driving, the suburbs give you a whole house with parking for the price of a city flat, with direct trains into town.

The right area depends on your trip. For a short break on foot, stay in the city centre or around the Royal Albert Dock. For bars and an edgier feel, try the Baltic Triangle. For leafy streets and parks, look at south Liverpool near Sefton Park. For families, groups, race goers and anyone driving, the suburbs give you a whole house with parking for the price of a city flat, with direct trains to Liverpool Central.

City centre

The city centre is the obvious choice for a first visit. A travel guide updated in January 2026 calls it the best base for first-time visitors, nightlife and shopping, with most of the city's hotels and both Lime Street and Liverpool Central stations on the doorstep.

The catch for groups: holiday lets here are mostly apartments, so a group of six or seven may need two flats. And parking costs. A 2022 round-up listed 24-hour prices of about £17 at Q-Park Queen Square and £22 at Q-Park Liverpool ONE, and prices may be higher now. Liverpool ONE's car parks are open 24 hours and are cheaper if you pre-book.

Royal Albert Dock

The waterfront is home to the Museum of Liverpool, the Beatles Story and plenty of places to eat. It's lovely for a couple or a small family on a museum-focused trip. Like the centre, expect paid parking and mostly flats or hotels.

Baltic Triangle

Described by one travel guide as "the creative and digital hub of Liverpool", the Baltic Triangle has bars, street food and independent shops. It suits couples and younger groups. If you're a light sleeper, check how close your flat is to the nearest late-night venue.

South Liverpool

Toxteth, Sefton Park, Aigburth and Lark Lane offer a quieter, residential feel with big parks. It's popular with families and with visitors who want a neighbourhood rather than a hotel district. If you need kosher or Shabbat features, see our kosher homes page.

North Liverpool

North Liverpool is where you'll find whole houses with gardens and parking. It's not a tourist district, and that's why it works for:

  • Groups and families who want everyone under one roof
  • Grand National visitors, as Aintree Racecourse is on the doorstep. The 2027 festival runs from 8 to 10 April
  • Football fans, because the Northern line runs through Sandhills, the match-day station for the Soccerbus to Anfield and the walk to Hill Dickinson Stadium
  • Hospital visitors to Aintree University Hospital in Fazakerley
  • Contractors and business travellers working near the M57 and M58

Getting into town: Northern line trains run direct to Liverpool Central, and the M57 and M58 serve drivers.

The trade-off: you can't walk to the city's bars and restaurants. You'll take a train, bus or taxi in, and plan your last train home.

Crosby, the coast and the Wirral

Crosby has a beach and Antony Gormley's Another Place sculptures, with a direct train to the city. The Wirral, across the river, has miles of coastline and the Mersey Ferry. Both suit a slower trip with a car. See our Crosby area guide.

How to choose: quick questions

  1. How many of you? More than four usually means a house beats two flats or several hotel rooms. See house vs hotel for groups.
  2. Are you driving? At around £17 to £24 a day per car in central car parks (2022 prices), free parking adds up quickly.
  3. What's the main event? Racing, football, a hospital visit or a concert all point to different areas.
  4. Do you want nightlife on the doorstep, or a quiet night and a big breakfast table?

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in Liverpool for first-time visitors?

The city centre, including Liverpool ONE and the streets around Lime Street and Liverpool Central, puts most sights within walking distance. The Royal Albert Dock is a good choice for museums and the Beatles Story.

Where should a family or group stay in Liverpool?

If you need several bedrooms, a kitchen and parking, look outside the centre. The suburbs have more whole houses with parking, and Merseyrail runs direct trains into town.

Is it expensive to park in Liverpool city centre?

It can be. A 2022 round-up of city car parks listed 24-hour prices of around £17 to £24 at several central car parks, and prices may have risen since. Many city flats don't include parking.

Where should I stay for the Grand National?

As close to Aintree as you can, or on the Merseyrail Northern line. North Liverpool is minutes from the racecourse and avoids the city-centre scramble for trains on race days.

Where should I stay for a football match?

Anywhere on the Merseyrail Northern line works well, because Sandhills is the match-day station for the Soccerbus to Anfield and the walk to Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium.
