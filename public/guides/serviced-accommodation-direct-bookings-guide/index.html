Serviced Accommodation Direct Bookings: A Practical Guide

How UK serviced accommodation operators build direct and repeat bookings from work, relocation and family guests, alongside the booking sites they already use.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Keep your booking-site listings and add direct demand around them: a simple booking page, a Google Business Profile, relationships with local employers, contractors and relocation agents, invoices that suit finance teams, and niche marketplaces that reach work and family guests. Follow each site's rules on guests who booked there.

Serviced accommodation does well with guests who stay for weeks: contractors, relocating staff, insurance claims and families. Many of those bookings can come to you directly, alongside the booking sites you already use. This guide covers the practical steps. We run Book Homes, which is one of the channels mentioned, so read it with that in mind.

1. Keep your existing channels

Do not take down listings that work. Direct bookings grow slowly, and booking sites keep your calendar full meanwhile. Keep every calendar connected, so a direct booking closes the dates everywhere.

2. A simple booking page

A one-page site with clear photos, a total price, and a way to book or enquire is enough to start. Take card payments through a recognised provider. Show your cancellation policy and house rules clearly.

3. A Google Business Profile

A Business Profile helps local searches such as "serviced apartments near" a hospital or business park find you. Use real photos and a service area if you do not want to show the address.

4. Local employers and agents

Make a short list of the organisations that bring people to your area for weeks at a time: construction firms on big local projects, hospital and NHS staffing teams, relocation agents and insurers who rehouse families. Send a short, useful pack: photos, sleeping arrangements, parking, wifi speed, weekly and monthly rates, and how invoicing works.

5. Make finance teams' lives easy

  • Give a total price, including cleaning, before booking.
  • Provide a company receipt or invoice with the company's name.
  • Be clear about VAT if you are VAT registered.
  • Offer weekly and monthly rates.

6. Repeat business

Most direct bookings come from guests and bookers who already know you. Keep a simple contact list of business bookers who booked you directly, and follow up when their next project starts.

7. Niche marketplaces

Some marketplaces focus on particular guests. On Book Homes, work guests can filter for parking, van parking and workspaces, and can get a company receipt. Listing there is one more source of bookings, synced by iCal with your other channels.

Rules to keep in mind

  • In England, a unit available for 140 nights or more a year and let for 70 nights or more can be assessed for business rates instead of council tax.
  • The furnished holiday lettings tax rules ended in April 2025. Ask your accountant how that affects you.
  • If you lease the property, you need your landlord's written consent for short lets.
  • A national short-let register for England is expected from March 2027.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask guests who booked on a booking site to book direct next time?

Read each site's terms first. Many limit contact with guests outside their platform. Build direct demand from your own channels instead, such as your website, local employers and repeat business contacts.

What do corporate bookers want?

A clear total price, a proper invoice or company receipt, flexible dates, parking, fast wifi and a contact who answers quickly.

Do I still need booking sites?

Most operators keep them. Direct bookings and niche marketplaces sit alongside them and spread your bookings across more sources.
