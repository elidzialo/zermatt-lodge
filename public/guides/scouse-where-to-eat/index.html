Where to Eat Scouse in Liverpool

Want to try scouse in Liverpool? What the stew is, where it comes from, places that serve it, Global Scouse Day on 28 February and a simple pot to cook for a group.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Scouse is Liverpool's own stew of lamb or beef, potatoes, carrots and onions, usually served with pickled beetroot or red cabbage and crusty bread. Places known for it include Maggie May's on Bold Street, Ma Boyle's Alehouse near the Pier Head, Ma Egerton's Stage Door, the Welsford Bistro at Liverpool Cathedral, the Bluecoat cafe and Homebaked's scouse pie near Anfield. Global Scouse Day is 28 February.

If you only try one local dish in Liverpool, make it scouse. It's the stew that gave the city's people their nickname, and in winter it's on menus across town. Here's what it is, where to try it and how to cook your own if you're staying somewhere with a kitchen.

We checked these places on 24 November 2026. Menus change, so check a venue still has scouse on before you go.

What is scouse?

Scouse is a thick stew, usually made with lamb or beef, potatoes, carrots and onions, cooked slowly until the potatoes start to break down. It's served with pickled beetroot or red cabbage and crusty bread and butter.

The name comes from lobscouse, a dish sailors ate across Northern Europe. It became so linked with the port of Liverpool that it gave Scousers their name. Most Liverpool families have their own recipe, and there are strong opinions about the right meat and whether it should be thick or runny.

Blind scouse is the meat-free version.

Global Scouse Day: 28 February

Global Scouse Day is 28 February. In 2027 it falls on a Sunday, the last weekend of February. Some venues mark the day, so it's a good weekend to plan a visit.

Make a pot of scouse for your group

Scouse is perfect for a group in a self-catering house. It's cheap, it feeds a crowd and it's better the next day. A simple pot for 6 to 7 people:

  • About 1kg of stewing lamb or beef, cut into chunks
  • 2kg of potatoes, half cut small (to break down and thicken) and half in bigger chunks
  • 4 carrots and 2 large onions
  • 2 beef or lamb stock cubes and water to cover
  • Salt and pepper

Brown the meat, add the onions and carrots, then the potatoes and stock. Simmer gently for 2 to 3 hours, stirring now and then, until thick. Serve with pickled red cabbage or beetroot and crusty bread.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is scouse?

A thick stew, usually lamb or beef with potatoes, carrots and onions. It's traditionally served with pickled beetroot or red cabbage and crusty bread and butter.

Why are people from Liverpool called Scousers?

The name comes from the stew. Scouse is short for lobscouse, a dish eaten by sailors across Northern Europe, which became linked with Liverpool.

What is blind scouse?

Scouse made without meat. Ma Boyle's Alehouse is one of the places that serves a meat-free blind scouse.

Where can I get a scouse pie?

Homebaked, the community-owned bakery near Anfield, is known for its award-winning scouse pie. It's a popular stop before Liverpool matches.

When is Global Scouse Day?

28 February. In 2027 that's a Sunday, a good excuse for a pot of scouse at home or a pub lunch.
