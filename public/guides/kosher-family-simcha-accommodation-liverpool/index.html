Staying for a Simcha in Liverpool or Manchester: Where Big Families Can Stay

Coming to a bar mitzvah, wedding or simcha in Liverpool or Manchester? Where to stay, walking distance, kosher food, travel and houses for big families.

If you're coming to a bar or bat mitzvah, wedding, aufruf or other simcha in Liverpool or Manchester, the first question is whether it includes Shabbat. If it does, stay within walking distance of the shul: ask the simcha family which shul and venue, and contact the local community directly for any other details. If it's a weekday simcha, a whole house with parking anywhere with good transport is usually easier and cheaper for a big family than a row of hotel rooms. Shabbat simchas: what guests need

For a Shabbat simcha the rules for your accommodation are tighter:

  1. Walking distance. Map the walk from the actual door to the shul and the kiddush venue. In winter it will be dark on Friday night from mid afternoon
  2. Key, not keypad. Electronic locks are a problem on Shabbat. Ask for a physical key
  3. Hotplate and urn. Ask what's in the house, or bring your own
  4. Food. Agree with the simcha family who's feeding whom, and order anything you need well ahead
  5. Early arrival on Friday. Ask for early check-in

Weekday simchas: more choice

For a Sunday wedding or a midweek party, guests can stay further out:

  • A whole house keeps a family of 6 or 7 together, with one kitchen and space to get dressed
  • Free parking matters if you're driving between Liverpool and Manchester
  • Trains between Liverpool Lime Street and Manchester are frequent: around 259 a day on Trainline's listing, from about 31 minutes

Travelling between Liverpool and Manchester

Many simchas run across both cities: a Shabbat in one and a Sunday wedding in the other, or relatives split between them.

  • On weekdays and Sundays, the train is often easier than the car. Liverpool Lime Street to Manchester takes from about 31 minutes, with an average of about 51 minutes, and Trainline lists advance singles from £2.70
  • If you drive, agree who is driving after the wedding before the day, and check parking at the venue
  • Late finishes: Trainline lists the last Liverpool to Manchester trains around midnight. Check the exact time for your date
  • Big families: one house in a central spot with parking for two cars can be easier than splitting across hotels in both cities

Food at the house

If your accommodation isn't kosher, the simplest approach is to treat the kitchen as a fridge and a worktop. Bring sealed food, disposable tins and your own equipment, or order from a Manchester Beth Din licensed caterer and collect it on the way. When the visit is for sad reasons

Families also travel for a levaya or to sit shiva. If you're coming at short notice, contact the local community directly. Merseyside Jewish Community Care offers hospital visiting and can help arrange kosher meals in Merseyside hospitals. A house nearby can give a family space to be together.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where should simcha guests stay?

If the simcha includes Shabbat, stay within walking distance of the shul. Ask the simcha family for the shul and venue. For a weekday wedding or party, any area with parking and good transport works.

How far is Liverpool from Manchester?

About 31 miles. Trainline lists the fastest Liverpool Lime Street to Manchester trains at about 31 minutes, with an average of about 51 minutes and around 259 trains a day.

Where can simcha guests buy kosher food?

Use the Manchester Beth Din's official licensee list for shops, bakeries and caterers. Liverpool's own kosher supply is limited, so plan ahead.

Is there an eruv for Shabbat guests?

Ask the simcha family or the local community directly for current eruv details.

Can we hold part of the simcha at Zermatt Lodge?

No. Zermatt Lodge is a place for family to sleep, eat and get ready. It has strictly no parties or events, and it is not kosher or within walking distance of a shul.
