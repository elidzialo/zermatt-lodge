Planning a Multi-Generational Family Trip to Liverpool

Planning a Liverpool trip with grandparents and grandchildren? Step-free sights, Shopmobility, pacing the days and choosing a house for all ages.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

A Liverpool trip with grandparents, parents and children works best when you plan around the slowest walker and the youngest napper: pick step-free sights, keep each day to one main outing, and stay somewhere with a shared kitchen and living room so people can rest without splitting up. The city helps, with level-access museums, Shopmobility at Liverpool ONE and plenty of free attractions. Start with who's coming

Before you book anything, write down each person's needs. It sounds formal, but it saves arguments later.

  • Mobility: who can manage stairs, long walks or uneven ground? Who might need a wheelchair or scooter for longer days?
  • Sleep: who needs a ground-floor or quiet room, a cot, or an early night?
  • Bathrooms: who needs a shower rather than a bath, or a bathroom close to their bedroom?
  • Food: diets, allergies and the toddler who only eats pasta.
  • Energy: who's happy with two outings a day and who needs an afternoon off?

Then share the plan with the whole group so nobody is surprised.

Choosing where to stay

Hotel rooms

Hotels are simple for short stays, especially if someone needs an accessible room with a wheel-in shower. Visit Liverpool's accessibility guide lists city-centre hotels with accessible rooms. The downside for big families is being spread across several rooms with nowhere to sit together in the evening.

A whole house

A house suits three generations when the group wants to spend time together. Grandparents can stay in with a cup of tea while the teenagers go out. Everyone eats together at one table. Children can go to bed upstairs while adults talk downstairs.

Before you book any house, ask the host:

  1. How many stairs are there to the front door and to the bedrooms?
  2. Is there a bedroom and bathroom on the same floor?
  3. Is there a bath, a shower, or both? Are there grab rails?
  4. How close can you park to the door?
  5. Is there a cot or high chair, or should you bring your own?

If any of these matter, check them before paying. A good host will answer honestly.

Getting around

Driving

With older relatives, a car is often the easiest way to reach parks, the coast and family visits. City-centre parking is expensive and busy, so many families park at their base and take the train into town.

Trains

Merseyrail serves north Liverpool and the city centre. Fazakerley station, for example, is about 15 minutes from Liverpool Central by train, according to Trainline. National Rail lists Fazakerley as having lifts and step-free access, but also notes that step-free access isn't available between platform 1 and the exit. It has no car park, only a drop-off point. Always check the accessibility details for your stations on National Rail or Merseyrail before you travel, and book passenger assistance if you need it.

Shopmobility

If a grandparent can walk short distances but tires on longer days, Shopmobility at Liverpool ONE hires out manual wheelchairs, electric wheelchairs and scooters. It's on Level 3 of the Liverpool ONE car park on Strand Street. You don't need to be registered disabled to use it: Visit Liverpool says anyone with reduced mobility due to age, disability or injury can.

Opening hours listed by Liverpool ONE and Visit Liverpool:

  • Monday, and Wednesday to Saturday: 10am to 5pm
  • Sunday: 11am to 5pm
  • Tuesday: closed

Call 0151 707 0877 before your visit, as hours can change.

Sights that work for all ages

World Museum

World Museum on William Brown Street has dinosaurs, an aquarium, space displays and more, so there's something for every generation. Euan's Guide describes a level entrance and lifts to all public galleries, and it has a Changing Places toilet.

Museum of Liverpool

On the waterfront at the Pier Head, the Museum of Liverpool covers the city's history, from the docks to football and music. It has automatic doors and a Changing Places toilet. From 16 January to 31 October 2027 it's hosting an exhibition for the Cavern Club's 70th anniversary, which can be a nice way to get grandparents sharing their own memories of the 1960s.

Walker Art Gallery

Next to World Museum, with a ramp entrance and a lift to the first floor. Good for a quieter hour.

The Royal Albert Dock

The dock has level walkways, places to sit and plenty of cafes. The Beatles Story is here. Note that the Merseyside Maritime Museum and International Slavery Museum are closed for repairs and redevelopment, with National Museums Liverpool saying both are due to reopen in 2028.

Liverpool Cathedral

Visit Liverpool lists the cathedral among its accessible attractions. It's huge and impressive at any age. Check the cathedral's website for opening times and any charges for the tower.

Parks and the coast

On a dry day, a park or beach gives children space to run while adults sit down. See our family days out in north Liverpool for ideas close to Fazakerley.

Pacing the days

A pattern that suits most mixed-age groups:

  • Morning: the main outing, while everyone's fresh
  • Lunch: somewhere with seats and toilets nearby
  • Early afternoon: back to base for naps and rest
  • Late afternoon: a short local walk, or games at home
  • Evening: a meal together at the house, or one restaurant booking for everyone

Two big days out in a row is usually enough before a quieter day. Build in at least one "home day" in a week-long stay.

Eating together

Booking a table for seven or more needs planning in Liverpool, especially at weekends. Many families do a mix: one or two restaurant meals, and the rest cooked at home. Shopping on arrival day and sharing the cooking between generations is part of the fun.

Evenings in

Bring a few games that work across ages, such as cards, dominoes or a quiz. A pool table or a film night can keep teenagers and grandparents in the same room for an evening.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What are the best step-free attractions in Liverpool for older relatives?

World Museum has a level entrance and lifts to all public galleries, and the Museum of Liverpool and World Museum both have Changing Places toilets. Check each venue's own access page before you go, as facilities change.

Can we hire a wheelchair or scooter in Liverpool city centre?

Yes. Shopmobility at Liverpool ONE, on Level 3 of the Strand Street car park, hires manual and electric wheelchairs and scooters. Visit Liverpool lists it as open Monday and Wednesday to Saturday 10am to 5pm and Sunday 11am to 5pm, closed Tuesday. Call 0151 707 0877 to check.

Is the Merseyside Maritime Museum open?

No. National Museums Liverpool closed the Maritime Museum and the International Slavery Museum for repairs and redevelopment, with both due to reopen in 2028.

Is a house better than a hotel for three generations?

Often. A house gives everyone a shared kitchen and living space, so grandparents can rest while others go out, and you can eat together without booking a big restaurant table every night.
