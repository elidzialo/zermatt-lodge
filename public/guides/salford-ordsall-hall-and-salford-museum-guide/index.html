Salford Beyond the Quays: Ordsall Hall and Salford Museum

Two free days out in Salford: Tudor Ordsall Hall by Exchange Quay tram stop, and Salford Museum and Art Gallery with its Victorian street, Lark Hill Place. Opening days, parking and homes nearby.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Ordsall Hall (322 Ordsall Lane, M5 3AN) is free and open Monday to Thursday 10am to 4pm and Sunday 11.30am to 4pm, closed Friday, Saturday and bank holidays. Exchange Quay tram stop is a 2 to 3 minute walk. Salford Museum and Art Gallery in Peel Park (M5 4WU) is also free, open Tuesday to Friday 9.30am to 4pm and weekends 11.30am to 4pm, and is about 2 minutes' walk from Salford Crescent station. Its highlight is Lark Hill Place, a recreated Victorian street.

Most visitors to Salford head straight for the Quays. Two of the city's best free days out are a short tram or train ride away: a Tudor manor house with a ghost story or two, and a museum with a full Victorian street inside it. We checked these details on the venues' own websites on 25 November 2026.

Ordsall Hall

  • What: a historic manor house, famous for its ghost stories, with a café, gift shop and a picnic area in the orchard.
  • Where: 322 Ordsall Lane, Ordsall, Salford, M5 3AN.
  • Price: free.
  • Open: Monday to Thursday 10am to 4pm, Sunday 11.30am to 4pm. Closed Friday, Saturday and bank holidays. That catches out a lot of weekend visitors, so plan a Sunday or weekday.
  • By tram: take the Eccles or MediaCityUK line to Exchange Quay, about a 2 to 3 minute walk.
  • Parking: a gated pay-and-display car park with 48 spaces including 4 accessible bays: £2.50 up to 3 hours, £5.50 for 3 to 6 hours, £9 over 6 hours. Blue Badge holders park free. The gates lock at 4.15pm.
  • Access: step-free, with lifts to all floors and a hearing loop. The café and shop area is dog-friendly.

Salford Museum and Art Gallery

  • Where: Peel Park, The Crescent, Salford, M5 4WU.
  • Price: free, with a suggested £3 donation.
  • Open: Tuesday to Friday 9.30am to 4pm, Saturday and Sunday 11.30am to 4pm. On Mondays only the café and shop are open. The whole building closes on bank holidays.
  • Highlight: Lark Hill Place, a recreated Victorian street showing Salford in 1897. In Salford school term time it opens at 11.30am.
  • Also: the Victorian Gallery, the Langworthy Gallery and changing exhibitions.
  • By train: Salford Crescent station is about 2 minutes' walk, with trains from Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road.
  • Parking: limited. A small pay-and-display car park at the front, with the same prices as Ordsall Hall, and 3 accessible bays.

Putting a day together

  • Weekday: Salford Museum in the morning, lunch, then Ordsall Hall in the afternoon (before it closes at 4pm).
  • Sunday: both open from 11.30am, so start late and keep it relaxed.
  • Saturday: Ordsall Hall is closed, so pair the museum with the Quays instead. See our Salford Quays guide.
  • Gardens: RHS Garden Bridgewater in Worsley is also in Salford. See our Bridgewater guide.

Over Christmas

Both venues close on bank holidays, which include Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Day. Check their websites for any other festive changes.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Ordsall Hall free?

Yes. Entry is free. The pay-and-display car park costs £2.50 for up to 3 hours, and Blue Badge holders park free.

Is Ordsall Hall open on Saturdays?

No. It is open Monday to Thursday 10am to 4pm and Sunday 11.30am to 4pm, and closed on Fridays, Saturdays and bank holidays.

What is Lark Hill Place?

A recreated Victorian street inside Salford Museum and Art Gallery, showing Salford in 1897. In Salford school term time it opens at 11.30am.

How do I get to Salford Museum and Art Gallery?

Salford Crescent station is about a 2-minute walk, with trains from Manchester Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road. There is also a small pay-and-display car park at the front.
