Grand National Festival: A First-Timer's Guide

First time at the Grand National? The three festival days, choosing an enclosure, bags, cash, children's tickets, what to wear and getting home.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

The 2027 Grand National Festival runs from Thursday 8 to Saturday 10 April at Aintree Racecourse. First-timers should choose a day and an enclosure, travel by train if they can, and leave big bags and cash at home.

If it's your first Grand National Festival, the essentials are simple: choose your day, pick an enclosure that fits your budget and how you want to watch, travel by train if you can, and leave big bags and cash at home. The 2027 festival runs from Thursday 8 to Saturday 10 April at Aintree Racecourse in Liverpool. This guide explains what each day is like, how the enclosures differ, the rules people get caught out by, and how to get home without the crush.

The three festival days

Day Date (2027) What it's like
Opening Day Thursday 8 April The first day of the festival, with a full day's racing
Ladies Day Friday 9 April The style day, with the Style Awards. Busy, colourful and dressy
Grand National Day Saturday 10 April The big one, with the Grand National itself. The biggest crowds

Race times and gate opening times are on the Jockey Club's event schedule. The Jockey Club says car parks open at 6am, but you can only enter the racecourse once the gates open. Arrive early for security checks.

If your group is going on the Friday, see our Ladies Day group stay guide.

Choosing an enclosure

Your ticket decides where you can go on the course. The Jockey Club lists a wide range. A few examples, with the starting prices it listed when we checked:

Enclosure What you get From
Festival Zone Views of the closing stages of every race, a bar and live music in the Aintree Pavilion £39
The Embankment Outdoor area by the first fences with bars, big screens and unreserved seating, but no access to the main course £47
Earl of Derby Terrace Outdoor terrace beside the horsewalk, with access to the Red Rum Garden £61
Lord Daresbury Roof Raised view above the finishing post, with a roof bar £71
Queen Mother Seats Partly covered reserved seat near the finishing post £123
Princess Royal Seats and Gallery Reserved seat near the finishing post with a champagne lounge. Over 21s only £140

Prices vary by day and change over time, so check the Jockey Club website when you book.

How to choose

  • Want to see the finish? Pick an enclosure near the finishing post
  • Want a seat? Many enclosures are standing only. Choose a reserved seat if anyone in your group can't stand all day
  • Bringing under-21s? Avoid over-21 areas
  • On a budget? The cheaper outdoor areas rely on big screens for much of the race

Rules that catch first-timers out

From the Jockey Club's FAQs:

  • Bags: no bigger than a small handbag (30cm x 10cm x 20cm). There's no left luggage
  • Food and drink: you can't bring your own in. Picnics are only allowed in the car parks
  • Cashless: food and drink are card or phone payment only. Cash is for the on-course bookies
  • Children: need a ticket at full price, bought with an adult ticket, maximum 2 per adult
  • Perfume: allowed, but you may be asked to spray some at security

What to wear

The Jockey Club says there's no official dress code. It encourages people to dress to feel their best, and asks you to avoid fancy dress, sportswear and team colours or kits.

Practical advice for April in Liverpool:

  • Layers and a smart waterproof
  • Shoes you can stand in all day, ideally ones that cope with grass
  • A spare pair of flat shoes in your small bag

See our Grand National dress code guide for more.

Getting there

The Jockey Club's travel plan recommends public transport:

  • Train: festival trains run every seven and a half minutes between Liverpool Central/Moorfields and Aintree, roughly 11:00 to 13:00 going and 16:30 to 20:30 returning
  • Bus: services 300, 310 and 345 to and from Liverpool
  • Taxi or lift: road closures apply. Drop-offs are directed to a shuttle into the racecourse enclosures
  • Car: parking must be booked online in advance. At the Steeplechase Car Park, the course crossing closes 30 minutes before the feature race and reopens 30 minutes after

If you're staying on the Kirkby line, note that its trains start and end at Sandhills during the busiest hours on festival days, so you change there. See getting to Aintree Racecourse from Fazakerley.

Getting home without the crush

  1. Agree a meeting point in case you lose each other
  2. Charge your phone. You'll use it for payments, tickets and maps
  3. Don't rush the exit. Have a drink or food after the last race while the first wave clears
  4. Know your last train. Festival trains run until about 20:30
  5. Pre-book any taxi well in advance and expect delays around the closures

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which day of the Grand National Festival is best for a first visit?

It depends what you want. Thursday is Opening Day, Friday is Ladies Day with the Style Awards, and Saturday is Grand National Day, the biggest and busiest. Many first-timers pick Saturday for the main race.

Can I bring my own food and drink to Aintree?

No. The Jockey Club says outside food and drink aren't allowed and will be confiscated. Picnics can only be eaten in the car parks.

Do I need cash at the Grand National?

Only for betting with on-course bookmakers. The Jockey Club says its racecourses are cashless for food and drink. There are cash machines on site.

Can children go to the Grand National?

Yes, with a ticket. The Jockey Club's FAQs say child tickets are bought with a full-price adult ticket, with no more than 2 children per adult, and they're capped at 200 per day.

What's the best way to leave after the last race?

Don't rush. The Jockey Club says festival trains run from Aintree until about 20:30, so many racegoers wait for the first wave to clear. Agree a meeting point beforehand.
