Accommodation with Van Parking in Liverpool: What Tradespeople Should Check

Need a Liverpool work stay with space for vans? What to ask hosts, car park height limits, tunnel tolls, tool security and why many crews park on site.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

If your crew is coming to Liverpool with vans, don't just look for "free parking" on a listing. Ask the host how many vehicles fit off-street, how long and high the space is, and whether vans are allowed. Many homes have driveways sized for cars, and city-centre multi-storeys often have height limits of around 2.1m. The good news is that Liverpool has no Clean Air Zone charge, and the suburbs near the M57 and M58 give quicker access to sites across the city region than the centre does. Why van parking matters more than people think

For a tradesperson, the van is the office, the stores and the toolbox. A bad parking set-up costs you time and money:

  • Overnight risk: a van left on a dark street is a target for tool theft.
  • Lost time: driving round looking for a space at the end of a shift is the last thing you want.
  • Fines: parking on yellow lines, in permit zones or across a neighbour's drive can mean penalty charges.
  • Neighbour complaints: large vans on narrow residential streets can cause friction, especially if they block light or access.

Questions to ask any host

Send these before you book.

  1. How many vehicles can park off-street? Is that cars, or vans?
  2. What are the dimensions of the parking space: length, width and any height limit, such as a carport or gate?
  3. Is it a private drive, a shared car park or on-street?
  4. Are there permit or residents' parking restrictions on the street?
  5. Is the space lit at night? Is there CCTV?
  6. Can a van be loaded and unloaded without blocking the road or a neighbour?
  7. Is commercial vehicle parking allowed under the property's rules or lease?
  8. What time do people usually leave in the morning, and will vans block each other in?

Ask for a photo of the parking area. If the host is vague, assume there's room for cars only.

Height limits in Liverpool car parks

If you can't park at your accommodation, or you need to park in town for a job, height is the big issue. Liverpool City Council lists these limits for its multi-storey car parks:

Car park Height limit Hours
King's Dock 3.1m on the ground floor only, 2.1m on upper levels 24 hours
Mount Pleasant 2.06m 24 hours
Paddington Village 2.1m 24 hours
Victoria Street 2.1m 24 hours

Most medium and large panel vans, especially with a roof rack or ladders, won't fit under 2.1m. The council's surface car parks have no height limit listed, but several of them only open from 7.30am to 7.30pm, so they're no use for overnight parking.

Always measure your van, including roof racks and ladders, and check the sign at the entrance.

Driving a van around Liverpool

No Clean Air Zone charge

Liverpool City Council decided in 2022 to move away from a charging Clean Air Zone. It chose other measures instead, such as changing traffic signals, improving bus stops and enforcing engine idling rules. So there's no daily charge for older vans in Liverpool.

Mersey Tunnel tolls

If your work takes you to the Wirral, the Mersey tunnels are tolled. Merseytravel lists the standard car toll at £2.00. Vans can fall into a higher class, so check the class for your vehicle on Merseytravel's site. Payment is by cash or card at the booths, or with a T-FLOW pre-paid account. Merseytravel says older Fast Tags are being replaced by T-FLOW.

Using the motorways

North Liverpool sits near the ends of the M57 and M58, which link to the M62 and M6. For sites at Knowsley, Kirkby, Aintree and Skelmersdale, or further out in Lancashire and Cheshire, starting from the north of the city means you avoid the city-centre traffic. Knowsley Safari's own directions, for example, use junction 6 of the M62 to join the M57, then junction 2. See our M57 and M58 accommodation guide.

Keeping tools safe

Police advice on van security, from Avon and Somerset Police's guidance for tradespeople, includes:

  • Take tools out overnight, or fit a tool safe or van vault with approved locks.
  • Mark tools and boxes with your postcode and house number, and register serial numbers on immobilise.com.
  • Park on a driveway or in a garage overnight if possible. Otherwise choose somewhere well lit and busy, ideally in view of CCTV.
  • Park close to a wall or hedge to block access to some doors.
  • Fit extra locks, such as deadlocks and slam locks, and use the factory deadlocks.
  • Use a Thatcham-approved alarm and immobiliser, and set it every time.
  • Consider a tracker, especially for leased vans.
  • Keep keys in an RFID-blocking pouch to prevent relay attacks.
  • Tell your insurer if you park overnight on private property, as it may lower your premium.

If your accommodation has space indoors for tools, ask whether you can bring them in overnight. Many hosts are fine with it if you're careful with floors and walls.

Options if your accommodation is car-only

If the place you like only fits cars:

  • Swap vans for cars for the commute and leave the vans at the yard or site compound, if your site allows it.
  • Bring one van and car-share in a crew car.
  • Rent a secure parking space nearby through a parking app.
  • Look for a different property with a yard or wide drive.

Be honest with the host about how many vehicles you're bringing. Turning up with three vans when the drive fits two cars causes problems for everyone.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I park a van in Liverpool city centre car parks?

Many multi-storeys have low height limits. Liverpool City Council lists 2.1m for Paddington Village and Victoria Street, 2.06m for Mount Pleasant, and King's Dock at 3.1m on the ground floor only, with 2.1m on upper levels. Check your van's height, including roof racks.

Does Liverpool have a Clean Air Zone charge for vans?

No. Liverpool City Council moved away from a charging Clean Air Zone in 2022 and chose other measures instead, such as traffic signal changes and idling enforcement.

How do I keep tools safe in a van overnight?

Police advice is to take tools out overnight where possible or use a tool vault, mark tools with your postcode and register serial numbers, park on a driveway or in a well-lit spot, and use extra locks, an alarm and a tracker.
