Contractor Stays for Birkenhead Shipyard Work at Cammell Laird

Working at Cammell Laird in Birkenhead? The yard's new owner, its defence and ferry work, crossing the Mersey by tunnel or train, and a crew house with parking.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Cammell Laird is a shipbuilding and ship repair yard in Birkenhead, dating back to 1828. In June 2026 its owner APCL Group, part of Peel, was bought by Balaena. The yard works on Royal Navy programmes including Type 26 frigate sections and carrier and submarine blocks, plus ship repair. Crews can stay on the Wirral or in Liverpool: from north Liverpool you cross by the tolled Kingsway Tunnel, or take Merseyrail's Wirral line.

Cammell Laird is one of the best-known names in British shipbuilding, and its Birkenhead yard brings regular work for welders, fitters, electricians, painters and specialist marine contractors. This guide covers what the yard does now, how to cross the Mersey, and how to choose between a Wirral base and a Liverpool one.

We used public sources only, checked on 22 November 2026. Site access, inductions and parking are set by the yard, so confirm them with your contact.

The yard today

  • Location: Birkenhead, on the Wirral side of the Mersey
  • History: the yard dates to 1828. Famous builds include the aircraft carrier Ark Royal (commissioned 1955) and the polar research ship RRS Sir David Attenborough
  • New owner: in June 2026, Maritime Executive reported that Balaena, a Cornwall-based maritime engineering group, had bought APCL Group, the Peel Group subsidiary that held Cammell Laird, A&P Tyne and two Falmouth yards. The combined group has more than 2,000 employees and 12 dry docks
  • Current work: blocks for Royal Navy aircraft carriers and submarines, work on Type 26 frigates, and repair services to the Ministry of Defence, according to the same report
  • Workforce: Wikipedia lists more than 550 employees

Defence and repair work often comes in peaks, with extra crews for a dry dock period or a build phase. That's when short-notice, flexible accommodation matters most.

Crossing the Mersey

By road

  • The Kingsway Tunnel runs between Liverpool and Wallasey and carries the A59. It's the only Mersey tunnel that takes HGVs
  • The tunnels are tolled. Merseytravel takes cash or card at the booths and is moving drivers from Fast Tag to T-FLOW, an account linked to your number plate. Check the current toll for your vehicle class on Merseytravel's site
  • A daily crossing for several vans adds up over a long job. Factor it into the budget

By train

  • Merseyrail's Wirral line runs under the river from Liverpool city centre stations to Birkenhead
  • From Fazakerley, take the Northern line into the city and change to the Wirral line at Moorfields or Liverpool Central, which interchange with both lines. Check the full journey in a planner

Wirral base or Liverpool base?

Base Best for
Birkenhead or the Wirral Crews working every shift at the yard, no tunnel tolls
North Liverpool Crews who drive, also work Liverpool or Merseyside sites, or want a whole house

Be realistic: if your team's only job is at the yard, a Wirral base will usually mean a shorter commute. If the crew moves between the yard, the Liverpool docks and sites off the M57 and M58, north Liverpool can work well.

What crews need

  • Off-street parking for vans, with tools out of sight. See our van parking guide
  • Separate beds and two bathrooms for a crew of four or more
  • A washer and dryer. Our laundry guide covers what can and can't be washed at your accommodation
  • A big kitchen for early breakfasts
  • Flexible dates, because dry dock periods change

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who owns Cammell Laird now?

Maritime Executive reported in June 2026 that Balaena, a Cornwall-based maritime engineering group, had bought APCL Group, the Peel Group subsidiary that held Cammell Laird, A&P Tyne and two Falmouth yards.

What work does Cammell Laird do?

Ship building and repair. Maritime Executive says it builds blocks for Royal Navy aircraft carriers and submarines, works on Type 26 frigates and has provided repair services to the Ministry of Defence. Past builds include the research ship RRS Sir David Attenborough.

How do I get from Liverpool to Birkenhead?

By road through the Mersey tunnels, which are tolled (the Kingsway Tunnel runs between Liverpool and Wallasey and is the only Mersey tunnel that takes HGVs), or by Merseyrail's Wirral line from Liverpool city centre stations.

How do I pay the Mersey tunnel toll?

Merseytravel accepts cash or card at the booths, and is replacing Fast Tag with T-FLOW, an account linked to your number plate. Check current charges on Merseytravel's site.

Should a shipyard crew stay on the Wirral or in Liverpool?

If every shift is at the yard, staying on the Wirral avoids the daily tunnel toll. A Liverpool base suits crews who drive, split work between sites, or want a whole house with parking.
