What to Look for in Contractor Accommodation

What makes a home work for contractors: beds per person, parking for vans, laundry, Wi-Fi, a proper kitchen, flexible check-in, receipts and long-stay terms.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Look for enough beds for one person each, off-street or nearby parking that fits your vans, a washing machine, a kitchen, reliable Wi-Fi and self check-in. Then check the paperwork (company receipt or VAT invoice) and the long-stay terms. On Book Homes, contractor-friendly homes have at least 2 bedrooms and list free parking, a washing machine, a kitchen and Wi-Fi.

Good contractor accommodation is less about style and more about whether the crew can sleep, eat, wash their gear and get to site without hassle. These are the things that matter most, in order.

Beds, not "sleeps"

The guest number on a listing tells you the most people allowed, not how they sleep. Ask for the bed list. Most crews want one bed per person, and many want one room per person on longer jobs. Twin rooms and singles are worth more to a crew than one big double.

Parking that fits your vans

Ask how many vehicles fit off the street, and give the host your van heights and lengths. Street parking may be fine, but check for permit zones. The Highway Code says you must not park on a road at night facing against the direction of traffic unless you are in a recognised parking space, and vehicles over 7.5 tonnes laden must not be parked on a verge or pavement without police permission.

Laundry

A washing machine is close to essential for a stay of a week or more. A dryer, or at least somewhere to hang wet gear, makes a big difference in winter. See laundry on a work stay.

A kitchen the crew can actually use

A full kitchen, a big fridge and enough pans and plates for everyone. Cooking at the house is one of the biggest savings over eating out every night. Our self-catering guide for crews has meal ideas.

Wi-Fi and somewhere to sit

For timesheets, drawings, video calls and evenings off. If someone needs to work at a desk, check for a workspace.

Check-in that suits shift work

Self check-in helps when the crew arrives late, or in shifts. Ask what time check-in opens and how keys work.

Paperwork

Decide what finance needs: a receipt with the company name, or a full VAT invoice. On most Book Homes listings you can tick a box at checkout for a company receipt (PDF). Only a VAT-registered host can charge VAT and give a VAT invoice. HMRC's VAT Notice 709/3 explains how VAT works for hotels and holiday accommodation. See our invoices and VAT guide.

Long-stay terms

Ask about weekly or monthly discounts, a mid-stay clean, fresh linen and towels, and what happens if the job runs over and you need to extend.

House rules

A crew in a residential street needs to be a good neighbour: quiet late at night, considerate parking and bins out on the right day. No parties.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is a whole house better than a hotel for contractors?

For a crew staying more than a few nights, a whole house often works better: one booking, a kitchen, a washer and a living room. For one person on a one-night job, a hotel may be simpler. Our cost guide shows how to compare.

How does Book Homes decide a home is contractor-friendly?

It uses the host's own listing: at least 2 bedrooms, and free parking, a washing machine, a kitchen and Wi-Fi are all listed, or the host has set the home up for work crews.

Do contractor-friendly homes have van parking?

They all list free parking, but the size and number of spaces varies and some may be on the street. Always check with the host for your vans.
