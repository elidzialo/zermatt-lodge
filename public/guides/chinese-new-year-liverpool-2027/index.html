Lunar New Year 2027 is Saturday 6 February, the start of the Year of the Goat. Liverpool is one of the best places in the UK to see it: the city's Chinatown is home to the oldest Chinese community in Europe, and the celebration fills the streets around Great George Square with dragons, lions, firecrackers and music.

When we checked on 22 November 2026, the 2027 city celebration dates hadn't been announced. This guide covers what we know, what happened in past years, and how to plan a stay.

What the celebration usually includes

The celebration is run by Liverpool City Council's Culture Liverpool team with the Liverpool Chinese Business Association. In 2026 (Year of the Horse) the programme was:

Saturday 21 February, 12pm to 4.30pm

  • Traditional Chinese dance and music on the main stage in Great George Square
  • A Hong Kong plucked string orchestra and the Pagoda Youth Orchestra
  • Circus workshops

Sunday 22 February, 11.30am to 5pm

  • Tai Chi and martial arts demonstrations
  • A firecracker display with Lucky Man, Dragon and Unicorn parades at 1pm
  • Performances by local Chinese community groups
  • A fire finale at 4.30pm

Earlier years followed a similar pattern. In 2023, for example, Liverpool Express described dragon, lion and unicorn parades, firecrackers, family workshops, a small fairground and food stalls, with activity on Berry Street, Bold Street and at St Luke's Bombed-Out Church.

Chinatown in brief

  • Location: the southern edge of the city centre, near Liverpool Cathedral, around Nelson Street, Berry Street, Great George Square and Duke Street
  • The arch: on Nelson Street, built in 1999 to 2000 with craftsmen and components from Shanghai, Liverpool's twin city. It stands 13.5 metres tall with 200 hand-carved dragons, and was officially opened at Chinese New Year 2000
  • Street signs are in English and Chinese

Tips for the day

  • Arrive early for the parades. The firecracker display draws big crowds.
  • Bring ear defenders for young children. Firecrackers are loud.
  • Use the train. Parking near Chinatown is likely to be busy. Take the train to Liverpool Central and check the walking route in a map app.
  • Book restaurants ahead if you want to eat in Chinatown that weekend.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When is Chinese New Year 2027?

Saturday 6 February 2027, according to Royal Museums Greenwich. It begins the Year of the Goat.

When is Liverpool's Chinese New Year celebration in 2027?

The dates hadn't been announced when we checked on 22 November 2026. In 2026, when Lunar New Year was in mid February, the city celebration was held on Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 February. Watch Culture Liverpool's website.

What happens at Liverpool's Chinese New Year celebration?

In 2026 the programme included traditional dance and music on a main stage in Great George Square, Tai Chi, martial arts, a firecracker display with Lucky Man, Dragon and Unicorn parades, circus workshops and a fire finale.

Where is Liverpool's Chinatown?

On the southern edge of the city centre near Liverpool Cathedral, around Nelson Street, Berry Street and Great George Square. The arch on Nelson Street was built with craftsmen and parts from Shanghai, Liverpool's twin city.

Is the celebration free?

Culture Liverpool's past programmes have been public street events in Chinatown. Check the 2027 listing for any ticketed parts.
