Your First Trip to Anfield from Overseas: A Practical Guide

Flying in to see Liverpool at Anfield? How LFC tickets really work, avoiding touts, getting from the airport, match day travel and a two-day plan.

ZBy Book Homes, hosts of Zermatt Lodge, Liverpool

Quick answer

Sort your Liverpool FC ticket through official channels before booking flights, because home tickets go mainly to club members. Then book travel and accommodation around the match date.

For an overseas fan, the most important thing to know before booking flights is how Liverpool's tickets actually work: home tickets go mainly to the club's members, so sort your ticket through official channels first, then book travel and accommodation around it. Once you have a ticket, Liverpool is an easy city to visit, with a compact centre, simple trains and plenty to do the day before the match. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in north Liverpool, the same side of the city as Anfield, about 15 minutes by train from the city centre, sleeping up to 7.

This guide walks you from planning to the final whistle.

Step 1: Understand how tickets are sold

Liverpool's home tickets are in very high demand. According to the club's home ticket information:

  • Tickets are sold mainly to All Red members, with priority based on credits built up from previous purchases
  • All Red Ticket Sales happen in batches, typically in July and November
  • Additional Members Sales offer tickets returned through the official Ticket Exchange, usually about a week before each match, with registration about two weeks before
  • Local ballots are for supporters with an "L" postcode, so most overseas fans won't qualify

In practice, that means:

  1. Join as a member if you want a chance at standard tickets, and understand it's not a guarantee
  2. Look at official hospitality packages if you need certainty for a once-in-a-lifetime trip
  3. Ask your official supporters' club whether it has any route to tickets

Avoid touts and fake sellers

This catches out many overseas fans. In England, it's an offence under section 166 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 to sell a ticket for a designated football match without the organiser's authority. Liverpool says passing tickets on outside official channels breaches its terms, even if no money changes hands, and it works with Merseyside Police against touting. The club says it has detained more than £1.2 million in assets linked to organised touts.

If a website offers guaranteed tickets at a big mark-up, be very cautious. Don't book non-refundable flights until your ticket is confirmed.

Step 2: Choose your airport

Liverpool John Lennon Airport

The closest airport, with flights from across the UK, Ireland and Europe. The Arriva 500 bus runs to the city centre about every 30 minutes, taking around 30 minutes. See our airport to Fazakerley guide if you're staying in north Liverpool.

Manchester Airport

A bigger airport with more long-haul flights. It's a longer journey to Liverpool, by rail or road. See our guide to Manchester Airport to Fazakerley.

Step 3: Practical things for visitors

  • Payments: card and phone payments are widely used. Since February 2026, Merseyrail accepts contactless bank cards, phones and watches: tap in and tap out. Check with your bank about overseas transaction fees
  • Mobile data: an eSIM or local SIM avoids roaming charges and keeps maps and match apps working
  • Weather: Liverpool's weather is changeable. Bring a waterproof jacket, whatever the season
  • Plugs: the UK uses three-pin type G sockets

Step 4: Match day at Anfield

Getting there

Anfield has no station on the doorstep. The usual options are:

  • Train to Sandhills, then the Soccerbus shuttle to the ground
  • Train to Kirkdale and walk just under a mile
  • Bus 917 from the city centre on match days
  • Taxi, though roads get very busy

There's no paid parking at Anfield on match days, and the streets around it are a residents' parking zone. Don't plan to drive.

Bags

Only small bags (A5 or smaller) are allowed into Anfield, and all bags are searched. Leave anything bigger at your accommodation.

Stadium tour and museum

Liverpool runs Match Day Tours on home match days, which include the LFC Museum. The club says the last museum entry is one hour before kick-off. On non-match days, the standard tour is a great way to see the dressing rooms and the tunnel. See our Anfield stadium tour guide.

A simple two-day plan

Day 1: the city - Arrive, check in and get your bearings - The waterfront: Royal Albert Dock, the Pier Head and the Three Graces - The Beatles Story or the Museum of Liverpool - Dinner in the city centre

Day 2: match day - Stadium tour and museum in the morning (if it's a match day tour, check timings) - Walk around Stanley Park, between Anfield and Goodison - The match - Let the crowds clear before travelling back

For more ideas, see our two-day football and city itinerary.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do overseas fans get Liverpool tickets?

Liverpool sells home tickets mainly to its All Red members, through members' sales and ballots, plus returned tickets via the official Ticket Exchange. Official hospitality packages are another route. Buy only through the club's official channels.

Is it legal to buy Liverpool tickets from resale websites?

Selling a ticket for a designated football match without the organiser's authority is an offence in England under section 166 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994. The club says passing on tickets outside official channels breaches its terms and it acts against touting.

Can I do the stadium tour on match day?

Yes. Liverpool runs Match Day Tours on home match days, with museum entry. The club says the last museum entry is one hour before kick-off. Book in advance.

How do I get from Liverpool Airport to the city?

The Arriva 500 bus runs between Liverpool John Lennon Airport and the city centre about every 30 minutes, taking around 30 minutes. Taxis are also available.

Should I stay near Anfield?

Not necessarily. Anfield is in a residential area, and most visitors stay in the city centre or elsewhere in Liverpool with good transport. A base with its own parking and easy trains can be just as convenient.
