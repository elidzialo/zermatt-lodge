Ladies Day at Aintree: Planning a Group Stay in Liverpool

Ladies Day at Aintree is Friday 9 April 2027. Plan getting ready, bags, travel and the trip home, plus a quiet 4-bed house in Liverpool.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Ladies Day at Aintree is Friday 9 April 2027. A good group stay has enough bathrooms, a kitchen for breakfast and a plan for getting to the course; Zermatt Lodge has two bathrooms, sleeps up to 7 and is a calm base, not a party house.

Ladies Day at Aintree falls on Friday 9 April 2027, and the best group stays are the ones that make the morning easy: enough bathrooms, enough mirrors, a kitchen for breakfast and a plan for getting to the course. Zermatt Lodge is a whole 4-bedroom house in Liverpool, with two bathrooms and room for up to 7. It suits friendship groups who want a calm base rather than a party house.

This guide focuses on the practical side of a Ladies Day trip: getting ready as a group, what you can and can't take in, travel on the Friday and coming home.

Ladies Day at a glance

  • Date: Friday 9 April 2027, day two of the Randox Grand National Festival (8 to 10 April)
  • Venue: Aintree Racecourse, Ormskirk Road, Liverpool, L9 5AS
  • What makes it different: the Jockey Club describes it as the day when style takes centre stage, with on-course racing and off-course entertainment
  • The Style Awards: these return on Ladies Day, so if anyone in the group fancies entering, check the Jockey Club's Style Award page for how it works

Race times and gate opening times are published on the Jockey Club's event schedule nearer the date. Check them before you plan your morning.

Getting ready as a group

This is where the choice of accommodation really shows. Four or five people sharing one hotel bathroom is a recipe for a rushed morning.

Plan a bathroom rota

Work backwards from the time you need to leave. Put the longest routines first and give everyone a slot. With two bathrooms, including an en-suite, a group of 6 or 7 can be ready far quicker than in a single room.

Set up a getting-ready station

  • Pick the room with the best natural light for make-up
  • Bring your own mirror and extension lead if you're all using hair tools at once
  • Lay outfits out the night before, with shoes and a spare pair together
  • Have a steamer or travel iron ready for last-minute creases

Eat a proper breakfast

The Jockey Club says outside food and drink are not allowed into the racecourse and picnics can only be eaten in the car parks. A cooked breakfast at the house, eaten before outfits go on, sets everyone up for a long day.

What to wear and what to bring

Outfits

The Jockey Club says there's no official dress code. Racegoers are encouraged to dress to feel their best, and many treat the festival as a chance to show off their favourite racing outfits. The only things it asks people to avoid are fancy dress, sportswear and team colours or kits.

April in Liverpool can be cool, windy or wet. Pack a smart coat or wrap and consider block heels or wedges, as much of the course is on grass. Our Grand National dress code guide goes into more detail.

Bags and payments

From the official Grand National FAQs:

  • Bags should be no larger than 30cm x 10cm x 20cm, a small handbag
  • There's no left luggage, so anything bigger stays behind
  • Perfume and aftershave are allowed, but you may be asked to spray some on yourself at security
  • The racecourse is cashless for food and drink. Cash is only for on-course bookmakers, and there are cash machines inside

Tickets and enclosures

Check your enclosure before you book. For example, the Jockey Club lists the Princess Royal Seats and Gallery as over 21s only. If anyone in your group is younger, choose an enclosure that admits everyone. See our first-timer's guide for a run-down of the options.

Getting to Aintree on the Friday

Train

The Jockey Club's festival travel plan says trains run every seven and a half minutes between Liverpool Central/Moorfields and Aintree on each festival day. If you're travelling on the Kirkby line, note that on festival days its trains start and end at Sandhills between 11:00 and 14:00 and between 17:00 and 21:00. You change at Sandhills for an Aintree train in both directions. Full detail is in our guide to getting to Aintree Racecourse.

Taxi or lift

Road closures operate around the course. The Jockey Club says taxis and lifts drop off and passengers are directed to a shuttle that runs to the Racecourse Enclosures. Pre-book your taxi home too, as demand is huge after the last race.

Driving

Car parking must be booked in advance. With a group of 7 in two cars, most people find it simpler to leave the cars at the house and take the train.

After the racing

Restaurants and bars across Liverpool are busy on festival evenings. If you want to eat out, book a table well ahead. Plenty of groups prefer to come back, kick off their shoes, order a takeaway or cook something simple, and spend the evening with a film or a game of pool.

If you're also going on Grand National Day, an early, quiet Friday night is a sensible plan.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When is Ladies Day at Aintree in 2027?

The Jockey Club lists Ladies Day as Friday 9 April 2027. It's the second day of the Randox Grand National Festival, which runs from Thursday 8 to Saturday 10 April.

Is there a dress code for Ladies Day?

The Jockey Club says there's no official dress code and encourages racegoers to dress to feel their best. It asks people to avoid fancy dress, sportswear and team colours or kits.

How big a bag can I take to Aintree?

The Grand National FAQs say bags should be no larger than a small handbag (30cm x 10cm x 20cm), and there's no left luggage. Leave anything bigger at your accommodation.

Can I pay cash at Aintree?

The Jockey Club says its racecourses are cashless, including food and drink concessions. Cash is only used for bets with on-course bookmakers.

Is Zermatt Lodge a party house for Ladies Day?

No. It's a quiet residential home with strictly no parties or events. It suits groups who want a comfortable place to get ready, sleep and have a calm evening, not a venue to carry on the celebrations.
