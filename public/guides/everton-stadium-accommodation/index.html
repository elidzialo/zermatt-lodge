Accommodation near Everton's New Stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock

Here's how to plan your trip to the Hill Dickinson Stadium from a north Liverpool base.

Where the stadium is

Everton's stadium sits on Liverpool's northern docklands at Bramley-Moore Dock, off Regent Road (A565). It replaced Goodison Park for the men's team. Goodison is now the permanent home of Everton Women, so both grounds stay busy with football. If you'd like to see the women's team, read our guide to Goodison Park.

Getting there from Fazakerley

By train to Sandhills (best option)

According to the fan site Grand Old Team's visitor guide, Sandhills is the closest station, about a 15-minute walk from the stadium entrance.

Fazakerley and Sandhills are on the same Merseyrail line. The Kirkby branch runs Fazakerley, Rice Lane, Kirkdale, Sandhills, then into the city. So it's a short direct train ride with no changes.

The council's stadium plans also included an additional entrance at Sandhills station, developed with Merseyrail, Network Rail and Everton, to help match crowds.

After the match: expect queues at Sandhills while crowds are managed. The stadium is also about 30 minutes' walk from the city centre, so some fans walk into town and catch the train home from Moorfields or Liverpool Central once things have calmed down.

By shuttle bus

Matchday shuttle buses cost £2 single:

  • 919: Commutation Row or Lime Street to Great Howard Street at Blackstone Street
  • 929: Liverpool ONE Bus Station to Great Howard Street at Blackstone Street
  • 939: Bootle Strand Bus Station to Derby Road at Wellington Employment Park

They run from two hours before kick-off and start again 15 minutes after the final whistle. They don't run during the match. They're mainly useful if you're starting the day in the city centre or Bootle.

By taxi

Taxi ranks have been planned at Sandhills station, Boundary Street (near Vauxhall Road) and Dublin Street. Agree a pick-up point away from the crowds if you're booking ahead.

Walking and cycling

There's a scenic waterfront walk from the city centre via Princes Dock and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal. It takes about 30 minutes. Cycle stands are along the dock wall inside the stadium site. Don't cycle through crowds in the road closure areas.

Parking: don't rely on it

Matchday parking near the stadium is limited, and Everton encourages public transport. Liverpool City Council has introduced a football match parking zone around the stadium. The original proposals covered everything within about a 30-minute walk, including the Ten Streets area and into the city centre, with new residents' parking areas and restrictions. The scheme has been adjusted since, so check the council's latest information before you drive.

Leaving your car at a house in Fazakerley and taking the train is the simplest approach. See our match day parking guide.

Match day tips

  • Arrive early. It's a big new stadium and the waterfront approach is worth seeing. Give yourself time for security.
  • Dress for the river. Being right on the Mersey, it can be windy. Bring a warm layer even in spring.
  • Check train times for evening games. Trains run less often later in the evening and on Sundays.
  • Plan a meeting point in case your group gets separated in the crowds.
  • Combine it with the waterfront. Spend the next morning at the Pier Head, the Royal Albert Dock or on a Mersey ferry.

If it's your first visit, read our guide to what to expect at Everton's new stadium.

Making a weekend of it

The stadium sits on the same stretch of waterfront as many of Liverpool's best sights. On the morning after a match, walk south along the river to the Pier Head and the Three Graces, take a Mersey ferry cruise, or explore the Royal Albert Dock. If you've got children with you, the Museum of Liverpool by the ferry terminal is free and has plenty about the city's football history.

Away fans

Away supporters travelling a long way often stay over after evening or late kick-offs. A house shared between several fans can be better value than separate hotel rooms. See our away fans' guide to Everton's new stadium.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What's the nearest station to Everton's new stadium?

Sandhills on Merseyrail's Northern line is the closest station, about a 15-minute walk from the stadium entrance.

How do I get from Fazakerley to the Hill Dickinson Stadium?

Take a Northern line train from Fazakerley towards Liverpool and get off at Sandhills, a few stops down the line via Rice Lane and Kirkdale. From Sandhills, follow the crowds on foot to Bramley-Moore Dock.

Is there parking at Everton's new stadium?

Matchday parking is limited, and Everton encourages fans to use public transport. Liverpool City Council has brought in a matchday parking zone around the stadium covering a wide area, so don't count on finding a street space nearby.

Are there shuttle buses to the stadium?

Yes. Matchday shuttle buses cost £2 single. They are the 919 from Commutation Row/Lime Street, the 929 from Liverpool ONE Bus Station and the 939 from Bootle Strand. They run from two hours before kick-off and start again 15 minutes after the final whistle.

Where do Everton Women play?

Everton Women have made Goodison Park their permanent home from the 2025/26 season, so both grounds stay in use.
