Long-Stay Accommodation in Manchester: Furnished Homes by the Week or Month

Long-stay accommodation in Manchester, Salford and Prestwich: furnished whole homes by the week or month for relocations, insurance claims, renovations and long contracts.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

For a stay of a month or more in Manchester, a furnished whole home is usually better value and more comfortable than a hotel or an aparthotel. It suits people relocating, families out of their home during insurance repairs or renovation, and staff on long contracts. Book Homes has 8 whole homes in Greater Manchester, sleeping 4 to 12, most with parking. Ask hosts about weekly or monthly rates, cleaning, bills and invoices before you book.

Some stays aren't trips. You might be moving to Manchester and need somewhere while you find a place to rent or buy. Your own home might be out of action after a leak or fire, or you're renovating. Or you're on a contract that runs for months. In all these cases a furnished whole home, booked by the week or month, gives you a normal life in the meantime: your own kitchen, a washing machine and a sofa.

Book Homes has 8 whole homes in Greater Manchester, sleeping 4 to 12, most with parking. See them on the Manchester hub.

Who long stays suit

  • Relocating families. Live in an area before you commit. Homes on Book Homes are mostly in Prestwich and Crumpsall in north Manchester, with family houses and gardens, plus homes in Salford.
  • Insurance claims. If your home is being repaired after a flood, leak or fire, your insurer or loss adjuster may arrange alternative accommodation. A whole house keeps the family together, and pets in some cases, which a hotel can't.
  • Renovations. When the kitchen or bathroom is out, staying nearby keeps school runs and commutes the same.
  • Long work contracts. For staff and crews on long jobs, see our contractor accommodation guide. Manchester City Council's Victoria North programme alone plans 15,000 new homes over two decades.

What to agree with the host

  1. The rate. Ask for a weekly or monthly rate.
  2. What's included. Utilities and wifi are usually included on short lets. Confirm this for long stays.
  3. Cleaning and linen. Agree whether there's a mid-stay clean or linen change, and the cost.
  4. Payment. Monthly in advance, or the whole stay up front. Who pays if it's an insurer or employer.
  5. Invoice. Name and address on the invoice, and whether VAT applies. See invoices and VAT for business stays.
  6. Extensions. Repairs and house moves overrun. Ask how easy it is to extend, and the cancellation terms.
  7. Post and parcels. Ask whether you can receive deliveries.

Choosing the area

  • Near school or work. Keep the daily routine the same if you can.
  • Near a tram stop. Metrolink routes run to Bury (through Prestwich), Eccles, Manchester Airport, East Didsbury, Altrincham, Ashton-under-Lyne and Rochdale, all meeting in the city centre.
  • School holidays. Manchester's February half term runs from 12 to 22 February 2027 (school closes and reopens), according to the council. Book long stays around those weeks early.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I book a home in Manchester for a month?

Many hosts accept weekly and monthly stays. Ask the host about a long-stay rate and what's included.

Can my insurer pay for the stay?

Insurers and loss adjusters often arrange alternative accommodation while a home is repaired. Ask your insurer what they'll cover first, then ask the host for an invoice in the name the insurer needs.

Are bills included?

On short-let homes, utilities and wifi are normally included in the price. Confirm this for long stays, and ask whether there's a fair-use limit.

Is there cleaning during a long stay?

It depends on the host. Ask whether a mid-stay clean and linen change is included or can be added.
