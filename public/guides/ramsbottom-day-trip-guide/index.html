A Day Trip to Ramsbottom

Plan a day in Ramsbottom, Greater Manchester: the climb to Peel Tower on Holcombe Hill, East Lancashire Railway trains from Bury, Nuttall Park and the Irwell Sculpture Trail, with homes nearby.

BBy Book Homes, Liverpool guides

Quick answer

Ramsbottom is a small town in the Borough of Bury, about 4 miles north of Bury town centre. The fun way in is the East Lancashire Railway heritage line from Bury Bolton Street. Above the town, Peel Tower (128 feet tall, opened in 1852) stands on Holcombe Moor at about 1,100 feet, and the climb up is steep. Nuttall Park has a river walk and playground, and the station is in the middle of town with limited free parking.

Ramsbottom feels like the countryside, but it's still in Greater Manchester. You can ride a heritage train in, climb a hill to a Victorian tower, and be back in Manchester by the evening. We checked these details on 25 November 2026. Winter train days are limited, so check timetables first.

Getting there

  • Heritage train: the East Lancashire Railway runs from Bury Bolton Street, calling at Burrs Country Park and Summerseat, then Ramsbottom, Irwell Vale and Rawtenstall. Reach Bury on the Metrolink Bury line first. Our Bury guide has the details.
  • By car: from junction 1 of the M66, follow signs for Ramsbottom. The station (BL0 9AL) is in the town centre with limited free parking nearby and more car parks around town.
  • By bus: buses run from Bury. Use the Bee Network app for current routes and times.

Peel Tower and Holcombe Hill

  • Peel Tower (the Peel Monument) is 128 feet (39 metres) tall. It was opened on 9 September 1852 in memory of Sir Robert Peel.
  • It stands on Holcombe Moor at about 1,100 feet (335 metres) above sea level, so the views are big on a clear day.
  • The climb is steep. You can walk up from the town centre, from Holcombe Brook to the south or from Stubbins to the north. Wear walking boots in winter: the paths get muddy.
  • Going inside: the tower is owned by Bury Council and only opens occasionally, usually when the flag is flying. Don't count on it.

In the valley

  • Nuttall Park: woodland, a river walk by the Irwell and a children's playground.
  • Irwell Sculpture Trail: look out for the "Tilted Vase" near the station.
  • Irwell Works Brewery offers tours and has a shop.
  • The town centre has plenty of cafés, pubs and restaurants for lunch.

Station access

The railway says platform 2 has flat, street-level access. To reach platform 1 you are escorted across the barrow crossing by staff, and the ramp onto the train is fairly steep. There are disabled parking bays.

In December

The East Lancashire Railway runs Santa Specials in December. See our Santa Specials 2026 guide.

A simple day plan

  1. Tram to Bury, then the heritage train to Ramsbottom.
  2. Walk up to Peel Tower in the morning while the light is good.
  3. Lunch in town.
  4. Nuttall Park in the afternoon, then the train back to Bury.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How tall is Peel Tower?

128 feet (39 metres). It was opened on 9 September 1852 in memory of Sir Robert Peel, the Prime Minister who founded the modern police force.

Can you go up Peel Tower?

Only on certain days. Bury Council owns it and it is opened occasionally, usually when the flag is flying. Check before you plan the climb around it.

Can I get to Ramsbottom by steam train?

Yes. The East Lancashire Railway runs heritage trains from Bury Bolton Street through Burrs Country Park and Summerseat to Ramsbottom and on to Rawtenstall. Check its timetable for running days.

Is there parking at Ramsbottom station?

The railway says there is limited free parking near the station, with other car parks around the town centre. The station postcode is BL0 9AL.
